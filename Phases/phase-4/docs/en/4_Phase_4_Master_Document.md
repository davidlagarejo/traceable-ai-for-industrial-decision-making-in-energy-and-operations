# 4A — Constitution of the Verification Bridge

> Canonical governance note: this document belongs to an earlier working line of the framework. The authoritative constitutional document for the integrated 8-phase architecture is `Phases/phase-0/docs/en/0_Phase_0_Master_Document.md`. If this document conflicts with Phase 0 on numbering, phase authority, allowed claims, support ceilings, or boundary logic, Phase 0 governs until this phase is formally reconstituted.

## 1. Objective
Define the operational constitution of Phase 4 as the layer of the framework that governs the passage from preliminary claims toward explicit routes of evidence hardening. Its function is not to verify by desire or to produce terminal closure, but to take claims, tensions, candidate opportunities, uncertainties, and evidence gaps already structured upstream and convert them into a disciplined architecture in which each relevant claim can be linked to required local evidence, baseline hardening, necessary contrast or observation, measurement when applicable, instrumental and operational dependencies, upgrade conditions, and explicit causes of hold, degrade, or block.

## 2. Why it matters
Without Phase 4 the framework is exposed to two symmetrical failures. The first is to raise claims on the basis of plausibility, benchmark, or pressure for closure without a real hardening architecture. The second is to leave the case trapped in indefinite provisionality, with relevant claims, dominant tensions, and visible candidate opportunities, but without an explicit structure saying what is missing, which evidence actually matters, and under what conditions a claim could legitimately elevate its status.

Phase 4 matters because it does not duplicate the general epistemic policing already closed in Phase 0; it makes it operational for the upgrade attempt. Phase 0 fixed the law of claim status. Phase 4 fixes the practical mechanism for trying to harden that status without pretending there is verification where there still is none. Its value is not to make the system timid, but to organize an economy of hardening: to reorder claims by validation value and epistemic cost, to turn tensions, operating frictions, regulatory triggers, and candidate opportunities into explicit architecture of evidence, baseline, and observability, and to prepare the passage toward field verification without false closure.

## 3. What Phase 4 is and what it is not
Phase 4 is the Verification Bridge of the framework. It is the layer that takes the already structured upstream universe and decides which claims deserve an explicit hardening route, which local evidence matters for them, which baseline must be hardened, which observation or contrast must occur, which measurement applies when it applies, which dependency of instrumentation, access, or operation conditions the upgrade, and which cause obliges the claim to remain in hold, degrade, block, or do_not_upgrade within a delimited domain of validity.

Phase 4 is not a final audit, compliance certification, closed savings verification, or a sovereign measurement stack. Nor does it exist to redo Phase 2 or to extend Phase 3 with verification language. Its work begins exactly where the case has already been structured and made legible, but has not yet been hardened through material routes of local support.

## 4. What enters Phase 4 and what does not
The entry rule for Phase 4 is strict: only that which can be treated as a legitimate claim-hardening candidate within an explicit upgrade architecture enters. Not every visible hypothesis enters, nor every candidate opportunity. What enters is that which, due to material relevance, validation value, and capacity to change the reading of the case, deserves to be subjected to a governed hardening route.

### 4.1 What enters Phase 4
The following enter Phase 4:
- selection and prioritization of `claim_upgrade_candidate` from claims, tensions, candidate opportunities, uncertainties, and evidence gaps already structured upstream;
- explicit statement of required local evidence per claim;
- definition of baseline hardening when the claim depends on a baseline that still does not support upgrade;
- identification of required contrast, observation, documentary capture, or measurement when appropriate;
- explicit statement of instrumental, operational, temporal, and access dependencies;
- fixation of upgrade conditions and of causes for hold, degrade, block, or do_not_upgrade;
- delimitation of the domain of validity within which the claim could elevate its status.

A technical claim derived from a dominant refrigeration tension enters, for example, when Phase 2 has already shown insufficient local evidence and high validation value. In that case, Phase 4 may treat it as a relevant `claim_upgrade_candidate` and link it to required local evidence such as setpoints, cycles, alarms, and operating logs, to baseline hardening need, to an observation or measurement stub, and to an explicit do_not_upgrade rule until the real control architecture is confirmed. The bridge does not state that the claim has already hardened; it makes visible what would need to happen for it to harden.

A regulatory claim also enters when Phases 1 and 2 suggest plausible regulatory pressure but a real field trigger is still missing. In that case Phase 4 does not state that it “applies.” It converts that reading into required local evidence, an upgrade condition, a field-specific dependency, and an explicit block against elevation until the trigger is confirmed.

### 4.2 What remains outside the MVP
The following remain outside the MVP:
- final compliance and any form of terminal regulatory certification;
- closed savings verification;
- national-scale verification architecture;
- exhaustive sensor, meter, or instrumentation design;
- full-stack M&V;
- live monitoring or verification dashboards;
- full field operational deployment;
- full verification modeling or simulation at product-final scale;
- autonomous terminal verification reports.

Also outside the MVP is treating every visible hypothesis as if it deserved a bridge. A generic lighting opportunity, with low material relevance, high uncertainty, and low impact on the reading of the case, does not necessarily need to enter Phase 4. It may stay outside hardening prioritization, remain as a low-priority backlog item, or receive no bridge yet. The Verification Bridge does not exist to dignify every intuition with the same methodological weight.

## 5. Central unit of Phase 4
The central unit of Phase 4 is the **`claim_upgrade_candidate`**. That unit governs the phase because it preserves continuity with the claim-centered architecture, keeps explicit the guiding question of Phase 4 — whether a claim may or may not attempt to elevate its status — and forces hardening to be treated as a governed operation on the claim rather than as a scattered collection of technical tasks.

### 5.1 Why this is the correct unit
`claim_upgrade_candidate` is the correct unit because it:
- preserves continuity with the framework, where the claim is the governing unit;
- avoids making measurement sovereign too early;
- supports different hardening routes without collapsing the phase into a single technique;
- allows the change in claim status to be governed explicitly;
- and allows promising claims and claims that must remain in hold, degrade, block, or do_not_upgrade to be treated under the same discipline.

This is decisive because Phase 4 does not work over “measurements in the abstract” or over “loose evidence.” It works over delimited attempts at claim upgrade. Measurement may be one route; baseline hardening may be another; documentary confirmation of control architecture may be another; validation of a regulatory field trigger may be another. The center is not the chosen technique. The center is the claim attempting to harden under explicit conditions.

### 5.2 Why the other options are not better
Units centered on measurement are too narrow: they make sovereign a route that in many cases is neither the first nor the most pertinent. Units centered on required evidence are too small: they capture missing pieces, but do not by themselves govern the change in claim status. Units centered on verification packages or workstreams are too large and too late: they mix several claims, lose granularity, and make it opaque which exact claim is trying to harden and under what domain of validity.

`claim_upgrade_candidate` avoids those drifts. It keeps the focus on the correct question: which concrete claim deserves a hardening architecture, what support it lacks, and under what conditions it could or could not attempt an upgrade.

## 6. Mother law of Phase 4
The mother law of Phase 4 is fixed as follows:

**No claim may elevate its status in Phase 4 through narrative convenience, need for closure, benchmark, proxy, documentary presentation, or sector plausibility; it may only attempt upgrade if there is an explicit, traceable, and materially pertinent hardening route that increases its support within a delimited domain of validity.**

This law does three things at once. First, it prohibits elevation through rhetoric or closure pressure. Second, it requires every upgrade attempt to be tied to a concrete hardening route rather than to a desire for verification. Third, it makes clear that even a successful upgrade remains valid only within a delimited domain and not as license for unrestricted extrapolation.

A single anti-example is enough to fix the point: treating benchmark as if it were local validation, calling a non-delimited intuition a `measurement requirement`, or saying that a claim “can now rise” without a baseline, without a target variable, and without required local evidence constitutes bad design for Phase 4. There is no bridge there; there is an unguided epistemic jump.

## 7. Relationship to Phase 2
The relationship to Phase 2 is one of strict continuity, not replacement. Phase 2 has already produced the structured universe of inferences, hypotheses, tensions, conflicts, candidate opportunities, uncertainties, evidence gaps, validation queue, and next best questions. Phase 4 does not rediagnose that universe or reason it from scratch again. It takes it as the base from which to decide which claims deserve an explicit hardening route and which do not.

This means that Phase 4 does not duplicate the Decision Core. Phase 2 answered what is structurally alive in the case and where tension, uncertainty, and opportunity concentrate. Phase 4 answers which of those fronts justify methodological investment in hardening, which local support actually matters, and which condition blocks or enables an upgrade attempt.

The refrigeration case shows this clearly. If Phase 2 detects a dominant refrigeration tension, with insufficient local evidence and high validation value, Phase 4 may elevate that front into a relevant `claim_upgrade_candidate`. From there, the hardening architecture may require setpoints, cycles, alarms, operating logs, baseline hardening, and confirmation of control architecture before any upgrade attempt. Phase 2 identified the tension. Phase 4 designs the explicit route for hardening the claim without pretending the tension has already been verified.

The opposite case is equally constitutive. If Phase 2 leaves visible a generic lighting opportunity with low material relevance, high uncertainty, and low impact on the overall reading, Phase 4 is not obliged to dignify it as a bridge candidate. It may leave it outside hardening prioritization or keep it as a low-priority backlog item. The Verification Bridge does not exist to treat everything visible as equally hardenable.

## 8. Relationship to Phase 3
The relationship to Phase 3 is equally strict. Phase 3 materialized admissible upstream content into governed visible outputs, but without closing verification, without certifying compliance, and without turning candidate opportunity into terminal decision. Phase 4 begins where Phase 3 ends: when the case is already legible, but still lacks explicit hardening routes.

Phase 4 does not use `report_package`, `artifact_register`, or `audience_view_register` as substitutes for verification support. Visible representation may help reveal which claims matter most, which tensions dominate, and where the validation agenda carries more weight. But no caption, table, or artifact may by itself elevate the status of a claim. Reporting makes the case visible. The bridge makes visible what is missing to harden it.

The regulatory example again fixes the boundary. If Phase 3 showed plausible regulatory pressure, Phase 4 does not inherit that visibility as if applicability were already confirmed. It converts that reading into required local evidence, field trigger, upgrade condition, and explicit block against elevation until specific confirmation exists. Reporting does not verify. The Verification Bridge does not confuse visibility with support.

## 9. Positive product of Phase 4
The positive product of Phase 4 is not a prettier assertion or a stronger claim by wording. It is an **explicit hardening architecture**.

That architecture makes visible, for each `claim_upgrade_candidate`, which local evidence actually matters, which baseline must be hardened, which observation, contrast, or measurement applies, which instrumental or operational dependency conditions the attempt, which domain of validity limits the upgrade, and which cause obliges the claim to remain in hold, degrade, block, or do_not_upgrade. In industrial terms, that means turning intuitions about refrigeration, steam, compressed air, BMS, power quality, electrification, or regulatory pressure into governed routes of real support rather than into diagnoses by inertia. It also means revealing the real observability economy of the case: where the site already offers sufficient support, where the baseline remains unstable, and where the claim still depends on access, logging, or measurable boundary that the case does not yet have.

That is Phase 4's distinctive contribution. It does not impoverish the framework; it makes it stronger wherever hardening is pertinent. It does not exist to produce thin reports or to extinguish analytical ambition. It exists to convert analytical ambition into legitimate routes of support without pretending closure ahead of time.

## 10. Realistic MVP of Phase 4
The realistic MVP of Phase 4 must remain compact. It must be sufficient to govern attempts to upgrade relevant claims without collapsing into a total verification infrastructure.

That minimum MVP must be able to sustain, at least:
- disciplined selection of `claim_upgrade_candidate`;
- explicit statement of required local evidence per claim;
- baseline hardening where applicable;
- minimum observation, contrast, or measurement routes where materially pertinent;
- explicit statement of instrumental, operational, and access dependencies;
- upgrade conditions and causes of hold, degrade, block, or do_not_upgrade;
- and honest treatment of sparse cases.

This implies that measurement is not sovereign. Some claims will harden through logs, field triggers, confirmation of control architecture, local documentary evidence, or operating contrast. Others will require bounded measurement. Others should not enter the bridge yet. The MVP does not need a giant ontology of sensors or a full M&V stack in order to be constitutively valid.

The sparse case is decisive in proving that the MVP is well designed. If the case has few strong claims, many evidence gaps, little local material, and dominant uncertainty, Phase 4 must still be useful with fewer `claim_upgrade_candidate`, more blocks, more hold, and a shorter but honest hardening architecture. If the bridge only looks robust when the case already arrives almost hardened, then it is not well constituted.

## 11. What would be over-engineering in 4A
The following would be over-engineering in this constitution:
- building giant ontologies of sensors or instrumentation;
- introducing decorative verifiability scores;
- collapsing Phase 4 into full-stack measurement design;
- attempting final compliance or terminal verification reporting inside the MVP;
- designing field operational deployment at product-final scale;
- opening national simulation, live monitoring, or verification dashboards;
- or turning the phase into an exhaustive M&V layer before the minimum bridge has been closed.

It would also be over-engineering to turn every visible hypothesis into a `claim_upgrade_candidate` out of methodological anxiety. The MVP must remain compact precisely in order to concentrate hardening where it truly changes the reading of the case.

## 12. Typical errors that 4A must block
- Treating benchmark as if it were local validation.
- Treating proxy as if it already authorized upgrade.
- Calling a non-delimited intuition a `measurement requirement`.
- Allowing a claim to “rise” without baseline, without target variable, and without required local evidence.
- Making measurement sovereign and impoverishing other hardening routes.
- Duplicating Phase 0 as a general warning rather than as a practical mechanism.
- Rebuilding Phase 2 under verification language.
- Using `report_package` or artifacts as if they were sufficient support for upgrade.
- Taking low-materiality hypotheses into the bridge only for completeness or methodological prestige.
- Designing Phase 4 so that a sparse case can only look “robust” through filler or pseudo-routes.

The anti-example of bad design is unequivocal: a system that takes a plausible opportunity, associates it with a sector benchmark, invents a non-delimited “measurement requirement,” and concludes that the claim can now elevate without hardened baseline, without required local evidence, and without a domain of validity. That is not a Verification Bridge. It is a narrative jump with measurement vocabulary.

## 13. Exact decisions that 4A closes
- Phase 4 is defined as a Verification Bridge and not as terminal verification.
- Its function is to govern the passage from preliminary claims toward explicit hardening routes.
- The correct central unit of Phase 4 is `claim_upgrade_candidate`.
- Measurement is recognized as one possible hardening route, not as sovereign over the phase.
- The mother law of Phase 4 prohibits every upgrade through narrative convenience, benchmark, proxy, sector plausibility, or closure pressure.
- Phase 4 does not redo Phase 2 or extend Phase 3 with verification language.
- Its relationship to Phase 2 is one of continuity over structured claims; its relationship to Phase 3 is one of continuity over material already made legible but not verified.
- The positive product of Phase 4 is an explicit hardening architecture and not a stronger claim by formulation.
- The MVP of Phase 4 must remain compact and may not collapse into full-stack M&V, final compliance, or national verification deployment.
- The sparse case is a legitimate bridge configuration and not a defective exception.

## 14. Completion criterion
Subphase 4A is considered closed when there is no longer ambiguity about what Phase 4 is, what it is not, what enters the bridge, what remains outside the MVP, why `claim_upgrade_candidate` is its central unit, what its mother law is, how it continues Phase 2 and Phase 3 without duplicating them, what positive product it contributes, and what constitutes bad design.

At that point, future implementation can no longer freely reinterpret Phase 4 as audit, reinforced reporting, sovereign measurement stack, or terminal verification. It may only build it as what has been closed here: a compact, traceable, and serious architecture for attempting to harden relevant claims without pretending support that does not yet exist.

# 4B — Selection and Design of Hardening Routes

## 1. Objective
Define the central operation of the Verification Bridge for the MVP: how the claims that do deserve real hardening cost are selected and how their explicit routes of evidence, baseline, contrast, observation, or measurement are designed. Its function is to convert an already structured upstream universe into a governed set of `claim_upgrade_candidate` and into explicit hardening pathways, without pursuing exhaustiveness, without collapsing into measurement design, and without introducing upgrade through desire for closure.

## 2. Why it matters
The existence of Phase 4 is not justified by saying that “more data is needed.” It is justified by knowing how to distinguish between what is still visible but immature and what already deserves real methodological investment in hardening. If 4B fails, the bridge becomes decorative in either of two ways: either it pursues everything plausible and dilutes focus, or it limits itself to producing vague lists of missing items without claim, without boundary, and without pathway.

4B matters because this is where the new capability of Phase 4 becomes operational: organizing an economy of hardening. That means selecting which claims truly deserve support cost, which ones should not consume bridge resources, and how to convert vague uncertainty into concrete dependencies of evidence, baseline, observability, and blocking. That capability does not add rhetoric; it adds structure. A system that only asks for “more data” does not have a bridge. A system that knows which claim matters, why it matters, what local support it lacks, what baseline it needs, which variable should be observed, contrasted, or measured, which instrumental dependency exists, and what would still block upgrade even after observing something, does.

## 3. What Phase 4 takes as operational input
The operational input of 4B is structured, not narrative. It arises from upstream objects already closed in Phase 2, mainly:
- `hypothesis_register`
- `tension_map`
- `conflict_register`
- `opportunity_candidate_matrix`
- `uncertainty_register`
- `evidence_gap_register`
- `validation_queue`
- `next_best_questions`

It may also rely on structured context inherited from Phase 1 whenever that context is needed to delimit claim, boundary, reference condition, or evidence dependency. What does not constitute operational intake is the documentary style of Phase 3, the sense of importance produced by a `report_package`, or the fluency of an `executive_view`. Phase 3 may make visible where validation value seems concentrated, but 4B does not select claims by reading impression; it selects from structured registers and from the already closed governance of the claim.

## 4. What enters selection and what remains outside
What enters selection are claims, tensions, conflicts, and candidate opportunities whose hardening could materially change the reading of the case, affect prioritization, viability, regulatory judgment, or the validation agenda, and for which a plausible hardening route exists that does not depend on analytical magic.

The following remain outside:
- low-materiality hypotheses;
- sector-typical opportunities with minor impact on the global reading;
- fronts whose only “route” would be benchmark, plausibility, or intuition;
- claims whose formulation is still too vague to delimit boundary, required evidence, or domain of validity;
- and any candidate whose pursuit would only serve to give an impression of exhaustiveness.

This implies a hard rule: Phase 4 does not select by completeness. It selects by material relevance and by realistic capacity to design explicit hardening.

## 5. Eligibility rule for bridge
A claim only passes eligibility into the bridge if it meets four conditions simultaneously:
- its hardening can materially change the reading of the case, prioritization, viability, regulatory judgment, or validation agenda;
- the claim can be delimited with a sufficiently clear boundary;
- a plausible hardening route exists based on local evidence, baseline, contrast, observation, or measurement;
- and the upgrade attempt does not depend solely on benchmark, proxy, sector custom, or desire to close the case.

This explicitly excludes the logic of pursuing everything plausible. It also excludes pathways opened out of methodological anxiety. If a claim does not pass eligibility, the correct behavior is not to open a weak route “just in case,” but to keep it out of the bridge, degrade its priority, or maintain it as an unprioritized backlog.

In practice, this eligibility usually concentrates in four families of claims: operating-control or performance claims, regulatory-trigger claims, opportunity claims strongly dependent on baseline, and claims whose hardening depends on real observability infrastructure. The usefulness of this distinction is not ontological; it is operational. It allows faster recognition of why certain fronts deserve methodological cost and others do not.

## 6. Operational sequence of 4B
The central operation of 4B is organized into five non-interchangeable steps: screening of candidate claims, delimitation of the claim and its boundary, design of required evidence, design of the hardening pathway, and explicit statement of dependencies and blocking. The sequence matters because it prevents generic routes from being designed over claims that are still poorly formulated or lack a domain of validity.

### 6.1 Screening of candidate claims
Screening takes the structured upstream universe and asks which claims merit real hardening cost. It does not seek exhaustiveness; it seeks methodological concentration. At this step, fronts that are only visible or interesting are separated from those whose upgrade could materially change the case.

Proper screening does not ask “what else could be reviewed,” but “which claim, if it hardens or fails to harden, changes something important in the case.” That is why a dominant tension, a central operating conflict, or a high-consequence regulatory claim may pass eligibility, while a typical low-impact opportunity may remain outside without weakening the framework.

### 6.2 Delimitation of the claim and its boundary
No serious pathway can be designed over a poorly delimited claim. Once a candidate passes screening, 4B must fix what exactly the claim states, over which system or condition it states it, in which time window, under which operating regime, with what spatial or functional scope, and within which domain of validity it could harden.

The boundary cannot be excessively broad. A technical claim about refrigeration cannot remain formulated as “there is a refrigeration problem at the site” if what actually matters is a specific interaction between setpoints, control sequencing, and cycling in a subsystem under certain operating conditions. Likewise, a regulatory claim cannot remain formulated as “the rule probably applies” if the true boundary depends on jurisdiction trigger, filing status, occupancy type, or asset classification.

### 6.3 Design of required evidence
Once the claim is delimited, 4B must specify which required local evidence actually matters to attempt its upgrade. The rule here is not to ask for “more data,” but to define evidence with explicit function.

Required evidence may include, depending on the case:
- operating logs;
- historian trends;
- control sequences;
- alarm histories;
- reconstructed schedules;
- field documents;
- regulatory trigger fields;
- observation of operation;
- spot measurement;
- or preexisting instrumental evidence with sufficient resolution.

Each required evidence item must be justified by its capacity to harden, degrade, or block the claim. If the evidence requested changes nothing relevant about the claim's potential status, then it is not required evidence; it is methodological noise.

### 6.4 Design of the hardening pathway
The hardening pathway is the explicit sequence through which a `claim_upgrade_candidate` could increase its support within its boundary. It is not a vague list of tasks. It is a governed route connecting claim, required evidence, baseline hardening where applicable, relevant observation, contrast, or measurement, the upgrade condition, and possible causes of blocking.

In some claims the pathway will be mainly documentary and operational: log review, historian review, confirmation of control sequence, schedule reconstruction, verification of field trigger. In others it will include bounded contrast between operating states or spot measurement. In others it will require short logging or temporal resolution that does not presently exist. What matters is that the pathway states which operation hardens which part of the claim and under what condition upgrade still remains unauthorized.

### 6.5 Explicit statement of dependencies and blocking
Every serious pathway must make its dependencies and possible blockages visible. These may include absence of sensor, historian without useful resolution, poorly defined boundary, lack of access to control sequences, operational impossibility of observing a certain regime, absence of a stable reference period, critical seasonality, uptime or retrofit-window constraints, dependence on unstable operator intervention, or field trigger not yet confirmed.

Making these dependencies explicit does not weaken the bridge; it makes it real. A claim may deserve a pathway and still remain in hold or block because of instrumental or operational support gaps. That result is not a framework failure. It is a positive design output because it shows exactly what prevents hardening.

## 7. What a hardening route is
A hardening route is an explicit, traceable, and materially pertinent sequence of local-support operations through which a `claim_upgrade_candidate` could increase its level of support within a delimited boundary. It must indicate:
- which claim it is trying to harden;
- which local evidence it requires;
- which baseline must be hardened, if the claim depends on reference;
- which contrast, observation, or measurement applies;
- which instrumental or operational dependency conditions the route;
- which condition would enable upgrade;
- and which cause would still oblige hold, degrade, or block.

A hardening route is not synonymous with a sensor campaign. Nor is it synonymous with “requesting more data.” If it cannot say which variable matters, which local evidence is missing, which boundary governs the reading, and what would still block upgrade even after observing something, then it is not yet a pathway.

For the MVP, it is useful to think of pathways in minimum families. There are mainly documentary and trigger-based pathways, typical of regulatory or asset-architecture claims. There are operation-and-log pathways, typical of control sequences, BMS, refrigeration, or compressed air. There are delimited-contrast pathways between modes, states, or operating windows, often relevant in steam, district energy, or power quality when the key is to compare regimes and not merely to capture a variable. And there are measurement-assisted pathways when the critical variable is not observable otherwise or when current temporal resolution is insufficient, as sometimes happens in electrification, thermal balance, or submetering limits. The distinction matters because not all claims require the same kind of hardening nor carry the same operational cost.

## 8. Minimum distinction among evidence types
For the MVP, 4B must distinguish at least among these minimum evidence types:

**Local documentary evidence** includes configurations, sequences, asset records, filing status, asset classification, drawings, equipment lists, and equivalent documentation. It serves to confirm architecture, trigger, scope, or baseline condition of the claim.

**Operational temporal evidence** includes logs, historian trends, alarms, cycles, schedules, observable sequences, and operating series. It serves to harden behavior, persistence, repetition, or operating regime.

**Field observational evidence** includes directed walk-through, observation of operation, confirmation of real state, contextual review of control, and bounded situational verification. It is useful when the claim depends on a visible condition not yet well captured in records.

**Contrast evidence** includes bounded comparisons among states, modes, periods, or operating conditions, always within explicit boundary. It serves to separate apparent causality from genuinely discriminating behavior.

**Measurement evidence** includes spot measurement, short logging, or specific instrumental capture when the claim requires a variable not otherwise observable or a resolution the current system does not have.

This minimum distinction does not make measurement sovereign. It simply prevents every hardening need from being treated as if it were the same.

## 9. Baseline hardening as part of the pathway
When a claim depends on an operating or quantitative reference, baseline hardening is not optional. It is a constitutive part of the pathway. No serious upgrade attempt can be discussed if the reference condition against which the claim is read remains blurred.

For that reason, 4B must make at least these questions visible:
- what the relevant reference condition is;
- which period matters;
- which dominant variable must be stabilized;
- which adjustment may be material;
- and what makes the current baseline still too weak.

A claim about operating inefficiency, control deviation, candidate savings, or overconsumption cannot harden with integrity if the reference operation remains unstable, mixed with schedule changes, contaminated by different modes, or supported by traces that are too poor. Opening a pathway without baseline hardening when the claim depends on baseline is to simulate method.

Not all baseline needs are the same. Sometimes the problem is a reference period that is too short; sometimes a mixture of operating regimes; sometimes unstable schedule; sometimes an unclear dominant variable; sometimes a poorly closed energy boundary; and sometimes strong operator dependence or production-window dependence. Making that difference visible increases industrial power because it avoids treating a compressed-air claim, a power-quality claim, a steam-balance claim, and a thermal-electrification claim with the same recipe.

## 10. Instrumentation gap is not a secondary detail
Instrumentation gap is not a secondary detail or a minor technical note. It is a valuable output of 4B. If a claim cannot yet harden because of missing sensor, insufficient historian, non-measurable boundary, poor temporal resolution, or absence of observable variable, the framework must say so explicitly.

That does not mean every instrumentation gap forces hardware deployment. It may also mean that the claim does not yet merit methodological cost, that baseline must first be hardened, that recovering existing logs is enough, or that the correct route is observation rather than measurement. A compressed-air system without a reliable load profile, a BMS with present but untrustworthy tags, and a power-quality front without sufficient event data do not have the same gap even though all three may block hardening. What matters is that 4B does not treat lack of instrumentation as an implicit detail. It must treat it as a positive design condition: something that explains why the claim cannot yet rise.

For the MVP, these gaps may be thought of at least in four forms: access gap, when the information exists but is not available; observability gap, when the critical variable is not captured; resolution or temporal-quality gap, when historian, tags, or logs do not support the pathway; and measurable-boundary gap, when the asset or subsystem cannot be isolated with enough integrity. This minimum hierarchy makes the bridge more useful without inflating it.

## 11. Normative examples

### 11.1 Technical claim that does merit bridge
Case: Phase 2 detects a dominant refrigeration tension, with high material relevance and insufficient local evidence.

The correct treatment in 4B is to let it pass eligibility because its hardening can materially change case reading, affect prioritization, and reorder validation. But it must not pass as a broad claim of the form “there is a refrigeration problem.” It should instead be delimited toward a more governable claim regarding control logic, anomalous cycling, inconsistent setpoints, or deficient response under a specific operating regime.

The boundary cannot be too broad because then the pathway becomes trivial and methodologically poor. “Site refrigeration” is not a useful boundary. What may be useful is, for example, a subset of units, a specific loop, a particular sequence, or a recurrent operating condition within a delimited time window.

The required local operational evidence may include:
- setpoints;
- cycles;
- alarms;
- control sequences;
- operating logs;
- historian trends;
- short logging when sufficient resolution is missing.

Baseline hardening enters if the claim depends on comparing expected behavior against actual behavior, or if deviation can only be read against an operating reference that is not yet stable. The pathway may combine historian review, reconstruction of control sequence, observation of transition between modes, and, if necessary, short logging. Even so, the claim should not rise until control architecture is confirmed. Without that confirmation, any apparent support improvement would still leave open a structural dependency that is too strong.

The incorrect treatment would be: “there is a refrigeration problem, measure temperatures and confirm.” That is methodologically poor because it does not fix the claim, does not delimit the boundary, does not distinguish whether control, cycling, setpoint, load, or schedule matter, does not clarify which variable hardens which part of the claim, and confuses measurement with pathway.

### 11.2 Hypothesis that does not deserve bridge yet
Case: generic lighting opportunity, with low material relevance, high uncertainty, and little impact on the case's overall reading.

The correct behavior is not to bring it into the bridge. It does not pass eligibility because, even if it hardened, it would change little in the structural reading of the case while consuming methodological cost disproportionate to claims of greater weight. Pursuing it at this point would be decorative.

It may remain as a low-priority backlog item or outside the bridge without weakening the framework. On the contrary: that demonstrates that the bridge knows how to concentrate effort where hardening truly matters.

The incorrect treatment would be to activate a pathway only because “lighting is usually an easy opportunity.” That mixes sector custom with real case relevance and erodes the eligibility rule.

### 11.3 Regulatory claim
Case: Phase 1 and Phase 2 suggest plausible regulatory pressure, but a real field trigger is missing.

The correct treatment is to define the claim as a conditioned preliminary reading, not as confirmed applicability. The boundary of the claim must be tied to the fields that actually govern possible applicability, for example:
- GFA;
- occupancy type;
- filing status;
- asset classification;
- jurisdiction field or equivalent.

Required local documentary evidence matters here more than general rhetoric about “regulatory pressure.” The legitimate upgrade condition is not that “everything indicates it applies,” but that the relevant trigger is confirmed within the correct boundary. While that trigger is missing, the claim must remain blocked from elevation.

The incorrect treatment would be: “everything indicates it applies.” That mixes plausibility with confirmed applicability and turns preliminary regulatory pressure into a claim stronger than support allows.

### 11.4 Sparse case
Case: few strong claims, many evidence gaps, dominant uncertainty, and little local observability.

The correct behavior of 4B is to remain useful with fewer `claim_upgrade_candidate`, shorter pathways, more evidence discovery, more hold, and more block. In a sparse case, the bridge's value does not lie in producing many pathways, but in honestly identifying where hardening is worthwhile and where even the minimum basis for good design is still missing.

Inflating the case with multiple weak pathways so that it “looks complete” would be bad practice. It would give the impression of verification capacity where there is only methodological noise. A well-treated sparse case may produce few candidates, several explicit instrumentation gaps, and several blockages, and still be a good output of 4B.

## 12. What 4B produces exactly
The output of 4B is structured. It is not working notes or loose suggestions. This subphase must produce, at minimum, four governable registers.

### 12.1 claim_upgrade_candidate_register
Contains the claims that did pass eligibility into the bridge. For each one, at minimum, the selected claim, its material relevance, its boundary, its justification for entering the bridge, and its preliminary priority condition must be explicit.

### 12.2 verification_pathway_register
Contains the explicit hardening pathway for each `claim_upgrade_candidate`. It must show which type of route was designed, which minimum sequence it follows, which upgrade condition could be enabled, and which blockages or dependencies might prevent it.

### 12.3 required_site_evidence_register
Contains the required local evidence per claim. Its job is to prevent the vagueness of “more data is needed” and convert it into a disciplined list of relevant local support with explicit function inside the pathway.

### 12.4 baseline_hardening_register
Contains baseline hardening needs wherever the claim depends on operating or quantitative reference. It must make visible which baseline matters, why the current baseline is insufficient, and what would need to harden before upgrade can be attempted.

## 13. What would be over-engineering in 4B
The following would be over-engineering in 4B:
- opening pathways for everything visible;
- introducing decorative eligibility or verifiability scores;
- building a giant taxonomy of sensors;
- designing hardware campaigns before delimiting claim and boundary;
- turning every observability gap into a heavy instrumental project;
- trying to close full M&V inside the MVP;
- or multiplying pathway types without material need.

It would also be over-engineering to design registers so complex that the phase loses operational legibility before proving its core: selecting well and designing explicit routes.

## 14. Typical errors that 4B must block
- Selecting by exhaustiveness rather than by material relevance.
- Asking for “more data” without defined claim or boundary.
- Opening a measurement pathway without clarifying which variable matters, for what function, and for which claim.
- Treating benchmark as if it hardened a local claim.
- Activating baseline hardening without saying which baseline matters.
- Taking low-impact claims into heavy instrumental routes.
- Using Phase 3 as if its visible form were enough to justify eligibility or upgrade.
- Designing pathways that do not say what would still block the claim even after observing something.

These errors deform the bridge in different ways, but they converge toward the same result: they replace explicit hardening architecture with merely apparent methodological movement.

## 15. Exact decisions that 4B closes
- The intake of 4B is structured and arises from the upstream universe already closed in Phase 2, not from free narrative or documentary impression.
- Phase 4 does not select by exhaustiveness; it selects only claims whose hardening can materially change the reading of the case and for which a plausible hardening route exists.
- Bridge eligibility requires material relevance, delimitable boundary, and plausible pathway not dependent on benchmark, proxy, or desire for closure.
- The central operation of 4B is fixed in five steps: screening, claim and boundary delimitation, required evidence design, pathway design, and explicit statement of dependencies and blocking.
- A hardening route is not synonymous with measurement; it may include documentary contrast, log review, historian review, field observation, schedule reconstruction, bounded comparison, or spot measurement.
- Baseline hardening is constitutive of the pathway whenever the claim depends on operating or quantitative reference.
- Instrumentation gap is a positive design output, not a minor detail.
- The output of 4B is structured and materializes, at minimum, in `claim_upgrade_candidate_register`, `verification_pathway_register`, `required_site_evidence_register`, and `baseline_hardening_register`.

## 16. Completion criterion
Subphase 4B is considered closed when there is no longer ambiguity about which claims do merit real hardening cost, which claims should remain outside, what pathway means, what required evidence means, when baseline hardening is mandatory, why instrumentation gap is a valuable output, and which minimum structured registers the bridge must produce.

At that point, future implementation may no longer reinterpret 4B as a generic audit-step list, as indiscriminate measurement design, or as a factory of vague missing items. It may only build it as what has been closed here: the operational core of the Verification Bridge for selecting relevant claims and designing explicit hardening routes without methodological theater.

# 4C — Governance of Claim Status Change

## 1. Objective
Define how the framework governs the change or non-change of a claim's status after it has passed through an explicit hardening route. Its function is to turn the pathways designed in 4B into formal status consequences for each `claim_upgrade_candidate`, prevent partial support gains from becoming unjustified upgrade, and avoid leaving everything in a technically elegant but methodologically undecided ambiguity.

## 2. Why it matters
Without 4C, Phase 4 could design plausible pathways and still fail in its main function. Failure would appear in two opposite forms. The first would be that any partial support gain ended up inflating the claim by narrative inertia. The second would be that, even after hardening work, the system could not say with precision whether the claim advanced, weakened, remained pending, should stop being pursued, or merely retained the same status.

4C matters because it turns hardening routes into explicit status consequences. It does not add a new theory of the claim; it executes the already inherited governance at the decisive moment of the bridge: which outcome corresponds after the hardening attempt and what semantic ceiling remains allowed from that point onward.

## 3. What 4C governs exactly
4C governs the status consequence that corresponds to a `claim_upgrade_candidate` once its pathway, local evidence, baseline, methodological dependencies, boundary, and domain of validity have been examined. It does not rediscuss the general governance of the claim already fixed in Phase 0, it does not redesign pathways, and it does not perform terminal verification. Its function is narrower and harder: to decide which outcome corresponds and how much language the claim supports after that hardening attempt.

This means governing the relationship among:
- claim;
- executed or partially executed pathway;
- local evidence obtained or still missing;
- hardened or still-weak baseline;
- methodological dependencies resolved or unresolved;
- restrictions preserved or lost;
- and the claim's material value for the reading of the case.

## 4. Permitted outcomes
The permitted outcomes of 4C are exactly five:
- `maintain`
- `degrade`
- `hold`
- `block`
- `upgrade`

No additional outcomes, ornamental substates, long maturity scales, or continuous verification-readiness scores exist. The MVP needs few outcomes, hard and useful ones.

### 4.1 maintain
`maintain` means that the claim preserves its prior status because hardening work did not materially increase its support, but also did not weaken it enough to require degradation. The claim remains alive in the case and preserves its prior semantic ceiling. It does not advance, but it does not recede either.

### 4.2 degrade
`degrade` means that hardening work revealed that the claim had been formulated with more force than its support actually allows. The claim does not necessarily disappear, but it must return to a weaker, more conditioned, or narrower form in both boundary and language.

### 4.3 hold
`hold` means that the claim remains material and deserves to stay alive, but the bridge cannot assign upgrade, degrade, or definitive maintain because a decisive methodological dependency is still missing. The claim is not cancelled; it is suspended while waiting for an unresolved hardening condition.

### 4.4 block
`block` means that the claim must no longer remain active as a hardening candidate in the bridge. This may be because of low materiality, disproportionate methodological cost, unjustifiable route, ungovernable boundary, or lack of real value for changing the reading of the case. `block` protects epistemic resources; it does not express analytical frustration.

### 4.5 upgrade
`upgrade` means that the support of the claim increased materially and traceably, without erasing critical restrictions, such that the claim may elevate to a stronger and still bounded formulation. It does not mean total verification, confirmed savings, final compliance, closed causality, or terminal recommendation.

## 5. General rule of default no-upgrade
The system operates under a simple and hard rule: **default no-upgrade**. No claim rises unless sufficiently structured proof exists in favor of doing so. The bridge does not reward methodological effort with automatic ascent; it requires a material support improvement that survives boundary, baseline, methodological dependency, and critical restriction.

If that improvement does not appear, the claim does not rise. It must be maintained, degraded, placed on hold, or blocked as appropriate. The default is not ascent; it is containment.

## 6. What a legitimate upgrade requires
A legitimate upgrade is not recognized by technical tone or by partial evidence improvement. It is recognized by the accumulation of hard conditions that materially increase the support of the claim without erasing its limits. A claim may rise only if the constitutive conditions of the bridge remain satisfied simultaneously.

### 6.1 Delimited claim
The claim must be formulated with enough precision to know what exactly is being asserted and which exact part of the case is trying to harden. A broad, ambivalent, or narratively comfortable claim cannot rise legitimately because it is unclear what truly gained support.

### 6.2 Explicit boundary
The boundary must remain explicit and governable. Upgrade may occur only within a delimited system, subsystem, period, operating regime, or condition. If support improvement exists only within a narrow boundary, the outcome does not authorize automatic extrapolation outside it.

### 6.3 Material local evidence
There must be material local evidence and not merely reinforced plausibility. Benchmark, proxy, sector custom, improved technical wording, or narrative consistency do not replace local evidence. Upgrade requires that the new support affect the claim from the site, asset, operation, or field trigger that matters.

### 6.4 Visible methodological dependency
The claim cannot rise while hiding methodological dependencies. If the pathway still depended on an unobserved decisive variable, an unconfirmed sequence, insufficient instrumentation, or an unverified trigger, then upgrade is not legitimate. A claim may rise only if critical dependencies were resolved or explicitly reduced to a level compatible with the new status.

### 6.5 Preserved restrictions
Legitimate upgrade does not erase critical restrictions. If the new formulation needs to hide conflict, lose conditionality, dilute uncertainty, or relax operating limits in order to sound stronger, then there is no upgrade; there is hardening by presentation.

### 6.6 Delimited domain of validity
The claim may only rise within a delimited domain of validity. Even when support improves, the system does not acquire license to universalize it. The domain of validity must remain visible: under which conditions the claim rises, in what scope, with which limits, and without which extrapolations.

## 7. Difference between legitimate upgrade and hardening by presentation
A legitimate upgrade changes support. Hardening by presentation only changes the visible form of the claim: the order, the technical density, the appearance of coherence, or the ease with which it is narrated.

If the boundary remains blurry, the baseline remains weak, or critical restrictions disappear so that the claim sounds better, there is no upgrade. There is hardened presentation. No formal improvement of the package, the prose, or the technical framing raises the status of a claim by itself.

## 8. maintain, hold, degrade, block, and upgrade are not synonyms of subjective confidence
The outcomes of 4C do not represent psychological states of the analyst or soft impressions of confidence. They do not mean “it sounds good,” “it seems strong,” “it convinces me,” or “it still does not close for me.”

Each outcome is a structural decision about the relationship among claim, pathway, local evidence, baseline, boundary, methodological dependencies, and material value for the case. That is the positive capacity of the subphase: turning hardening work into comparable, traceable, and semantically governed consequences. Operationally, the correct reading follows a short sequence: first decide whether the claim still deserves bridge resources or should leave the bridge; then whether a critical dependency prevents closing its state; and only then whether support stayed where it was, fell, or rose. Treating outcomes as methodological moods destroys the usefulness of the bridge and replaces it with an aesthetics of expert judgment without hard rules.

## 9. Rules of degrade
`degrade` applies when hardening work reveals that the claim rested on assumptions that were too strong, on a baseline that was too weak, on a boundary that was too broad, or on an operating reading less stable than initially assumed.

`degrade` is correct when:
- new support partly contradicts the claim's prior intensity;
- the operating reference weakens;
- the claim remains sustainable only in a narrower or more conditional formulation;
- or the information obtained reduces the legitimate scope of the claim without eliminating it completely.

`degrade` does not mean automatically discarding the claim. It returns the claim to proportionality. It is the correct outcome when the bridge learned something relevant that obliges speaking with less force, but not with total silence.

## 10. Rules of hold
`hold` applies when the claim remains material and the hardening route remains pertinent, but a decisive methodological dependency is missing in order to assign a more definitive outcome.

`hold` is correct when:
- there was no access to the relevant historian;
- alarms could not be exported;
- the real control sequence could not be sufficiently confirmed;
- the field trigger is still absent;
- or a key instrumental or operational dependency prevents closing the hardening attempt.

`hold` is not indecision. It is correct state governance when the claim remains alive, the pathway remains justified, and the problem is not one of material value but of dependency not yet resolved.

## 11. Rules of block
`block` applies when the claim should no longer remain active as a hardening target. The bridge must block when pursuing the claim does not materially change the case, when the route is methodologically disproportionate, when the claim lacks a governable boundary, or when the pathway no longer has real justification.

`block` is correct when:
- the claim has low material impact;
- hardening it would not alter prioritization, viability, regulatory judgment, or validation agenda;
- the route required would be costly or heavy without relevant epistemic return;
- or the claim depends on assumptions so weak that it no longer deserves to consume bridge resources.

`block` does not weaken the framework. It protects epistemic resources and avoids artificial completeness.

## 12. Rules of maintain
`maintain` applies when hardening work leaves the claim essentially where it was: without material support gain and without material loss of proportionality. The claim preserves its place, its relevance, and its prior semantic formulation.

`maintain` is correct when:
- review found no sufficient evidence for upgrade;
- nor did sufficiently strong evidence appear for degrade;
- the claim remains valid as a preliminary or conditioned reading;
- and no new critical dependency exists that would force hold.

`maintain` is not an elegant version of non-decision. It is the correct outcome when the real methodological consequence is proportional continuity.

## 13. Rules of upgrade
`upgrade` applies only when the claim's support increased materially and survives all the demands of this subphase:
- delimited claim;
- explicit boundary;
- material local evidence;
- visible and sufficiently resolved methodological dependencies;
- preserved critical restrictions;
- delimited domain of validity.

In addition, `upgrade` is correct only if the claim may become stronger without turning into total verification, confirmed savings, final compliance, closed causality, or terminal recommendation. The outcome elevates the claim to a stronger and still bounded form; it does not turn it into unrestricted truth.

## 14. Do-not-upgrade flags
`do_not_upgrade_flags` are explicit conditions that prohibit upgrade even when some partial support improvement exists. Their function is to prevent the system from rewarding incomplete evidence with a semantic ascent that still does not correspond.

Among the minimum flags that 4C must be able to recognize are:
- non-explicit boundary;
- still-missing decisive local evidence;
- non-hardened baseline when the claim depends on reference;
- unresolved critical methodological dependency;
- instrumentation gap on a decisive variable;
- unconfirmed trigger field;
- support that remains only testimonial;
- loss of critical restriction in the new formulation;
- presentation gain without material support gain.

A `do_not_upgrade_flag` does not by itself decide whether the final outcome will be `maintain`, `hold`, `degrade`, or `block`, but it does prevent `upgrade`.

## 15. Relationship between outcome and allowed language
4C does not only decide internal state. It also fixes the semantic ceiling allowed afterward. Each outcome governs how much language the claim supports and what type of formulation is no longer admissible.

### 15.1 If the outcome is maintain
If the outcome is `maintain`, the allowed language preserves the prior ceiling of the claim. It may sustain the provisional or conditioned reading that already existed, but it may not intensify it. It may not sound stronger merely because methodological work occurred around the claim.

### 15.2 If the outcome is degrade
If the outcome is `degrade`, language must weaken with it. It must make explicit lower force, greater conditionality, narrower boundary, or weaker baseline. Any formulation that keeps the prior intensity after degradation is semantically inadmissible.

### 15.3 If the outcome is hold
If the outcome is `hold`, language may keep the claim alive as a material front and as a pending pathway, but it must make visible the unresolved dependency. It may not sound like a hardened finding. It must sound like a claim still suspended while waiting for decisive support.

### 15.4 If the outcome is block
If the outcome is `block`, the allowed language may no longer treat the claim as an active hardening front. It may mention it as blocked, non-prioritized, or methodologically not pursued, but not as a material reading still in ascent.

### 15.5 If the outcome is upgrade
If the outcome is `upgrade`, language may become stronger, but only within the explicit boundary and domain of validity. It must preserve critical restrictions and may not slide into total-verification language, final compliance, confirmed savings, or terminal decision.

## 16. Normative examples

### 16.1 Technical claim that can rise in a bounded way
Case: Phase 2 detected a dominant refrigeration tension. 4B designed a pathway with alarms, control sequences, cycles, and historian review. Local evidence confirms anomalous behavior consistent with deficient sequencing in a delimited subsystem.

The correct outcome here may be `upgrade`, but only in a bounded way. Terminal verification does not apply because the new support does not authorize claiming confirmed savings, closed causality over the whole HVAC system, or automatic extrapolation to the entire site. What it does authorize is a stronger reading of deficient control performance in the delimited subsystem under observed operating conditions.

That upgrade is legitimate because:
- the claim was delimited;
- the boundary is explicit;
- local evidence is material and not merely testimonial;
- the real sequence was sufficiently corroborated;
- and restrictions remain visible.

An incorrect formulation would be: `main HVAC system failure confirmed`. That sentence violates boundary, extrapolates beyond the domain of validity, and turns local technical support into terminal closure.

A proportional formulation would be: a stronger reading of deficient control performance in the delimited subsystem, still conditioned and not automatically extrapolable to the HVAC system as a whole. That expresses upgrade without theater.

### 16.2 Technical claim that must remain on hold
Same technical case, but now there was no access to the historian, only operating testimony exists, alarms could not be exported, and the real sequence was not sufficiently confirmed.

The correct outcome is `hold`. `upgrade` does not apply because decisive methodological dependency is missing. `block` does not apply either, because the claim remains material and the pathway still makes sense. Nor does `degrade` apply automatically, because no evidence has yet appeared that reduces the prior proportionality of the claim; what is missing is the critical support needed to move it.

Here the claim stays alive. `hold` does not express analyst indecision. It expresses a hard decision: the claim may not advance while historian access, alarm export, or sufficient sequence confirmation remain absent. The bridge did its job by turning that gap into an explicit outcome.

### 16.3 Generic hypothesis that must be blocked
Case: generic lighting opportunity, low material impact, costly or irrelevant hardening route, and no real capacity to change case prioritization.

The correct outcome is `block`. Continuing to pursue the claim simply because it is typical of the sector would be bad practice. The bridge does not exist to demonstrate total coverage; it exists to concentrate methodological resources where hardening matters.

`block` does not weaken the framework. It protects it. It avoids spending effort on a claim whose eventual hardening would not materially alter case reading. The anti-example here is clear: continuing to pursue lighting “because it is usually an easy opportunity” displaces focus from more relevant claims toward artificial completeness.

### 16.4 Regulatory claim that should be maintained or put on hold
Case: plausible regulatory pressure, but field trigger still missing.

Here `upgrade` does not apply. Confirmed applicability remains outside scope while the relevant trigger is missing. The correct outcome may be `maintain` or `hold`, depending on the exact position of the claim.

`maintain` applies when the review preserves the existing preliminary reading without strengthening or weakening it. For example, if GFA, occupancy type, or asset classification remain compatible with the already identified plausible regulatory pressure, but no sufficient new support appears to elevate the claim.

`hold` applies when the pathway already depends on a specific trigger that remains absent or inaccessible, for example:
- GFA not confirmed;
- occupancy type not closed;
- filing status unavailable;
- asset classification ambiguous;
- jurisdictional matching not yet confirmed.

In both cases the central deformation is avoided: turning regulatory plausibility into confirmed applicability. `maintain` preserves the preliminary reading. `hold` keeps the claim alive, but suspended because of unresolved dependency. Neither authorizes `upgrade`.

### 16.5 Legitimate degradation
Case: an opportunity candidate looked strong because it rested on an operating assumption; hardening work reveals that the real operating schedule was much more variable or unstable and that the associated baseline weakens.

The correct outcome is `degrade`. Immediate dismissal does not apply because there may still be material signal. But it is correct to return the claim to proportionality: lower force, greater conditionality, explicitly weakened baseline, and lower capacity to sustain strong prioritization.

`degrade` is correct precisely because it distinguishes partial support loss from total invalidation. Blocking it immediately would be excessive. Maintaining it unchanged would be inflationary. Degrading it returns the claim to a form compatible with what is now actually known.

## 17. What 4C produces exactly
4C produces structured and governable outputs. It does not produce vague technical impressions or rhetorical closures.

### 17.1 claim_upgrade_decision_map
Contains, for each `claim_upgrade_candidate`, the assigned outcome, the structural basis for the decision, the relevant boundary, the domain of validity, the active restrictions, and the semantic ceiling allowed from that outcome onward.

### 17.2 do_not_upgrade_register
Contains active `do_not_upgrade_flags` per claim and the structural reasons why upgrade remains explicitly prohibited even if some partial support improvement occurred.

## 18. What would be over-engineering in 4C
The following would be over-engineering in 4C:
- introducing long scales of confidence or maturity;
- creating substates such as `almost-upgrade`, `soft-hold`, or equivalents;
- designing continuous verification-readiness scores;
- multiplying outcomes in order to capture nuances that may be resolved through boundary, restrictions, and allowed language;
- or turning the subphase into analyst psychology rather than outcome governance.

The MVP needs few outcomes and clear consequences. Everything else erodes decisional clarity without improving real governance.

## 19. Typical errors that 4C must block
- Treating any partial evidence improvement as sufficient justification for `upgrade`.
- Using better presentation, better order, or better narrative as if they increased support.
- Treating outcomes as subjective states of confidence.
- Failing to distinguish `hold` from `block`.
- Failing to distinguish `maintain` from `degrade`.
- Allowing a claim to rise without preserving critical restrictions.
- Hardening visible language without corresponding formal outcome.

Each of these errors produces the same underlying deformation: confusing change in support with change in impression. 4C exists precisely to prevent that substitution.

## 20. Exact decisions that 4C closes
- 4C governs the status consequence of the claim after the hardening attempt, not the general governance of the claim nor the design of the pathway.
- The only permitted outcomes are `maintain`, `degrade`, `hold`, `block`, and `upgrade`.
- The system operates under default no-upgrade.
- `upgrade` requires delimited claim, explicit boundary, material local evidence, visible and sufficiently resolved methodological dependencies, preserved restrictions, and delimited domain of validity.
- `upgrade` does not mean total verification, confirmed savings, final compliance, closed causality, or terminal recommendation.
- Outcomes are not subjective states or soft scores.
- Each outcome fixes an allowed semantic ceiling for the claim.
- `do_not_upgrade_flags` are explicit prohibitions against upgrade in the presence of still-active structural deficiencies.
- 4C produces at minimum `claim_upgrade_decision_map` and `do_not_upgrade_register`.

## 21. Completion criterion
Subphase 4C is considered closed when there is no longer ambiguity about which outcome corresponds to a claim after the bridge, what a legitimate upgrade requires, what differentiates `maintain` from `hold`, what differentiates `hold` from `block`, what differentiates `degrade` from `maintain`, and how each outcome governs later allowed language.

At that point, future implementation may no longer reinterpret 4C as a soft scale of confidence or as an elegant layer of technical opinions. It may only build it as what has been closed here: the decisional layer of the Verification Bridge for determining, with discipline and without semantic inflation, which claims advance, which do not, and why.

# 4D — MVP Output Contract

## 1. Objective
Define the output contract of the Verification Bridge MVP as a small, sufficient, and governable set of structured outputs. Its function is to leave no ambiguity about what actually comes out of Phase 4, what the minimum function of each output is, how they relate to one another, and what remains explicitly outside the current contract.

## 2. Why it matters
Without an output contract, Phase 4 faces two symmetric risks. The first is becoming methodologically attractive but operationally diffuse: claims theoretically hardened, yet without concrete objects that allow the bridge to be built, reviewed, and governed. The second is inflating itself with redundant pseudo-outputs that multiply surface area without changing any real decision.

4D matters because it closes the translation of candidate, pathway, baseline, instrumentation, and outcome into minimum formal objects. Thanks to them the bridge is not only described; it becomes buildable, auditable, and capable of moving claim decisions without losing granularity. The value of the MVP is not measured by how many outputs it has, but by whether those outputs change real decisions, sustain explicit hardening routes, or protect a real semantic transition.

## 3. What Phase 4 must produce and what it must not
Phase 4 must produce a governed chain of structured registers that makes explicit:
- which claims were admitted to the bridge;
- which hardening pathway corresponds to each one;
- which local evidence actually matters;
- which baseline must be hardened when applicable;
- which instrumentation or observability gaps limit or block the pathway;
- which final outcome corresponds;
- and which barriers make upgrade inadmissible.

It must not produce final reports or aggregated blobs that mix candidate, pathway, evidence, baseline, instrumentation, and decision into one opaque object. The contract fixes minimum required content and governance dependencies among outputs, not final software design, persistence, or export formats.

## 4. Authorized formal outputs of the MVP
The authorized formal outputs of the MVP are exactly seven:
- `claim_upgrade_candidate_register`
- `verification_pathway_register`
- `required_site_evidence_register`
- `baseline_hardening_register`
- `instrumentation_gap_register`
- `claim_upgrade_decision_map`
- `do_not_upgrade_register`

No additional outputs exist within the current contract. There is no `verification summary`, `confidence pack`, `readiness note`, aggregated `verification package`, or stakeholder-specific variant.

## 5. Nature and minimum function of each output

### 5.1 claim_upgrade_candidate_register
This is the master output of the subphase and the anchor of the entire contract. It contains the claims that were actually admitted to the Verification Bridge and for which the system recognizes legitimate methodological hardening cost. It is not just one output among many; it is the register on which pathway, evidence, baseline, instrumentation, and decision depend.

### 5.2 verification_pathway_register
Contains the explicit hardening route associated with each candidate. Its function is to make visible how the support of the claim could increase and under which material logic: documentary contrast, log review, historian review, field observation, trigger confirmation, delimited comparison, or measurement when applicable.

### 5.3 required_site_evidence_register
Contains the local evidence required per claim. Its function is to prevent the bridge from dissolving into vague formulas such as “more data are needed.” It must make visible which site evidence truly matters for the claim and why that evidence has material value for the pathway.

### 5.4 baseline_hardening_register
Contains baseline-hardening work whenever the claim depends on an operational or quantitative reference that is still too weak. Its function is to separate clearly the problem of the claim from the problem of the reference on which the claim aims to rely.

### 5.5 instrumentation_gap_register
Contains gaps in sensors, historian, temporal resolution, access, observability, or instrumental boundary that limit or block the pathway. Its function is not to decorate the case with technical missing pieces, but to make explicit when the bridge cannot harden a claim because the observation infrastructure does not yet support that attempt.

### 5.6 claim_upgrade_decision_map
Contains the formal status consequence per claim after bridge work. Its function is to fix the corresponding outcome, the allowed semantic ceiling, and the structural basis of the decision. It is the point where the pathway ceases to be mere design and becomes governed consequence.

### 5.7 do_not_upgrade_register
Contains the flags that prohibit upgrade even when some partial support improvement exists. Its function is to protect the semantic transition of the framework and prevent incomplete evidence, weak baseline, absent trigger, or unresolved critical dependency from turning into narrative ascent.

## 6. Structural relationship among outputs
The structural relationship of the contract is not optional. `claim_upgrade_candidate_register` precedes and supports all other outputs. Without admitted candidate, there is no legitimate pathway, no pertinent required evidence, no baseline to harden, no relevant instrumentation gap, and no governable outcome decision.

`verification_pathway_register` derives from the candidate and organizes the material logic of hardening. `required_site_evidence_register` and `baseline_hardening_register` depend on the pathway and must not dissolve into generic notes. `instrumentation_gap_register` also depends on the candidate and the pathway, but its function is different: it makes explicit the observability limits that may block, narrow, or condition the hardening attempt.

`claim_upgrade_decision_map` depends on the joint reading of candidate, pathway, local evidence, baseline, and instrumentation gap. `do_not_upgrade_register` depends on that same structure, but it does not duplicate the decision: it protects against inadmissible upgrades and may coexist with outcomes such as `maintain`, `hold`, `degrade`, or `block`.

For the same reason, a `claim_upgrade_decision_map` without anchored candidate, a `do_not_upgrade_register` without identifiable claim, or an `instrumentation_gap_register` detached from its pathway do not constitute valid bridge outputs. The contract does not define objects only; it defines mandatory dependencies among them.

## 7. Minimum required content of each output
The contract fixes minimum conceptual requirements. It does not yet fix exhaustive technical schemas or final database design.

### 7.1 Minimums for claim_upgrade_candidate_register
It must contain, at minimum:
- an identifiable claim traceable to its upstream origin;
- the material reason why it enters the bridge;
- the preliminary boundary of the claim;
- the expected value of hardening for the reading of the case;
- and an explicit link to the technical, regulatory, or operational front that justifies its admission.

### 7.2 Minimums for verification_pathway_register
It must contain, at minimum:
- the claim to which it responds;
- the explicit logic of the pathway;
- the type of hardening required;
- visible methodological dependencies;
- and relevant blocking conditions.

### 7.3 Minimums for required_site_evidence_register
It must contain, at minimum:
- the claim and pathway it serves;
- the required local evidence in concrete terms;
- the reason why that evidence matters materially;
- the operational, documentary, or instrumental locus of acquisition;
- and the relationship between that evidence and the possible status change of the claim.

### 7.4 Minimums for baseline_hardening_register
It must contain, at minimum:
- the claim for which baseline matters;
- the nature of the reference to be hardened;
- the relevant period, condition, or operating regime;
- the dominant variable that must be stabilized or clarified;
- and the reason why the current baseline does not yet support the claim.

### 7.5 Minimums for instrumentation_gap_register
It must contain, at minimum:
- the affected claim or pathway;
- the concrete sensor, historian, resolution, access, or observability gap;
- an explanation of why that gap is material;
- and the effect of the gap on the pathway: limitation, hold, block, or prohibition of upgrade.

### 7.6 Minimums for claim_upgrade_decision_map
It must contain, at minimum:
- the evaluated claim;
- the assigned outcome;
- the structural basis of the decision;
- the pertinent boundary and domain of validity;
- the active preserved restrictions;
- and the semantic ceiling allowed from that outcome onward.

### 7.7 Minimums for do_not_upgrade_register
It must contain, at minimum:
- the affected claim;
- the active flag or flags;
- the structural reason for each prohibition;
- and the missing condition that would have to be resolved in order to lift the no-upgrade barrier.

## 8. Rule of contractual compactness
Every output of the Phase 4 MVP must fulfill at least one of these three functions:
- change a real decision;
- sustain an explicit hardening route;
- or protect a real semantic transition.

If an output does not do one of those three things, it does not deserve to exist in the MVP. This compactness rule blocks both vagueness and proliferation. The bridge does not need more objects; it needs objects that govern real consequences.

## 9. Sparse cases and valid asymmetry
A sparse case does not weaken the contract. It proves it. The contract remains correct even if it produces:
- very few entries in `claim_upgrade_candidate_register`;
- few truly justifiable pathways;
- much required evidence concentrated in basic discovery;
- dominant baseline hardening;
- several central instrumentation gaps;
- mostly `hold` or `block` outcomes;
- and a `do_not_upgrade_register` heavier than the `claim_upgrade_decision_map` in upgrades.

There is no obligation of symmetry among outputs nor among cases. A rich case may populate almost the entire contract with density. A sparse case may leave some registers very thin or even empty without that constituting failure. What would be incorrect would be inflating it with repetitive or speculative content so that it “looks complete.”

## 10. Normative examples

### 10.1 Rich case with multiple candidates
Case: an industrial system shows material tensions in refrigeration and sequencing; there is plausible regulatory pressure; there are candidate opportunities in compressed air and power quality; part of the site has good observability.

The correct output here is not a single aggregated `verification package`. That object would erase granularity among claims with different boundaries, different pathways, different observability, and different probable outcomes. The correct contract requires multiple entries in `claim_upgrade_candidate_register`: for example, one technical refrigeration candidate, another sequencing candidate, one plausible regulatory candidate, and separate candidates for compressed air and power quality if they truly passed eligibility.

`verification_pathway_register` must differentiate them. Refrigeration may require historian review, control sequences, alarms, and cycles; compressed air may depend on leakage observation and profile logging; power quality may require delimited contrast over events or load conditions; the regulatory front may rest on documentary trigger confirmation and jurisdictional matching. Merging them into one pathway would degrade governability.

`required_site_evidence_register` must also remain separated by claim. Setpoints, alarms, control sequences, and historian trends do not fulfill the same function as GFA, occupancy type, filing status, or asset classification. `baseline_hardening_register` will appear only where an operational or quantitative reference must be hardened. It may be central in compressed air or power quality and secondary or irrelevant in the regulatory claim. `instrumentation_gap_register` may concentrate on certain fronts and be almost null on others; that is a virtue of the contract, not a defect.

The `claim_upgrade_decision_map` may end with mixed outcomes: bounded `upgrade` for a well-observed refrigeration subsystem, `hold` for compressed air if temporal resolution is missing, `maintain` or `hold` in the regulatory claim while the trigger does not appear, and `block` for some weak candidate that no longer justifies methodological cost. `do_not_upgrade_register` must exist for claims that remain too weak even though the case, as a whole, is rich. That is precisely why an aggregated blob would be incorrect: it would hide that the bridge decides per claim and not per package.

### 10.2 Sparse case
Case: few relevant claims, very weak local evidence, no historian, and dominant uncertainty.

The contract remains correct even if it produces very few entries in `claim_upgrade_candidate_register`, one or two minimum pathways, very focused required evidence, dominant baseline hardening, central instrumentation gaps, several `hold` or `block` outcomes in the `claim_upgrade_decision_map`, and a clear and heavy `do_not_upgrade_register`.

What is correct here is for `required_site_evidence_register` to matter more than any illusion of upgrade. The bridge may say that the first real work is to confirm schedule, control architecture, presence or absence of historian, and minimum observable variables. `baseline_hardening_register` may carry much of the weight if the operating reference of the case remains too fragile. `instrumentation_gap_register` may become the most informative output in the case without degrading the contract.

What would be incorrect would be generating multiple outputs full of repetitive or speculative content in order to give an appearance of completeness. Duplicating pathways without evidence, multiplying low-value candidates, or disguising uncertainty with ornamental text would degrade the usefulness of the bridge. An honest sparse case may be short, asymmetric, and methodologically strong.

### 10.3 Regulatory claim
Case: plausible regulatory pressure, trigger field not yet confirmed.

This case requires differentiated outputs and not a single aggregated note because each function of the contract resolves a different question. In `claim_upgrade_candidate_register`, the regulatory claim must appear as an explicit candidate if hardening it can change regulatory judgment or validation agenda for the case. The candidate does not assert applicability; it asserts material relevance of the front.

In `verification_pathway_register`, the correct pathway will be mainly documentary and trigger-confirmation based: review of GFA, occupancy type, filing status, asset classification, jurisdictional matching, or other case equivalents. `required_site_evidence_register` must list exactly that required local evidence. This is where it becomes clear why an aggregated note is insufficient: the claim needs precise evidence and not a general feeling of plausibility.

`baseline_hardening_register` may be irrelevant or clearly secondary here if the claim does not depend on a quantitative operating reference. `instrumentation_gap_register` may also be irrelevant or lateral if the bottleneck is documentary and not instrumental. That asymmetry is correct and the contract must support it. In `claim_upgrade_decision_map`, the outcome will probably be `maintain` or `hold`, depending on whether the review preserves the preliminary reading or already depends on a specific trigger still absent. And in `do_not_upgrade_register`, the explicit barrier must appear: there can be no `upgrade` while the trigger field remains unconfirmed. A single comment such as “everything suggests it applies” would destroy case governability.

## 11. What remains explicitly outside the MVP contract
The following remain explicitly outside the current contract:
- final `verification report`;
- compliance packet;
- savings verification report;
- terminal investment memo;
- live dashboard;
- multi-stakeholder package;
- separate audience views;
- ornamental summary outputs;
- complex export bundles;
- and any pseudo-output created merely to present the bridge better.

None of the seven authorized outputs is a final document for end users. They are structured objects of bridge governance, internal auditability, and disciplined construction of the path toward real hardening.

## 12. What would be over-engineering in 4D
The following would be over-engineering in 4D:
- creating an aggregated output that mixes candidate, pathway, evidence, baseline, instrumentation, and decision in a single object;
- introducing new outputs because of completeness anxiety, such as `verification summary`, `readiness note`, or `confidence pack`;
- duplicating outputs by stakeholder too early;
- requiring symmetry between rich case and sparse case;
- turning bridge outputs into pseudo-final reports;
- or sliding the subphase toward exhaustive schemas, APIs, or export bundles before closing the minimum contract.

The MVP needs a small, governable, and sufficient contract. Everything else is ontological inflation or software moved ahead of semantics that are not yet necessary.

## 13. Typical errors that 4D must block
- Creating a single aggregated output that erases the difference among candidate, pathway, evidence, baseline, instrumentation, and decision. That error makes it opaque what actually changed and destroys governance granularity.
- Creating new outputs because of completeness anxiety. `Verification summary`, `readiness note`, or equivalents add surface area, not decision.
- Duplicating outputs by stakeholder too early. That proliferation turns a methodological bridge into a taxonomy of packaging.
- Requiring a sparse case to have the same output density as a rich case. That requirement produces filler and speculation.
- Converting bridge outputs into pseudo-final reports. That erases the boundary with terminal reporting and later strong verification.
- Treating `do_not_upgrade_register` as decorative residue. If it loses force, the contract stops protecting the semantic transition that 4C already closed.

Each of these errors degrades framework governability by replacing objects with real function by objects with only apparent function.

## 14. Exact decisions that 4D closes
- Phase 4 ends the MVP bridge in exactly seven formal outputs.
- `claim_upgrade_candidate_register` is the master output of the contract and the others structurally depend on it.
- `verification_pathway_register`, `required_site_evidence_register`, `baseline_hardening_register`, `instrumentation_gap_register`, `claim_upgrade_decision_map`, and `do_not_upgrade_register` exist because they fulfill distinct and non-redundant functions.
- The contract fixes minimum required content, not detailed technical implementation.
- No output in the contract is equivalent to a final verification report, compliance packet, live dashboard, or terminal end-user document.
- Every MVP output must change a real decision, sustain an explicit hardening route, or protect a real semantic transition.
- Asymmetry between rich cases and sparse cases is valid and does not require ornamental compensation.
- The contract exists to prevent both operational vagueness and ontological proliferation.

## 15. Completion criterion
Subphase 4D is considered closed when there is no longer ambiguity about what comes out of Phase 4 at the end of the Verification Bridge MVP, what the minimum function of each output is, why `claim_upgrade_candidate_register` is the master output, how the seven objects relate to one another, what minimums each one must support, and what remains outside the current contract.

At that point, future implementation may no longer reinterpret the bridge as an aggregated blob, a pseudo-final report, or an open collection of optional outputs. It may only build it as what has been closed here: a small, sufficient, and governable contract for materializing claims, pathways, required evidence, baseline, instrumentation, outcome decisions, and explicit no-upgrade barriers.

# 4E — Completion Criterion and Handoff

## 1. Objective
Define the exact point at which Phase 4 may be considered closed as a serious, buildable, and methodologically complete MVP. Its function is to fix what becomes frozen for implementation, what may vary technically without breaking the phase, what minimum proof the Verification Bridge must be able to sustain, and how the framework continues after the MVP under more demanding conditions, especially when the next serious step already requires real field data.

## 2. Why it matters
Without a closing subphase, Phase 4 remains exposed to two opposite errors. The first is being declared finished because of written volume, conceptual sophistication, or number of defined objects, without having demonstrated real operational integrity. The second is never closing because the final verification vision of the project does not yet exist inside the MVP.

4E matters because it resolves that tension with a hard rule: Phase 4 closes when the Verification Bridge already exists as a stable, compact, and buildable methodological contract. It does not need to exhaust future strong verification in order to be a complete phase. It does need to close semantics, outputs, outcomes, structural relationships, and minimum proof of honest functioning across more than one case type.

## 3. What must remain frozen when closing Phase 4
When closing Phase 4, the following decisions must remain frozen, and therefore outside silent reinterpretation by implementation:
- that Phase 4 is a layer of epistemic hardening and not total verification;
- that its central unit is `claim_upgrade_candidate`;
- that not every hypothesis deserves bridge;
- that the bridge works through explicit pathways with claim, boundary, required evidence, baseline need when applicable, instrumentation gap, and blocking conditions;
- that the permitted outcomes are exactly `maintain`, `degrade`, `hold`, `block`, and `upgrade`;
- that the system operates under default no-upgrade;
- that `upgrade` requires material increase of local support, explicit boundary, preserved restrictions, and delimited domain of validity;
- that the MVP ends in exactly seven formal outputs and not in final reports;
- that `claim_upgrade_candidate_register` is the master output;
- and that sparse cases are valid and necessary bridge configurations.

One continuity thesis must also remain frozen: the MVP of the bridge does not equal verification with real field data, but it does define, in irreversible form, the minimum serious bridge before entering that terrain.

## 4. What may vary in implementation without breaking the phase
The following may vary, without breaking Phase 4:
- serialization format;
- persistence technology;
- the move from simple tables or registers toward relational or graph structures;
- internal orchestration order;
- the separation among synchronous processes, batch processes, or manual checkpoints;
- and the degree of automation with which the seven outputs are built and maintained.

What may not vary is the methodological meaning of the phase. Implementation may not redefine candidate, pathway, baseline hardening, instrumentation gap, outcome, or do-not-upgrade. It also may not collapse several outputs into free-form notes, turn distinct outcomes into one convenient state, or reinterpret `upgrade` as “almost verified.” Closure freezes semantics; it does not impose a single implementation.

## 5. Minimum integrity proof of the MVP
Phase 4 may only be declared closed if it can sustain, at least conceptually and in buildable form, a complete path from structured upstream inputs to the seven formal outputs of the bridge. That minimum proof must show that the framework can:
- select claims eligible for bridge;
- design explicit pathways;
- separate required evidence, baseline hardening, and instrumentation gaps;
- assign formal outcomes;
- activate `do_not_upgrade_flags` where appropriate;
- and materialize all of that in the output contract without losing per-claim granularity.

This proof does not yet require real field campaigns, integrated historian, executed measurement, or strong verification. It requires something different and prior: that the bridge can already be built without ambiguity and that its logic can sustain real cases without inflation or dilution.

## 6. Minimum proof with a relatively rich case
The proof with a relatively rich case is mandatory because the bridge must demonstrate that it can govern complexity without collapsing into one aggregated object. There must exist at least one case where multiple eligible claims, distinct pathways, heterogeneous local evidence, selective baseline hardening, partial instrumentation gaps, and mixed outcomes coexist.

A rich case proves that Phase 4 can sustain several fronts at once without losing discipline. It also proves that the output contract does not exist only for thin cases, but to organize real complexity: technical claims with good observability, regulatory fronts with still-unconfirmed trigger, candidate opportunities that deserve bridge, and others that must be blocked or placed on hold.

Without this proof, the MVP could appear correct only because it was never forced to discriminate among several material fronts inside the same case.

## 7. Minimum proof with a sparse case
The proof with a sparse case is equally mandatory. A serious framework cannot validate its bridge only on rich cases. It must demonstrate that it can also behave in an honest and useful way when evidence gaps dominate, observability is weak, historian is absent, baseline is fragile, and only few claims are truly hardenable.

A sparse case proves that Phase 4 does not depend on artificial completeness in order to look robust. If the system only looks good when the case already comes with abundant observability and dense candidates, then the bridge is not properly closed. It must be able to produce few candidates, many holds or blocks, central instrumentation gaps, and dominant baseline hardening without filling the case with weak pathways or ornamental pseudo-outputs.

## 8. Closure by sufficiency and not by exhaustiveness
Phase 4 closes by sufficiency, not by exhaustiveness. This means that the MVP does not need to exhaust the entire verification vision of the framework in order to become methodologically complete. It only needs enough for the Verification Bridge to already exist as a serious layer of epistemic hardening, with central unit, explicit pathways, baseline hardening, instrumentation gaps, formal outcomes, and governable output contract.

The following are not required in order to close the phase:
- strong verification executed with field data;
- full integration with historian or site metering;
- final compliance;
- auditable packages for third parties;
- standardized national reproducibility;
- continuous verification engine;
- nor a complete institutional layer of external deliverables.

Sufficiency does not impoverish the framework. It makes it buildable without pretending that the bridge is already the final verification layer.

## 9. What remains explicitly outside the MVP
The following remain explicitly outside the Phase 4 MVP:
- end-to-end executed field campaigns;
- fully deployed instrumentation;
- operational historian integration as a constitutive closure requirement;
- sustained real logging or executed submetering;
- strong pre/post verification comparison;
- savings verification report;
- final compliance;
- terminal evidence packages for third parties;
- continuous verification;
- and a national or fully institutionalized verification layer.

All of that may belong to later scaling. None of it is a condition for declaring the phase closed as a serious MVP.

## 10. What comes after the MVP
What comes after the MVP is not simply “more of the same.” The next serious step of the framework enters a terrain where bridge architecture is no longer sufficient by itself and real site evidence starts becoming indispensable. The minimum bridge of the MVP organizes how a claim could harden; later scaling requires starting to materialize that hardening on real observability, data, and operation. In terms of maturation, the claim moves here from plausible claim to claim with a governed pathway; the next serious jump is already a claim contrasted against sufficient field data.

### 10.1 Operational scaling
Operational scaling introduces real observability conditions that the MVP does not need to have resolved in order to close, but that are necessary in order to rise to stronger verification. This includes, depending on the case:
- accessible historian;
- logging of relevant variables;
- clearer measurement boundaries;
- short or targeted capture campaigns;
- useful operating windows;
- access to real operation;
- reading of effective control sequences;
- and, where needed, submetering or additional instrumentation.

It also introduces very concrete site frictions: incomplete or badly named tags, historian with insufficient depth, partial export because of third-party restrictions, narrow maintenance windows, uptime constraints, shared boundaries between loads, and loss of operational context around overrides or manual modes. This scaling resolves one concrete problem: moving from plausible, well-designed pathways to pathways supported by sufficient real observation.

### 10.2 Methodological scaling
Methodological scaling hardens the way the framework treats baseline, comparison, adjustment, uncertainty, and quantification. Here appear needs that the MVP does not have to exhaust:
- stronger baselines;
- better temporal and operational delimitation;
- more serious adjustments over dominant variables;
- stronger pre/post comparison;
- more quantitative pathways;
- and more explicit, more worked uncertainty.

This scaling resolves another problem: not just observing more, but observing in a way that supports harder and more quantitatively defensible inference.

### 10.3 Institutional and verificatory scaling
Institutional and verificatory scaling turns the architecture of the bridge into packages that are more auditable, more externalizable, and eventually more demanding in front of third parties. That terrain may include:
- more robust evidence packages;
- verificatory deliverables usable by third parties;
- regulatory hardening;
- harder compliance where applicable;
- and, later on, a more continuous, more reproducible, or more standardized verification layer.

That block does not belong to the MVP because it requires something the MVP still does not promise: field evidence strong enough, harder methodology, and, in many cases, greater institutionalization of output use.

## 11. What the next level needs in order to truly exist
The next level of the framework does not exist by architectural will alone. It exists only when sufficient real evidence appears to put the pathway to the test against actual site operation. Depending on claim and case, this requires:
- real field data;
- improved local observability;
- effectively observed dominant variables;
- historian, logging, or operational export where the pathway requires it;
- confirmed field triggers;
- strong site documentary evidence;
- relevant operating windows;
- more robust baselines;
- more stable access to operation;
- and, on many fronts, targeted measurement, short campaign, or delimited submetering.

In other words: after the Verification Bridge MVP, the next serious step can no longer be achieved with more architecture alone. It is achieved when the designed architecture can meet field evidence strong enough to harden the claim, its baseline, and its domain of validity even further. In steam and district energy this usually requires more tightly closed thermal and measurement boundaries; in compressed air, load profiles and leak persistence; in power quality, sufficient resolution and event data; in BMS and controls, effective sequences, overrides, and trustworthy trends. That scaling does not replace candidate, pathway, evidence, baseline, instrumentation, and decision; it subjects them to a harder regime of observation, contrast, and real traceability.

## 12. Relationship between closure of Phase 4 and future implementation
The closure of Phase 4 and the handoff to implementation do not require that the next stage already be built. They require that the semantics of the bridge can no longer be freely reinterpreted. Implementation receives a phase closed in its central units, outcomes, outputs, structural relationships, and minimum proof.

That allows two things at once. First, to build the MVP without reopening candidate, pathway, baseline, instrumentation, or decision. Second, to prepare a reasonable continuity point toward later scaling when access to real field data exists. The correct handoff does not freeze one single software solution; it freezes semantics and leaves room for an implementation able to grow without betraying the bridge.

## 13. Normative examples

### 13.1 Admissible technical variation
An admissible technical variation would be to begin with simple serialized registers or basic tables for the seven bridge outputs and later migrate to a relational database or graph structure without changing the semantics of candidate, pathway, baseline hardening, instrumentation gap, decision map, or do-not-upgrade. It is also admissible to change internal orchestration order as long as the result still preserves the same objects and structural relationships.

This does not break Phase 4 because it changes implementation, not meaning. The bridge still has the same central unit, the same outcomes, the same output contract, and the same no-upgrade discipline. Technology may change; the phase may not.

### 13.2 Improper reopening in implementation
The following would be improper reopening:
- turning `hold` and `block` into one single `pending` state because of UI convenience or technical simplicity;
- eliminating `do_not_upgrade_register` under the excuse that “it can be inferred”;
- merging required evidence, baseline hardening, and instrumentation gap into free-form notes;
- or reinterpreting `upgrade` as “practically verified.”

Each of these changes breaks the phase even if the software works. The problem is not technological, but semantic. If implementation erases hard differences among outcomes, outputs, or upgrade barriers, it stops building the bridge that the phase closed and starts building something else.

### 13.3 Minimum proof with a rich case
A relatively rich case may include technical tensions in refrigeration and sequencing, plausible regulatory pressure, reasonable observability in part of the site, and some additional candidate fronts. The correct minimum proof does not consist in producing a pretty demo, but in demonstrating structural integrity:
- several claims pass into `claim_upgrade_candidate_register`;
- each one receives its own pathway;
- required evidence is separated by claim;
- baseline hardening appears only where it truly matters;
- instrumentation gaps are partial, not uniform;
- `claim_upgrade_decision_map` ends with mixed outcomes;
- and `do_not_upgrade_register` remains alive for claims that still cannot rise.

If the rich case cannot populate those seven outputs coherently without mixing fronts or losing granularity, Phase 4 is not truly closed.

### 13.4 Minimum proof with a sparse case
A sparse case may have few eligible claims, predominant evidence gaps, no historian, weak baseline, and high operating uncertainty. The correct minimum proof shows exactly that:
- few legitimate candidates;
- short and honest pathways;
- highly focused required evidence;
- baseline hardening more important than the immediate possibility of upgrade;
- central instrumentation gaps;
- several `hold` or `block` outcomes;
- and a `do_not_upgrade_register` with real weight.

This does not represent failure of the system. It represents structural honesty. If the sparse case had to be filled with soft claims, decorative pathways, or pseudo-upgrades in order to pass the proof, then the bridge would not be reliable.

### 13.5 Later scaling toward verification with field data
Suppose a technical claim about thermal control or performance whose pathway was well defined in the MVP. The bridge has already been able to do several things:
- delimit the claim;
- fix its boundary;
- list required evidence;
- make baseline need explicit;
- point out instrumentation gaps;
- and perhaps leave the claim on `hold` or produce a bounded `upgrade` if local support was sufficient.

But there is a point beyond which the bridge can no longer advance through better architecture alone. In order to harden that claim further or enter stronger verification, the following are required:
- accessible historian;
- logging of real operation;
- submetering or targeted measurement where appropriate;
- observation in specific operating windows;
- comparison against a harder baseline;
- and confirmation of dominant variables under real site conditions.

In many cases, site problems will also appear that the MVP can only anticipate, not resolve: poor tag quality, incomplete export, equipment operating in manual, inability to isolate a subsystem during production, uptime constraints, or lack of temporal synchronization across sources. In compressed air this may mean being unable to distinguish useful load from persistent leakage; in steam balance, failing to close returns or condensate sufficiently; in power quality, lacking waveform or event data in the correct window. That example shows the correct frontier. The MVP can design and govern the bridge. It cannot replace the field evidence that the next layer needs in order to truly exist.

## 14. What would be over-engineering in 4E
The following would be over-engineering in 4E:
- requiring full-stack integration with historian, logging, and field campaigns before the phase can close;
- designing the next full verification layer from now on;
- turning the “what comes next” section into a long and non-hierarchical wishlist;
- requiring terminal compliance, full M&V, or continuous verification as a condition of current closure;
- or freezing technical implementation details that are not needed in order to protect bridge semantics.

4E must close the MVP with discipline. It must not force it to solve today what can only exist tomorrow with real field data, more observability, and stronger quantitative rigor.

## 15. Typical errors that 4E must block
- Declaring the phase closed because of written volume and not because of conceptual closure and real minimum proof.
- Requiring full historian integration before 4E can close.
- Validating the bridge only with rich cases and not with honest sparse cases.
- Turning “what comes next” into an open wishlist without clear scaling conditions.
- Letting implementation redefine candidate, pathway, outcomes, or outputs.
- Closing Phase 4 as if it already included field verification, final compliance, or terminal packages.

Each of these errors distorts the handoff. Either it closes too early and leaves semantics open, or it delays closure artificially by requiring capabilities that already belong to the next verification step.

## 16. Exact decisions that 4E closes
- Phase 4 becomes closed when the Verification Bridge already exists as a stable, compact, and buildable methodological contract.
- Closure freezes semantics, not one single technical implementation.
- The minimum proof of the MVP must include a relatively rich case and a sparse case.
- Closure is by sufficiency and not by exhaustiveness.
- The next serious layer of the framework already requires real field data, greater observability, and more robust baselines.
- That later continuity does not belong to the current MVP, but it does remain explicitly delimited.
- Implementation may not reopen central units, outcomes, outputs, structural relationships, or the meaning of upgrade.
- Phase 4 is not considered closed because of documentary volume or future aspiration, but because of buildable methodological integrity.

## 17. Completion criterion
Subphase 4E is considered closed when there is no longer ambiguity about:
- what remains frozen when closing Phase 4;
- what may vary technically without breaking it;
- what the minimum integrity proof of the MVP is;
- why that proof requires a rich case and a sparse case;
- what remains outside the MVP;
- and how the framework continues afterward, under harder conditions that already require real field data.

At that point, Phase 4 may be handed to implementation without silent reinterpretation. It becomes sufficiently frozen to be built, sufficiently compact to be governed, and sufficiently honest to make clear that the next serious step can no longer be achieved with more architecture alone, but with verification supported by observability and real site evidence.
