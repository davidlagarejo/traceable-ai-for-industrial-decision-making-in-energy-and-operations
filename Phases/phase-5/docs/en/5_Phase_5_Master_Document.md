# 5_Phase_5_Master_Document
## Probabilistic Finance and Risk

## 1. Objective

Define Phase 5 as the layer that converts technical, operational, verification, and regulatory uncertainty into disciplined financial exposure, probabilistic economic ranges, scenario structure, and value-of-information logic without collapsing uncertainty into deterministic ROI theater.

## 2. Why it matters

Industrial decisions are often financially framed before the physical system is sufficiently understood. The usual failure mode is not only lack of numbers; it is false closure through single-point estimates, hidden baseline weakness, unpriced downside, and financial narratives that sound investable before the case has earned that right.

Phase 5 exists to keep finance useful early without letting finance pretend to be closed early.

## 3. What Phase 5 is and what it is not

Phase 5 is:

- a probabilistic finance layer,
- a risk translation layer,
- a sensitivity and scenario layer,
- a value-of-information layer,
- and a capital-exposure discipline layer.

Phase 5 is not:

- a deterministic ROI calculator,
- a financing memo by default,
- a bankability engine from sparse data,
- a substitute for baseline hardening,
- a substitute for verification,
- or a device for forcing precision when evidence is weak.

## 4. Central epistemic unit

The central unit of Phase 5 is the **`financial_exposure_case`**.

This is the correct unit because financial reasoning in the framework must remain attached to:

- a bounded technical front,
- an evidence posture,
- an uncertainty profile,
- a scenario structure,
- and an intended decision use.

The framework must not emit floating economic numbers detached from claim lineage.

## 5. Problems Phase 5 solves

Phase 5 exists to solve the following recurring failures:

- single-point payback claims built on unverified technical assumptions;
- inability to express downside if a promising claim later weakens;
- inability to justify why additional validation is worth paying for;
- inability to separate screening economics from finance-grade economics;
- and inability to connect uncertainty directly to capital risk.

## 6. Authorized inputs

Phase 5 may consume only governed upstream objects and bounded business context, including:

- Phase 1 physical priors and parameter ranges;
- Phase 2 twin-state structure where relevant;
- Phase 3 operating regime and actionability constraints;
- Phase 4 baseline, measurement, and verification-path information;
- tariffs, rate bands, and energy-cost structure;
- capex libraries, cost analogues, or local cost estimates;
- maintenance and operational cost assumptions;
- financing constraints, hurdle rates, payback norms, and timing;
- risk appetite, irreversibility, and budget constraints.

## 7. Authorized outputs

Phase 5 is authorized to produce:

- `financial_range_register`
- `risk_exposure_map`
- `scenario_band_set`
- `sensitivity_register`
- `downside_upside_profile`
- `information_value_register`
- `decision_finance_posture`

These outputs are useful even before full verification, but they remain bounded by evidence grade.

### 7.1 Required operational objects

To keep Phase 5 operable across low-data and hardened modes, the following operational objects must exist whenever a serious `financial_exposure_case` is opened:

- `financial_assumption_register`
- `tariff_basis_record`
- `cost_basis_record`
- `benefit_driver_register`
- `range_provenance_record`
- `finance_readiness_state_register`

Each `financial_exposure_case` should declare at minimum:

- target decision or action front;
- asset and subsystem boundary;
- baseline dependency state;
- tariff basis state;
- cost basis state;
- benefit-driver family;
- time-horizon basis;
- discount-basis rule;
- regulatory dependency state where relevant;
- and publication ceiling.

No material financial surface should be emitted without those fields.

### 7.2 Finance readiness states

The `decision_finance_posture` should use the following minimum ladder:

| State | Meaning | Maximum outward use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `screening_only` | exposure can be screened, but the case is still dominated by proxy structure | screening and triage only |
| `range_bound_preliminary` | bounded economic ranges exist, but proxy dependence remains material | early briefing and bounded case discussion |
| `bounded_decision_grade` | the case is economically useful for prioritization and no-go logic | decision-grade reporting |
| `held_for_overstatement_risk` | economic reasoning exists, but stronger publication would mislead | hold / internal use |
| `hardened_within_boundary` | baseline, tariff, cost, and implementation boundaries are materially tighter | strongest bounded finance surface |

None of these states implies bankability by default.

### 7.3 Variable maturity dependency

Phase 5 may not rely on report-level tone as a substitute for variable-level readiness.

Every material financial surface must be bounded by the maturity of the variables that support it.

At minimum, Phase 5 must consult:

- `variable_maturity_register`
- `claim_permission_register`
- `decision_permission_register`

before emitting:

- ROI
- payback
- NPV
- IRR
- DSCR
- LTV
- penalty exposure
- carbon-cost exposure
- value-of-information logic

#### 7.3.1 Minimum maturity discipline

The default ladder is:

| Variable state | Maximum finance surface |
| --- | --- |
| `L0` | no numeric financial claim |
| `L1` | directional economics only |
| `L2` | preliminary bounded range |
| `L3` | scenario-based decision-grade range |
| `L4` | strongest bounded financial surface allowed by the domain of validity |

#### 7.3.2 Dependency bottlenecks

No finance surface may outrun the weakest dependency among the variables required to compute it.

Examples:

- numeric ROI may not outrun `benefit_driver`, `CAPEX`, and `control_boundary`;
- penalty exposure may not outrun `jurisdiction`, `regulated_floor_area`, and `trigger_fields`;
- value-of-information may not pretend precision if the dependent technical front is still only proxy-based.

## 8. Prohibited outputs

Phase 5 may not produce:

- deterministic financial closure unsupported by evidence;
- guaranteed savings language;
- bankability by style;
- financing-grade certainty when Phase 4 support is absent;
- hidden single-scenario reasoning presented as objective;
- or a capital recommendation detached from upstream uncertainty.

### 8.1 Anti-theater and pseudo-bankability controls

Phase 5 must explicitly block:

- single-point payback when both benefit and cost sides remain materially proxy-based;
- single-scenario NPV or IRR presentation when range provenance is not explicit;
- finance-readiness promotion while baseline dependency remains unresolved;
- savings monetization that outruns Phase 4 hardening;
- and any wording that makes a bounded financial exposure case sound lender-ready by style alone.

## 9. Permitted claim families

The following claim families are admissible:

- `financial_range_claim`
- `sensitivity_claim`
- `downside_exposure_claim`
- `upside_candidate_claim`
- `value_of_information_claim`
- `capital_fragility_claim`
- `finance_readiness_state_claim`

All are bounded by evidence posture.

## 10. Prohibited claim families

The following are forbidden unless stronger support truly exists:

- `verified_savings_claim`
- `deterministic_roi_claim`
- `bankable_case_claim`
- `financing_closure_claim`
- `guaranteed_payback_claim`

## 11. Low-data mode

With public data, minimum intake, and preliminary technical structure only, Phase 5 may legitimately do the following:

- express range families rather than points;
- separate downside and upside;
- identify which assumptions dominate financial fragility;
- quantify why more evidence matters economically;
- and screen out cases that are too fragile to justify immediate CAPEX commitment.

In low-data mode, Phase 5 belongs primarily to screening-grade and early decision-grade use.

### 11.1 Low-data output ceiling

In low-data mode, Phase 5 may emit:

- directional financial bands;
- downside/upside structure;
- sensitivity ordering;
- information-value logic;
- and economic fragility statements.

It may not emit:

- single-point payback;
- deterministic ROI;
- implied project finance readiness;
- or finance surfaces detached from explicit proxy disclosure.

### 11.2 Canonical output permissions by maturity

The minimum default policy is:

- `ROI L0` -> not admissible
- `ROI L1` -> directional economics only
- `ROI L2` -> preliminary ROI range
- `ROI L3` -> scenario-based ROI
- `ROI L4` -> strongest bounded ROI surface

- `CAPEX L0` -> not admissible
- `CAPEX L1` -> cost benchmark only
- `CAPEX L2` -> preliminary cost range
- `CAPEX L3` -> engineering or vendor-supported cost basis
- `CAPEX L4` -> validated implementation cost

- `tariff L0` -> no energy-cost claim
- `tariff L1` -> proxy energy-cost framing only
- `tariff L2` -> estimated rate class and bounded sensitivity
- `tariff L3` -> bill-based scenario economics
- `tariff L4` -> strongest bounded contractual tariff surface

## 12. Local-evidence mode

When bills, local tariffs, operating profiles, equipment condition, early site observations, or partial baseline evidence enter, Phase 5 may:

- narrow scenario bands;
- tighten downside and upside;
- distinguish operating-mode-dependent financial outcomes;
- and identify which front materially changes capital exposure.

This is stronger than screening, but still not bankable closure by default.

### 12.1 Local-evidence strengthening logic

In this mode, Phase 5 may legitimately tighten:

- tariff basis;
- cost analogues;
- regime-dependent range structure;
- and downside concentration.

But it still may not:

- collapse remaining uncertainty into one-point economics;
- imply financing closure;
- or hide which remaining variables still control the case.

## 13. Hardened mode

Phase 5 may move toward a stronger finance posture only when:

- baseline is materially hardened;
- technical claims have narrowed materially;
- implementation costs are better bounded;
- regulatory exposure is better known;
- and Phase 4 has at least a serious validation architecture, if not already stronger support.

Even then, Phase 5 remains bounded by its domain of validity and may not universalize narrow evidence.

### 13.1 Domain-of-validity rule

Any materially hardened financial case must still declare:

- operating regime boundary;
- tariff regime boundary;
- implementation scope boundary;
- time-horizon boundary;
- benefit categories included;
- and benefit categories excluded or still provisional.

A hardened financial case that does not carry those boundaries is still not admissible as a strong external surface.

## 14. Relationship to Decision-grade and Verification-grade

Phase 5 contributes strongly to Decision-grade because capital decisions are never purely technical.

Phase 5 contributes to Verification-grade only indirectly and conditionally:

- it does not verify performance itself,
- but it hardens materially when verified or verification-supported performance enters.

Finance does not create verification. It reacts to it.

## 15. Relationship with the other phases

**With Phase 1**  
Phase 5 inherits physical uncertainty and may not erase it through economics.

**With Phase 2**  
Phase 5 may use twin structure to model affected scope, dependency, and exposure, but not as proof of realized savings.

**With Phase 3**  
Phase 5 depends heavily on operating regime because many economic outcomes are regime-sensitive.

**With Phase 4**  
Phase 4 is the main hardening bridge for performance claims that later tighten finance.

**With Phase 6**  
Phase 6 may inject regulatory cost, risk, deadline, or compliance exposure into the financial case.

**With Phase 7**  
Phase 7 governs when a financial case strengthens, weakens, or must remain on hold.

**With Phase 8**  
Phase 8 consumes Phase 5 to decide whether to act, defer, validate, or block.

## 16. Upgrade rules

Phase 5 may upgrade only when:

- a new evidence event materially narrows technical uncertainty;
- local tariff or cost data replaces weaker proxies;
- baseline weakness is reduced;
- implementation scope becomes more governable;
- or a major downside factor is eliminated or confirmed.

### 16.1 Canonical finance triggers

The following events are canonical upgrade or re-evaluation triggers for Phase 5:

- local tariff replaces proxy tariff;
- local quote replaces analogue cost;
- baseline narrows materially;
- baseline weakens materially;
- benefit-driver classification changes;
- regulatory-cost front opens or closes;
- implementation boundary narrows;
- or measured performance materially changes the range structure.

## 17. Downgrade, hold, and block rules

`degrade` applies when:

- core technical assumptions weaken;
- cost assumptions widen materially;
- expected value becomes more fragile;
- or prior point estimates must retreat to broader scenario bands.

`hold` applies when:

- a key tariff, capex, baseline, or operating variable remains unresolved;
- stronger finance would be misleading without that input.

`block` applies when:

- the economic front is too weakly linked to the case to justify its publication as a serious decision surface;
- or the apparent finance case depends almost entirely on unverified or unstable technical assumptions.

## 18. Language ceiling

In low-data and partial-evidence modes, preferred language includes:

- financially plausible
- economically fragile
- range-bound
- conditioned on
- highly sensitive to
- not yet finance-ready
- further validation has economic value

Blocked language includes:

- guaranteed
- bankable
- closed business case
- confirmed payback
- verified ROI

## 19. Completion criterion

Phase 5 is considered epistemologically closed when the framework can:

- produce bounded financial exposure under sparse evidence;
- distinguish screening economics from stronger economic closure;
- tie every material number to a support state;
- propagate upgrades and downgrades explicitly;
- prevent economic precision from outrunning technical support;
- and preserve a clear difference between bounded finance hardening and pseudo-bankability.
