## Master Working Document

The authoritative constitutional document for the integrated 8-phase architecture is `Phases/phase-0/docs/en/0_Phase_0_Master_Document.md`. If this document conflicts with Phase 0 on numbering, phase authority, allowed claims, support ceilings, or boundary logic, Phase 0 governs until this phase is formally reconstituted.

## 1. Purpose and Role of Phase 1

Phase 1 establishes the public observation layer of the ZLab framework. Its function is to construct the minimum structured external base required to support later constrained inference without granting sovereign epistemic status to any single source, benchmark, dataset, score, or LLM narrative.

Phase 1 is not a diagnostic engine. It is not a verification engine. It is not a high-weight recommendation engine. It is a bounded public-data engine whose role is to organize publicly available constraints, benchmarks, priors, archetypes, regulatory applicability signals, and contextual relations into a traceable substrate capable of producing a first `facility_prior` under Decision-grade limits.

Its expected deliverable is a curated, versioned, joinable, semantically disciplined public-data layer structurally suitable for downstream use.

Its output target is not site truth. Its output target is a disciplined, bounded, and useful prior.

## 2. Dependency on Phase 0

Phase 1 is strictly subordinate to **Phase 0 — Master Document of Operational Truth and Epistemic Governance**. It inherits without reinterpretation:

- the non-negotiable laws of the framework;
- the distinction between Decision-grade and Verification-grade;
- the thesis that the LLM is not sovereign;
- the primacy of traceability;
- semantic proportionality rules;
- non-compensation of structural failures by attractive scores or narratives;
- and the requirement that hallucination reduction is an architectural property, not a stylistic preference.

Phase 1 operates under the following laws:

1. No single model is trusted with truth.
2. The system restricts hypotheses more than it “knows”.
3. The LLM is not sovereign.
4. Hallucination reduction is an architectural property.
5. Phase 1 produces structured context and priors, not verification.
6. What is unknown about the site is not invented; it is represented as plausible hypothesis, comparable benchmark, explicit assumption, or uncertainty marker.
7. Every source enters by analytic function, not by prestige, availability, or volume.
8. Every Phase 1 output belongs, at most, to Decision-grade.

The LLM may participate only as a governed semantic layer for formulation, structuring, translation, summarization, and bounded semantic verification of already constrained material. It may not operate as the primary analytical engine of Phase 1.

## 3. Scope of Phase 1

### 3.1 Objective

Build the external observation layer: public datasets, benchmarks, factors, rules, and relations.

### 3.2 Expected deliverables

- curated public data lake;
- master schema by facility / jurisdiction / sector;
- stable join keys and versioning;
- repository of benchmarks and priors.

### 3.3 Exit decision

The system can create a reasonable `facility_prior` from minimum inputs.

### 3.4 Principal risk

Dataset sprawl: too much data without useful structure.

### 3.5 Advancement criterion

With ten user inputs, the system can generate a first energy and regulatory hypothesis that is structured, traceable, explicitly non-verificatory, and materially useful for downstream narrowing.

### 3.6 What Phase 1 may produce

Phase 1 may produce:

- structured public context;
- candidate archetypes;
- benchmark bundles;
- jurisdiction bundles;
- regulatory flags;
- prior assumptions;
- uncertainty markers;
- plausible systems layers;
- operational tension hypotheses;
- actionability and constraint realism;
- and a bounded `facility_prior`.

### 3.7 What Phase 1 may not produce

Phase 1 may not produce:

- strong diagnosis;
- closed causality;
- verified savings;
- final compliance determination;
- high-weight final recommendations;
- M&V;
- strong local evidence claims;
- or Verification-grade outputs.

## 4. Subphase 1A — Delimitación epistemológica y funcional del Public Data Engine

### 4.1 Function of Subphase 1A

Subphase 1A closes the epistemological and functional perimeter of the Public Data Engine. It defines which types of public observation, benchmark, restriction, rule, archetype, and contextual metadata may enter Phase 1 in order to generate a `facility_prior` that is Decision-grade, traceable, and explicitly non-verificatory.

Subphase 1A does not build the data lake, physical tables, or production ingestion pipelines. It defines the admissible perimeter. It exists to prevent dataset sprawl, semantic overreach, silent migration of Verification-grade language into a Decision-grade layer, and hidden dependence on the LLM as primary analyst.

Its governing criterion is not documentary richness. Its governing criterion is restrictive utility under epistemic governance.

### 4.2 Status of Phase 1 under governance hierarchy

Phase 1 belongs, at most, to Decision-grade. It may produce materially useful contextual structure, but it may not present any output as if it had crossed the threshold of verification, site-specific confirmation, or high-weight recommendation.

Every material Phase 1 output must be:

- structured;
- traceable to source and version;
- semantically proportional to the actual evidence base available;
- bounded in domain and intended use;
- explicitly marked as non-verificatory.

### 4.3 Governing scope rule

Only those public sources, benchmarks, rules, relations, archetypes, and contextual objects may enter Phase 1 that materially constrain hypotheses and improve disciplined construction of the `facility_prior`.

Nothing enters Phase 1 because it is:

- available;
- prestigious;
- voluminous;
- technically impressive in appearance;
- easy to scrape;
- easy for the LLM to read;
- or merely adjacent to the industrial domain.

Admission depends on analytic function, not documentary abundance.

### 4.4 Positive product of Phase 1

Phase 1 may produce only:

- structured public context;
- candidate archetypes;
- benchmark bundles;
- jurisdiction bundles;
- regulatory flags;
- prior assumptions;
- uncertainty markers;
- join-ready contextual objects;
- and a bounded `facility_prior`.

These outputs may discipline later work. They may not close later work.

### 4.5 What Phase 1 cannot produce

Phase 1 may not produce:

- `closed_causal_claim`
- `verified_performance_claim`
- `site_specific_savings_claim`
- `high_weight_recommendation_claim`
- `verification_result_claim`
- or strong diagnosis claims presented as operationally settled.

### 4.6 Claim families permitted in Phase 1

The following claim families are admissible in Phase 1, subject to traceability and proportionality:

- `contextual_public_claim`
- `benchmark_claim`
- `regulatory_applicability_claim`
- `archetype_candidate_claim`
- `prior_assumption_claim`
- `exploratory_structural_claim`

### 4.7 Claim families prohibited in Phase 1

The following claim families are inadmissible in Phase 1:

- `verified_performance_claim`
- `closed_causal_claim`
- `site_specific_savings_claim`
- `high_weight_recommendation_claim`
- `verification_result_claim`

### 4.8 Binary admissibility gates

No source, dataset, benchmark family, rule bundle, or contextual object may enter Phase 1 unless it passes all minimum binary gates below:

- restrictive utility
- canonical mapping
- traceable origin
- Decision-grade proportionality
- non-LLM dependence
- downstream role
- sprawl resistance

### 4.9 Closure statement of 1A

Subphase 1A closes the epistemological and functional perimeter of the Public Data Engine. No later subphase may widen that perimeter silently.

## 5. Subphase 1B — Taxonomía y política de admisión de fuentes públicas

### 5.1 Function of Subphase 1B

Subphase 1B defines which public source families are worth integrating into the MVP, which canonical objects each family feeds, in what priority order each family enters, what epistemic risks it introduces, and where the first implementation effort should search for materially admissible inputs.

Its governing rule is strict:

In Phase 1, sources are not integrated because they are generally good, prestigious, numerous, or technically impressive. They are integrated only if they can feed canonical `facility_prior` objects with real restrictive utility.

### 5.2 Priority source families for the MVP

The core of the Public Data Engine MVP is built on five source families.

#### 5.2.1 Geography and climate

Purpose:

- delimit climate operating context;
- constrain heating and cooling interpretation;
- support climate comparability;
- inform candidate archetypes and first prior assumptions.

Feeds:

- `ClimateContext`
- `climate_energy_context_bundle`
- `candidate_archetype_bundle`
- `facility_prior`

Typical variables:

- climate zone;
- HDD;
- CDD;
- average temperature;
- humidity band;
- climate classification.

First-stop references:

- NOAA / NCEI
- official degree-day publications

#### 5.2.2 Sectoral and typological benchmarks

Purpose:

- provide plausible ranges;
- constrain archetype candidates;
- support benchmark bundles and prior assumptions.

Feeds:

- `BenchmarkContext`
- `SectorArchetype`
- `benchmark_bundle`
- `prior_assumptions_pack`

First-stop references:

- EIA CBECS
- EIA MECS

#### 5.2.3 First-order jurisdiction and regulation

Purpose:

- provide plausible obligations;
- identify thresholds;
- surface benchmarking and reporting relevance;
- create initial regulatory flags.

Feeds:

- `Jurisdiction`
- `RegulatoryContext`
- `jurisdiction_bundle`
- `regulatory_flag_bundle`
- `facility_prior`

First-stop references:

- official city, state, and national regulatory publications

#### 5.2.4 Regional energy context and emission factors

Purpose:

- provide grid mix;
- public emission factors;
- regional energy-price context;
- fuel-availability logic.

Feeds:

- `EnergyContext`
- `climate_energy_context_bundle`
- `benchmark_bundle`
- `facility_prior`

First-stop references:

- EPA eGRID
- EPA GHG Emission Factors Hub
- EIA energy price references

#### 5.2.5 Structural technical libraries

Purpose:

- provide recurrent system families;
- typical end uses;
- typical opportunity families;
- missing-input implications;
- repeatable operational tensions.

Feeds:

- `SystemAsset`
- `OperationalPractice`
- `prior_assumptions_pack`
- `candidate_archetype_bundle`
- `benchmark_bundle`

First-stop references:

- AChEE
- DOE / LBNL
- public guides on steam, motors, refrigeration, lighting, controls, and automation

AChEE is especially relevant because it requires project profile, energy background, energy balance, baseline, USE logic, areas of interest, OMEE, and economic valuation discipline.

### 5.3 Useful but secondary sources

Admissible later, after the MVP core is stable:

- public municipal benchmarking and disclosure datasets;
- better calibrated operational proxies;
- public cases and case studies.

### 5.4 Interesting but not necessary for the MVP

Not required for MVP closure:

- exhaustive utility tariffs;
- detailed carbon-market logic;
- resilience-extreme contexts;
- complex public time-series structures.

### 5.5 Dangerous or noisy sources

High-risk source classes include:

- commercial whitepapers;
- benchmarks without methodological boundary;
- indiscriminate normative scraping;
- sources that require free LLM interpretation to become usable.

### 5.6 Closure statement of 1B

The core of the Public Data Engine MVP is built on five source families: (i) geography and climate, (ii) sectoral and typological benchmarks, (iii) first-order jurisdiction and regulation, (iv) regional energy context and emission factors, and (v) structural technical libraries. Any additional source must explicitly justify its restrictive utility, its mapping to canonical objects, and its marginal value relative to the complexity it introduces.

Phase 1 is not organized by files and not by datasets. It is organized by canonical entities and output bundles.

## 7. Subphase 1D — Contrato formal del `facility_prior` e inputs mínimos

### 7.1 Definition of the `facility_prior`

The `facility_prior` is the central output object of Phase 1.

It is a structured, traceable, versionable, non-verificatory representation of the probable context of a candidate facility, built from public sources, minimum user inputs, archetypes, benchmarks, explicit assumptions, and visible uncertainty markers.

Its function is not to establish site truth. Its function is to restrict the hypothesis space before later phases.

### 7.2 What the `facility_prior` contains

The `facility_prior` contains only what Phase 1 can legitimately sustain under Decision-grade.

#### 7.2.1 `normalized_facility_profile`

Structured translation of the minimum intake:

- facility type;
- sector;
- subsector;
- primary use;
- size or scale proxy;
- approximate vintage;
- jurisdiction;
- climate;
- primary energy;
- approximate schedule.

#### 7.2.2 `candidate_archetypes`

Ordered list of plausible archetypes with:

- applicability conditions;
- uncertainty note;
- benchmark relation.

It must not force a single false archetype.

#### 7.2.3 `climate_energy_context`

Includes:

- HDD / CDD;
- climate band;
- grid and emissions;
- regional energy cost band;
- plausible fuels.

#### 7.2.4 `benchmark_context`

Includes:

- applicable comparable metrics;
- low / mid / high range;
- methodological notes;
- explicit warning that benchmark is not local evidence.

#### 7.2.5 `regulatory_context`

Includes:

- applicable flags;
- thresholds;
- reporting relevance;
- pressure indicators;
- first-order normative obligations.

#### 7.2.6 `system_asset_hypothesis`

Functional hypothesis of common systems present or plausible.

Examples:

- HVAC
- boilers
- furnaces
- refrigeration
- DHW
- lighting
- motors and drives
- compressed air
- ventilation
- BMS / EMS
- CHP
- logistics / material handling

#### 7.2.7 `operational_tension_hypothesis`

Plausible operational tensions, not causal proof.

Examples:

- scheduling mismatch
- poor setpoint governance
- weak maintenance routines
- weak bill reconciliation
- hidden losses in repeated processes
- underuse of BMS / EMS
- no power factor monitoring

#### 7.2.8 `organization_actionability_profile`

Minimum action-capacity profile, not psychology.

Examples:

- outsourced-only maintenance model
- no facility manager
- BMS underused
- finance requires sub-2-year payback
- no formal energy review process
- low in-house technical control

#### 7.2.9 `improvement_constraint_profile`

Early room-to-act representation, not final ranking.

Examples:

- no shutdown window
- production continuity constraint
- capex freeze
- strict payback threshold
- compliance deadline
- tenant comfort constraint
- uptime-critical refrigeration

#### 7.2.10 `prior_assumptions_pack`

Explicit assumptions used to close unavoidable gaps.

This is non-negotiable.

#### 7.2.11 `uncertainty_markers`

Explicit uncertainty markers.

Examples:

- low certainty in facility type classification
- unknown tariff
- unknown real system mix
- limited regulatory applicability confidence
- unknown schedule precision
- low confidence in organization capability inference

### 7.3 What the `facility_prior` does not contain

The `facility_prior` must not contain:

- verified savings;
- measured real consumption;
- root cause analysis;
- final intervention recommendation;
- strong ROI;
- final compliance determination;
- M&V;
- closed causality.

### 7.4 Epistemic state

The `facility_prior` belongs to Decision-grade and must carry:

- `claim_family`
- `knowledge_mode`
- `validation_status`
- `intended_use`
- `critical_limits`
- `dependency_note`
- `uncertainty_level`
- `review_needed_flag`

Its primary family is `exploratory_structural_claim`. It must never be presented as `Verified`.

Its intended use is limited to:

- screening;
- scoping;
- early prioritization;
- next-measurement planning;
- initial regulatory reading.

### 7.5 Minimum user inputs

The minimum contract uses ten user inputs:

1. location
2. facility type
3. sector / subsector
4. primary use
5. approximate size
6. approximate vintage
7. operating schedule
8. primary energy or fuel
9. known main systems
10. main concern

### 7.6 Optional but valuable inputs

Useful but not mandatory:

- utility name;
- known tariff;
- approximate budget;
- payback threshold;
- existence of facility manager;
- existence of BMS / EMS;
- declared recurring problem.

### 7.7 Input-to-entity and bundle mapping

- location feeds `Jurisdiction`, `ClimateContext`, `EnergyContext`, `RegulatoryContext`, `jurisdiction_bundle`, `climate_energy_context_bundle`
- facility type + sector + primary use feed `Facility`, `SectorArchetype`, `BenchmarkContext`, `SystemAsset`, `candidate_archetype_bundle`, `benchmark_bundle`
- size + vintage feed `Facility`, `BenchmarkContext`, `RegulatoryContext`
- schedule feeds `Facility`, `OperationalPractice`, `candidate_archetype_bundle`, `operational_tension_hypothesis`
- primary energy + known systems feed `EnergyContext`, `SystemAsset`, `prior_assumptions_pack`
- main concern feeds `OrganizationCapability`, `ImprovementConstraint`, and reading orientation of the prior

### 7.8 Structured output form

The output is not free text. It is a structured object, logically equivalent to:

```yaml
  prior_id:

  knowledge_mode:
  validation_status:

  regulatory_context: {}

  operational_tension_hypothesis: []

```

### 7.9 Typical design errors

- turning the prior into pseudo-diagnosis;
- demanding too many inputs;
- forcing exactness where only plausibility exists;
- failing to make assumptions explicit;
- failing to distinguish plausible from confirmed systems;
- using benchmarks as local evidence;
- converting user drivers into technical conclusions.

### 7.10 Closure statement of 1D

The `facility_prior` is the central object of Phase 1. It summarizes, in structured, traceable, and non-verificatory form, the probable context of a facility from public sources, minimum user inputs, archetypes, benchmarks, and explicit assumptions. Its function is not to diagnose or verify, but to restrict the hypothesis space and prepare downstream inference.

## 8. Subphase 1E — Reglas anti-sprawl, recorte MVP, gobernanza de curación y criterio de terminado

### 8.1 Purpose of 1E

Subphase 1E closes the final defensive mechanism of Phase 1: what enters now, what enters later, what is deferred, what is blocked, how curation is governed, and what objective test must be passed before closure.

### 8.2 Central risk: dataset sprawl

Dataset sprawl is the central risk of Phase 1.

### 8.3 Concrete forms of sprawl

- source sprawl
- variable sprawl
- semantic sprawl
- functional sprawl
- narrative sprawl

Examples include:

- adding redundant regulatory portals;
- loading variables with no canonical destination;
- creating infinite taxonomies;
- trying to do diagnosis inside Phase 1;
- presenting dashboards that imply false certainty.

### 8.4 Hard anti-sprawl rules

1. No data enters without a canonical destination.
2. No field enters “just in case”.
3. No source enters by prestige.
4. No complexity enters without clear marginal gain.
5. No output depends on infrastructure unnecessary for the MVP.
6. Every assumption must be cheaper than the data it replaces.
7. Do not force resolution when uncertainty is the correct output.

### 8.5 MVP curation governance

In the MVP, core curation is governed by human judgment assisted by AI.

#### Human role

The human:

- defines admission criteria;
- approves or rejects new core sources;
- resolves epistemic ambiguity;
- blocks premature complexity;
- decides the MVP cut;
- authorizes new complexity.

#### AI role

The AI:

- proposes source families;
- classifies sources by priority and risk;
- normalizes names, taxonomies, and attributes;
- summarizes relevant content;
- maps variables to entities and bundles;
- documents provenance and methodological notes;
- detects duplication and sprawl;
- prepares admission matrices.

#### Not delegated to AI

The AI does not:

- define the source core on its own;
- decide alone what complexity enters the MVP;
- declare the system complete;
- replace architectural judgment on phase limits.

### 8.6 Manual curation, AI assistance, and later automation

#### Manual now

- selection of five core source families;
- canonical taxonomies;
- core benchmark approval;
- first-order regulatory rule approval;
- indispensable field decisions.

#### AI-assisted now

- source metadata extraction;
- source classification;
- mapping suggestions;
- duplicate detection;
- methodological boundary notes;
- coverage-gap detection.

#### Automate later

- stable-source refresh;
- version checking;
- dataset change detection;
- schema validation;
- coverage scoring;
- propagation of changes.

### 8.7 MVP trim

#### Indispensable now

- five source families from 1B;
- twelve entities from 1C;
- core output bundles;
- minimum provenance;
- explicit assumptions;
- visible limits;
- uncertainty control;
- no sovereign LLM dependence.

#### Useful later

- finer disclosure datasets;
- more specific utility context;
- better calibrated proxies;
- more granular system subtypes;
- refined internal scoring;
- more granular actionability profiles.

#### Future research

- exhaustive utility tariffs;
- deep international harmonization;
- carbon-market integration;
- extreme resilience;
- fine graph modeling;
- complex standards linking.

#### Hype

- chatbot that “looks at datasets and opines”;
- dashboards as substitute for the prior;
- vector DB as core;
- mass scraping without canonical model;
- infinite taxonomies;
- legalistic compliance engine from day one;
- exhaustive asset registry;
- decorative digital twin.

### 8.8 What “Phase 1 complete” means

Phase 1 is not complete if:

- datasets exist but no useful bundles;
- taxonomies exist but no useful prior;
- the prior requires forty questions;
- it depends on free LLM interpretation;
- assumptions are not explicit;
- provenance is missing;
- uncertainty is not visible.

Phase 1 is complete if with ten minimum inputs the system can produce a structured, traceable, explicitly non-verificatory `facility_prior` with useful archetypes, benchmark context, climate-energy context, regulatory relevance, plausible systems, plausible tensions, basic actionability realism, visible assumptions, and visible uncertainty.

## 9. Subphase 1F — Integración, coherencia final y gate computable de avance a Fase 2

### 9.1 Closure cannot depend on free subjective judgment

Phase 1 may not close because the output sounds convincing, because the document is elegant, or because the architect has a favorable general impression.

Closure must depend on explicit, auditable conditions.

### 9.2 Acceptance Gate Computable

The gate of Phase 1 closure must be as computable as Phase 1 legitimately allows.

This means formalizing:

- mandatory binary gates;
- auxiliary scores;
- non-compensation logic;
- mandatory test cases;
- pass / no-pass / rebuild conditions.

### 9.3 What is computable

The following are computable and must be formalized:

- existence of `facility_prior`;
- presence of mandatory bundles;
- generation using no more than ten minimum inputs;
- provenance completeness;
- assumption visibility;
- uncertainty visibility;
- explicit non-verificatory state;
- non-sovereign LLM dependence;
- presence of candidate archetypes;
- presence of plausible system layer;
- presence of operational or actionability layer;
- pass/fail under mandatory test cases.

Simple examples:

- if `uncertainty_markers` is absent, that is an objective absence;
- if 16 mandatory inputs are required instead of 10, that is countable;
- if the sparse case yields no candidate archetypes, that is measurable.

### 9.4 What must not be faked as fully mathematical

The following must not be falsely reduced to total objectivity in Phase 1:

- whether an operational tension is real;
- whether an organization is “good” or “bad”;
- whether one archetype is uniquely correct under sparse evidence;
- whether a final recommendation is justified;
- whether causality is closed.

These must remain represented as:

- hypothesis;
- candidate set;
- assumption;
- uncertainty marker;
- bounded profile.

### 9.5 Mandatory binary gates

The mandatory gates are:

- `facility_prior_exists`
- `mandatory_bundles_complete`
- `inputs_max_10`
- `traceability_minimum_met`
- `assumptions_explicit`
- `uncertainty_explicit`
- `non_verificatory_state_explicit`
- `llm_not_structural_dependency`
- `candidate_archetypes_nonempty`
- `regulatory_or_contextual_relevance_present`
- `system_layer_present`
- `operational_or_actionability_layer_present`
- `sparse_case_pass`
- `building_case_pass`
- `industrial_case_pass`

Each gate must have explicit pass/fail logic.

### 9.6 Critical gates

The following are critical and non-compensable:

- `facility_prior_exists`
- `mandatory_bundles_complete`
- `inputs_max_10`
- `traceability_minimum_met`
- `assumptions_explicit`
- `uncertainty_explicit`
- `non_verificatory_state_explicit`
- `llm_not_structural_dependency`
- `sparse_case_pass`
- `building_case_pass`
- `industrial_case_pass`

If any critical gate fails, Phase 1 does not close.

### 9.7 Auxiliary computable scores

Auxiliary scores do not replace gates.

Required scores:

- `bundle_completeness_score`
- `assumption_coverage_score`
- `traceability_completeness_score`
- `uncertainty_visibility_score`
- `input_efficiency_score`
- `archetype_resolution_score`
- `unknown_burden_score`
- `actionability_readiness_score`

Illustrative formulas include:

\[
S1 = \frac{\text{mandatory bundles present}}{\text{mandatory bundles required}}
\]

\[
S2 = \frac{\text{components with explicit assumptions}}{\text{components requiring assumptions}}
\]

\[
S3 = \frac{\text{major components with provenance}}{\text{major components total}}
\]

\[
S4 = \frac{\text{uncertain components with visible marker}}{\text{uncertain components total}}
\]

\[
S5 = 1 - \frac{\max(0,\text{inputs used}-10)}{10}
\]

\[
S6 = \max\left(0, 1 - \frac{\max(0,\text{candidate archetypes}-3)}{6}\right)
\]

\[
S7 = 1 - \frac{\text{critical fields marked unknown}}{\text{critical fields total}}
\]

\[
S8 = \frac{\text{layers present among operational, actionability, constraints}}{3}
\]

### 9.8 Non-compensation rule

A high score does not compensate for failure of a critical condition.

A detailed or attractive prior does not close Phase 1 if it lacks:

- minimum traceability;
- explicit assumptions;
- visible uncertainty;
- explicit non-verificatory limits;
- or robustness under mandatory test cases.

### 9.9 Mandatory test cases

#### Building / compliance case

Input example:

- NYC
- office
- 120,000 sq ft
- 1985
- commercial hours
- electricity + gas
- HVAC, lighting, BMS unknown
- main concern: compliance

Expected output:

- plausible candidate archetypes;
- useful benchmark context;
- plausible regulatory flags;
- common systems layer;
- plausible operational tensions;
- visible uncertainty.

#### Industrial / reliability case

Input example:

- industrial zone in Colombia
- food processing
- 24/7
- electricity + refrigeration + motors
- 2000s
- refrigeration, motors, boilers unknown
- main concern: cost + reliability

Expected output:

- process + refrigeration archetypes;
- reasonable benchmark context;
- plausible systems layer;
- maintenance / control / continuity tensions;
- uptime and process constraints;
- no false diagnosis.

#### Sparse-data case

Input example:

- city
- facility type
- size
- primary use
- main concern
- many unknowns

Expected output:

- still-useful prior;
- lower resolution;
- more explicit assumptions;
- more visible uncertainty;
- preserved structure.

### 9.10 Pass, no-pass, and rebuild rule

#### Minimum pass

All critical gates = `1`.

#### Sufficient quality to advance

In addition, minimum score thresholds apply:

- \(S1 \ge 0.90\)
- \(S2 \ge 0.80\)
- \(S3 \ge 0.90\)
- \(S4 \ge 0.80\)
- \(S5 = 1.00\)
- \(S6 \ge 0.50\)
- \(S7 \ge 0.60\)
- \(S8 \ge 0.67\)

#### `Rebuild Required`

Applies when:

- some critical gates pass;
- but material score thresholds fail;
- or mandatory test cases fail;
- or the prior remains too unresolved to close.

### 9.11 False closure examples

The following are false closures:

- “the document is already written”;
- “we already have many sources”;
- “we already have dashboards”;
- “we already have convincing text”;
- “we already have a prior, but it needs 18 questions and fails the sparse case”.

### 9.12 What Phase 1 hands to Phase 2

Phase 1 hands off only:

- `facility_prior`
- structured bundles
- candidate archetypes
- assumptions pack
- uncertainty markers
- source dependencies
- plausible systems layer
- operational tension hypotheses
- basic actionability and constraint realism

It does not hand off:

- strong local evidence;
- verification;
- final diagnosis;
- closed causality;
- final recommendation.

### 9.13 Closure statements of 1F

The closure of Phase 1 does not depend on free subjective global judgment. It depends on a computable acceptance gate composed of mandatory binary conditions and non-compensatory auxiliary metrics.

Not everything in the system can be reduced to one formula. But the Phase 1 acceptance gate can become sufficiently computable to limit bias, make pass criteria explicit, and prevent closure by impression.

Phase 1 is formally closed when the Public Data Engine can transform admitted public sources and minimum inputs into a structured, traceable, non-verificatory, and operationally useful `facility_prior` under explicit computable criteria, without epistemic contradiction, without over-engineering, and without sovereign analytic dependence on the LLM.

## 12. Controlled Vocabulary for Phase 1

**`facility_prior`**  
Structured, traceable, non-verificatory prior over a candidate facility.

**`candidate_archetype`**  
Plausible comparable archetype used to constrain interpretation without claiming unique correctness.

**`system_asset_hypothesis`**  
Structured representation of common systems that are known, reported, plausible, not applicable, or unknown.

**`operational_tension_hypothesis`**  
Structured set of plausible repeatable operational tensions, not causal proof.

**`organization_actionability_profile`**  
Minimum representation of organizational capacity to act, not psychology or culture diagnosis.

**`improvement_constraint_profile`**  
Early representation of room to act and practical constraints, not final prioritization.

**`public source family`**  
Governed class of external public information admitted for a bounded analytic function.

**`canonical entity`**  
Primary schema object through which public data is normalized and governed.

**`bundle`**  
Structured output grouping designed for downstream use, not free-form narrative.

**`admitted source`**  
Source that has passed Phase 1 admissibility gates and has a canonical destination.

**`critical gate`**  
Binary acceptance condition whose failure blocks closure and cannot be compensated by scores.

**`rebuild required`**  
State in which some structure exists but Phase 1 is not fit to close and must be corrected before advancement.

## 13. Final Closing Rule

Phase 1 is formally closed only when the Public Data Engine can transform admitted public sources and a minimal intake into a structured, traceable, explicitly non-verificatory `facility_prior` that passes all critical acceptance gates and provides a stable foundation for Phase 2 without over-engineering, false precision, or sovereign LLM dependence.
