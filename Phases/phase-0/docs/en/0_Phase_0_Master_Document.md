# 0_Phase_0_Master_Document
## Constitutional Epistemology of the Complete 8-Phase Framework

## 1. Executive framing

This document is the canonical epistemic constitution of the integrated 8-phase ZLab / Zircular framework.

Its function is not to produce analysis, software, reporting, or verification by itself. Its function is to govern what every later phase may legitimately do, say, consume, emit, upgrade, degrade, publish, and withhold.

The non-negotiable governing law of the framework is fixed here:

**The framework must deliver decision-grade intelligence with minimal initial data, and progressively converge toward verification-grade certainty as evidence accumulates.**

This law implies six architectural consequences:

1. The framework must remain useful under sparse evidence.
2. The framework must not fake certainty to compensate for sparse evidence.
3. All phases may be present from the first report, but not with equal semantic force.
4. No phase may become sovereign by merely being technically sophisticated.
5. Verification is not a style of writing; it is a support condition.
6. Evidence accumulation must strengthen the framework without forcing a redesign of its architecture.

The framework exists to prevent high-cost industrial, physical, and financial decisions from being made on top of shallow readings of reality. It is designed for complex physical assets and operating systems in which incomplete data, delayed instrumentation, fragmented documentation, inconsistent operations, and financial pressure routinely create false clarity.

The framework therefore does not optimize for answer speed. It optimizes for admissibility under uncertainty.

## 2. Constitutional definition of the 8-phase framework

### 2.1 Compact definition

The ZLab / Zircular 8-phase framework is a governed multi-phase decision-intelligence architecture for complex physical assets. It transforms sparse, heterogeneous, and progressively hardening evidence into traceable, bounded, upgradeable intelligence for scoping, prioritization, validation, compliance, financial judgment, and ultimately decision admissibility.

### 2.2 What problem it solves

The framework solves a recurring industrial failure mode:

- decisions are made too early,
- the physical system is poorly understood,
- benchmarks are overread as local truth,
- financial cases are built on fragile assumptions,
- regulatory pressure is misclassified,
- AI-generated narratives create false closure,
- and expensive diligence or CAPEX effort is directed at the wrong problem.

The framework does not promise omniscience. It promises disciplined convergence.

### 2.3 What it means that this is a framework and not just software

This is a framework because it defines:

- admissible problem decomposition,
- epistemic authority by phase,
- claim boundaries,
- upgrade and downgrade rules,
- evidence thresholds,
- reporting ceilings,
- and traceability obligations.

Software may implement the framework. Software does not replace the framework's constitutional logic.

### 2.4 What it means that the framework is multicapa

The framework is multicapa because no single layer can legitimately absorb all analytical responsibility.

Each phase contributes a different kind of disciplined reading:

- observation design,
- physical plausibility,
- system-state structure,
- operational behavior,
- verification architecture,
- financial implication under uncertainty,
- computable normativity,
- belief-state governance,
- and decision admissibility.

The phases are cumulative but non-substitutable.

### 2.5 What it means that the framework is epistemologically governed

The framework is epistemologically governed when:

- a claim's force is limited by support, not by rhetorical preference,
- the same evidence cannot be promoted differently in different layers without traceable cause,
- uncertainty remains visible,
- conflict remains visible,
- downgrade is treated as a valid output,
- and the framework refuses to publish stronger language than the evidence allows.

### 2.6 What it means that a phase contributes without becoming sovereign

A phase contributes without becoming sovereign when:

- it performs its own legitimate operation,
- it remains within its authority boundary,
- it cannot redefine the truth criterion of the framework,
- it cannot erase upstream uncertainty,
- it cannot manufacture local truth out of proxy evidence,
- and it cannot close the case by technical prestige alone.

### 2.7 Operational truth

**Operational truth** in this framework is not metaphysical certainty and not a free-form narrative consensus.

Operational truth is:

- boundary-delimited,
- evidence-proportional,
- use-specific,
- traceable to source and transformation lineage,
- phase-governed,
- and reconstructible by an auditor.

An operational truth statement is valid only inside:

- a defined claim boundary,
- a defined evidence basis,
- a defined semantic ceiling,
- a defined intended use,
- and a defined degradation policy.

### 2.8 Decision-grade intelligence

**Decision-grade intelligence** means:

- sufficient to prioritize, screen, triage, compare, direct diligence, allocate attention, sequence validation, reject weak directions, or justify further investigation,
- without pretending that the relevant claim is already verified, certified, or fully hardened.

Decision-grade does not mean "probably true." It means "admissible enough to act on the agenda."

### 2.9 Verification-grade certainty

**Verification-grade certainty** means:

- the claim has been hardened by sufficient local evidence,
- within an explicit domain of validity,
- with preserved boundary and restrictions,
- such that the claim can support stronger downstream uses such as financing defense, validated performance statements, M&V conclusions, formal compliance closure, or high-weight implementation decisions.

Verification-grade still does not mean omniscience. It means bounded, materially supported closure.

### 2.10 Preliminary but admissible intelligence

**Preliminary but admissible intelligence** is intelligence that:

- is explicitly non-terminal,
- preserves assumptions and uncertainty,
- uses bounded language,
- identifies what still blocks stronger closure,
- and remains useful for action sequencing without overstating truth.

It is the legitimate mode of the framework at low evidence.

### 2.11 Upgrade path

An **upgrade path** is the governed mechanism by which:

- new evidence enters,
- affected claims are re-evaluated,
- semantic ceilings are recomputed,
- ranges tighten or widen,
- priorities move,
- blocked fronts may reopen,
- and stronger outputs become admissible without silent reinterpretation.

Upgrade is never granted by narrative polish. It is granted by support gain under preserved governance.

### 2.12 Variable evidence maturity law

The framework must also obey the following non-negotiable law:

**No variable may enable a claim, calculation, compliance posture, ROI surface, decision front, or recommendation stronger than the evidence maturity that supports that variable.**

This law applies at the level of variables, not only at the level of reports, sections, or phases.

Every variable used by the framework must therefore carry, at minimum:

- a maturity level;
- a source basis;
- a scope classification;
- an authority state;
- a recency state;
- an uncertainty reason when not hardened;
- allowed outputs;
- prohibited outputs;
- an upgrade condition;
- a downgrade condition;
- and the decisions or claims it may unlock.

If a variable has no assigned maturity, it may not support a high-weight claim.

#### 2.12.1 Common maturity ladder

| Level | Meaning | Maximum semantic force |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `L0` | not observed / not admissible | missing-field handling only |
| `L1` | benchmark / proxy / archetype | screening, plausibility, bounded scenario framing |
| `L2` | asset-specific but unverified | preliminary scenario bands, directional ranges, targeted validation |
| `L3` | local measured or documented | stronger decision-grade ranges, preliminary compliance posture, bounded exposure modelling |
| `L4` | verified / hardened | strongest bounded claims allowed by the domain of validity |

#### 2.12.2 Variable semantic ceiling

Each variable has a semantic ceiling.

This means:

- a benchmark-only variable may support screening but not local closure;
- an asset-specific brochure or intake variable may support preliminary scenario construction but not verification-grade language;
- a locally measured variable may support materially stronger financial and operational outputs;
- and a verified variable may support the strongest bounded external surface that remains inside its domain of validity.

#### 2.12.3 Derived-variable dependency rule

Derived variables may never outrun the weakest dependency required to compute them.

Examples:

- `ROI` may not outrun the weaker of benefit maturity, cost maturity, and control-boundary maturity;
- `compliance posture` may not outrun trigger-field maturity and jurisdiction-confirmation maturity;
- `savings` may not outrun baseline maturity, operating-boundary maturity, and intervention-definition maturity.

#### 2.12.4 Source scope dependency rule

Variable maturity is invalid if it is upgraded by evidence from the wrong scope.

The minimum rule is:

- `ASSET_LEVEL` evidence may populate asset-critical variables;
- `ENTITY_LEVEL`, `PORTFOLIO_LEVEL`, `JURISDICTION_LEVEL`, and `BENCHMARK_LEVEL` evidence may contextualize;
- they may not fill missing `ASSET_LEVEL` fields by substitution.

#### 2.12.5 Prohibited shortcuts

The framework must explicitly prohibit:

- using `0`, blank, `null`, or `unspecified` as if they were real field values;
- converting a benchmark into local truth by narrative style;
- converting a proxy into closed ROI;
- converting a plausible trigger into compliance closure;
- letting technical scraping proceed before the target has been classified and routed;
- letting weak web search substitute for a canonical public dataset that is available for the same jurisdiction and asset type.

#### 2.12.6 Public data routing law

The framework must also obey the following routing law:

**No technical public-data discovery may begin until the system has produced a target classification, a jurisdiction resolution, an asset-type route, and a source routing plan.**

This law exists to prevent four recurring failure modes:

1. scraping before the framework knows what object it is evaluating;
2. scraping before the framework knows which jurisdiction-specific structured datasets are canonical;
3. over-collecting weak context before attempting strong local public records;
4. silently substituting entity, portfolio, benchmark, or wrong-jurisdiction evidence for missing asset-level truth.

The routing layer must therefore decide, before discovery execution:

- what the target is;
- where it is;
- what asset family it belongs to;
- what the mandatory public sources are for that route;
- what optional sources may be consulted later;
- what substitutions are explicitly forbidden;
- and what report-type ceiling applies if critical routed evidence is missing.

#### 2.12.7 Canonical structured source priority

When a canonical structured public source exists for the resolved route, it must be attempted before weak or unstructured search.

Examples:

- NYC `LL84` must be attempted before `CBECS` or generic web search for building EUI;
- NYC `PLUTO` or assessor records must be attempted before weak brochure language for building size;
- state or city benchmarking datasets must be attempted before sector-average energy claims;
- environmental registry records must be attempted before blog, news, or SEO summaries.

This priority rule is constitutional, not merely an optimization preference.

#### 2.12.8 Source substitution dependency rule

Every route must declare `disallowed_substitutions`.

If a source is marked as a disallowed substitution for a field or route, downstream phases may not use it to fill the missing field indirectly.

Minimum constitutional prohibitions:

- `ENTITY_LEVEL` evidence may not fill `ASSET_LEVEL` fields;
- `PORTFOLIO_LEVEL` evidence may not fill `ASSET_LEVEL` fields;
- benchmarks may not replace local measured or disclosed values when a canonical public local dataset exists;
- wrong-city, wrong-state, or wrong-rule records may not be laundered into admissible context by prose.
- or converting incomplete variable maturity into decision-grade language.

### 3.2 Phase 0 — Observation design, conceptual datasets, epistemic governance

**Purpose**  
Define the observational constitution of the framework.

**Epistemic function**  
Constitute admissibility, support grammar, phase authority, and truth ceilings.

**Problems it solves**

- free-form use of AI,
- mixed evidentiary semantics,
- silent scope expansion,
- and publication beyond support.

**Authorized inputs**

- phase contracts,
- source classes,
- ontology candidates,
- support taxonomies,
- reporting rules,
- audit criteria.

**Authorized outputs**

- claim grammar,
- evidence classes,
- phase boundaries,
- upgrade rules,
- downgrade rules,
- publication restrictions,
- and traceability obligations.

**Prohibited outputs**

- site-level findings,
- local diagnosis,
- compliance closure,
- financial closure,
- or decision closure.

**Permitted claims**

- constitutional claims,
- admissibility claims,
- semantic ceiling claims,
- evidence-governance claims.

**Prohibited claims**

- empirical or local claims about a case.

**Contribution to Decision-grade**  
It makes Decision-grade possible by defining what counts as admissible early intelligence.

**Contribution to Verification-grade**  
It makes Verification-grade legitimate by preventing fake upgrade.

**Hardening and degradation**

- hardens when contracts become explicit, auditable, and stable;
- degrades only if governance ambiguity reappears or silent exceptions are introduced.

**Traceability requirement**  
Every rule, exception, and override must be versioned and rebuildable.

**Required operational objects**  
Phase 0 must define, or authorize the definition of, the following cross-phase objects:

- `variable_maturity_register`
- `claim_permission_register`
- `decision_permission_register`
- `report_readiness_register`

No downstream phase may treat maturity or claim permission as a stylistic layer.

### 3.3 Phase 1 — Physics + PIML

**Purpose**  
Produce physically disciplined initial explanatory structure.

**Epistemic function**  
Constrain plausible mechanisms before strong local evidence exists.

**Problems it solves**

- implausible narratives,
- benchmark-only reasoning,
- and non-physical hypothesis inflation.

**Authorized inputs**

- public data,
- climate,
- geometry proxies,
- sector archetypes,
- engineering priors,
- physical laws,
- literature,
- minimal local observations.

**Authorized outputs**

- physically bounded hypotheses,
- candidate energy and process mechanisms,
- candidate parameter ranges,
- PIML-compatible model families,
- non-calibrated but structured priors.

**Prohibited outputs**

- calibrated truth claims,
- final causal closure,
- verified performance attribution,
- deterministic savings estimates.

**Permitted claims**

- physical plausibility claims,
- physically incompatible / compatible statements,
- boundary-conditioned mechanism hypotheses,
- parameter sensitivity bounds.

**Prohibited claims**

- "the cause is X",
- "the system definitely behaves as Y",
- "this model is verified."

**Low-data mode**  
Outputs remain prior-heavy and explicitly conditioned.

**Local-evidence mode**  
Parameter ranges tighten, implausible mechanisms die, and model-family selection improves.

**Measurement-hardened mode**  
Some physical claims may become bounded decision-grade or feed verified pathways through Phase 4.

**Contribution to Decision-grade**  
Blocks physically incoherent agendas and narrows where to look first.

**Contribution to Verification-grade**  
Provides model structure that later measurement can test, calibrate, and reject.

**Hardening and degradation**

- hardens with bills, trends, parameters, measured states, calibration;
- degrades when calibration fails, boundary assumptions break, or contradictory local evidence appears.

**Traceability requirement**  
Every physical prior must state source basis, boundary assumptions, and kill conditions.

### 3.4 Phase 2 — Systemic Digital Twin

**Purpose**  
Build a structured twin of the asset as an interdependent system-of-systems.

**Epistemic function**  
Represent topology, dependency, state coupling, and subsystem interaction.

**Problems it solves**

- fragmented subsystem reasoning,
- inability to see cross-effects,
- and failure to model interactions among energy, process, control, reliability, and constraints.

**Authorized inputs**

- Phase 1 physical priors,
- drawings,
- equipment data,
- topology docs,
- BMS/SCADA data,
- asset inventories,
- maintenance data,
- field observations.

**Authorized outputs**

- twin state hypotheses,
- dependency graph,
- subsystem maps,
- state transitions,
- coarse-to-fine system representation,
- observability gaps.

**Prohibited outputs**

- claims that a twin is high-fidelity because it is structurally rich,
- real-time truth claims without instrumentation,
- verified operational state closure.

**Permitted claims**

- structural dependency claims,
- candidate state claims,
- subsystem interaction claims,
- observability-gap claims.

**Prohibited claims**

- "the twin proves",
- "the twin confirms",
- "the plant behaves exactly like this."

**Low-data mode**  
Twin is coarse, sparse, and partly hypothetical.

**Local-evidence mode**  
Twin gains asset confirmation, actual topology, and better state observability.

**Measurement-hardened mode**  
Twin becomes fit for stronger operational and verification use, but only within instrumented boundaries.

**Contribution to Decision-grade**  
Improves where to inspect, measure, and prioritize across subsystem interactions.

**Contribution to Verification-grade**  
Provides the structural substrate against which validation and measurement routes become meaningful.

**Hardening and degradation**

- hardens with asset confirmation, topology confirmation, reliable trends;
- degrades when dependencies are disproven, missing subsystems matter more than assumed, or observability is lower than represented.

**Traceability requirement**  
Every twin relation must point to support class: confirmed, inferred, proxy-based, or unresolved.

**Purpose**  
Model how the asset actually operates across regimes, schedules, sequences, utilization, and operational constraints.

**Epistemic function**  
Translate structural and physical possibility into operationally relevant states.

**Problems it solves**

- static reading of dynamic systems,
- false assumption of nominal operation,
- inability to distinguish mode-dependent claims,
- and action plans detached from real operating windows.

**Authorized inputs**

- Phase 1 and 2 outputs,
- schedules,
- historian data,
- shift patterns,
- production context,
- interviews,
- field observations,
- overrides,
- alarms,
- control sequences.

**Authorized outputs**

- regime map,
- mode hypotheses,
- control logic hypotheses,
- schedule reconstruction,
- load-state constraints,
- actionability conditions,
- operational tensions.

**Prohibited outputs**

- final root-cause diagnosis,
- verified baseline,
- definitive operational truth without validation,
- or intervention command.

**Permitted claims**

- "operation appears compatible with",
- "mode interaction suggests",
- "this regime remains unresolved",
- "this operating pattern blocks simple action."

**Prohibited claims**

- "operations are definitively causing X",
- "the plant runs in Y mode at all relevant times."

**Low-data mode**  
Produces operating hypotheses and constraints, not confirmed sequences.

**Local-evidence mode**  
Can reconstruct meaningful regimes, detect contradictions, and narrow validation.

**Measurement-hardened mode**  
Feeds hardened baseline and causal discrimination into Phase 4.

**Contribution to Decision-grade**  
Separates technically plausible from operationally admissible.

**Contribution to Verification-grade**  
Provides the regime logic that valid measurement and baseline design require.

**Hardening and degradation**

- hardens with logs, trends, historian access, sequence confirmation, field observation;
- degrades when schedules prove more variable, sequence access is absent, or operating windows invalidate prior assumptions.

**Traceability requirement**  
Every operating claim must state time regime, subsystem scope, and evidence basis.

**Authorized outputs**

- measurement boundary,

**Prohibited outputs**

- IPMVP theater without baseline discipline,
- verified savings claims without executed support,

- weak measurement presented as conclusive M&V.

**Permitted claims**

**Prohibited claims**

- risk tolerance,
- timing,
- irreversibility,
- operational constraints,
- financial exposure,
- regulatory urgency.

**Authorized outputs**

- screening priority,
- validation priority,
- intervention sequencing,
- action-family-specific admissibility,
- decision burden logic,
- no-go flags,
- defer / investigate / act logic,
- final admissible decision statements when support truly allows.

**Prohibited outputs**

- sovereign recommendations under weak evidence,
- fake optimization certainty,
- hidden value judgments not stated as criteria.

**Permitted claims**

- provisional priority claims,
- conditional decision claims,
- defer / no-go / investigate-first claims,
- bounded final decision claims when phase support is sufficiently hardened.

**Prohibited claims**

- "best decision" without support,
- implementation command based on elegance rather than admissibility.

**Contribution to Decision-grade**  
Primary outward layer for early action ordering.

**Contribution to Verification-grade**  
Final decision closure depends on hardened support from upstream phases, not on TAD sophistication.

**Hardening and degradation**

- hardens when upstream phases harden and trade-offs become bounded;
- degrades when high-impact assumptions collapse, risk widens, or regulatory/financial conditions change materially.

**Traceability requirement**  
Every priority or decision must expose action family, criteria, evidence basis, unresolved blockers, downside / irreversibility basis, and downgrade triggers.

## 4. Epistemic status architecture

### 4.1 Why a single status ladder is insufficient

The framework must distinguish:

- whether a phase is present in the report,
- whether it is materially contributing,
- whether its outputs are decision-usable,
- and whether any part of it is verified.

Therefore the framework uses three orthogonal status axes.

### 4.2 Axis A — Phase presence state

`absent`  
The phase is not represented in the case.

`scaffolded`  
The phase exists structurally but has no material case-specific contribution yet.

`present_preliminary`  
The phase emits bounded, early, explicitly non-terminal reading.

`present_decision_supporting`  
The phase materially influences prioritization, screening, or resource allocation.

`present_hardened`  
The phase contains materially strengthened outputs, though not necessarily verified.

`present_verified_component`  
At least one bounded component of the phase contains verified or verification-supported outputs.

### 4.3 Axis B — Claim support state

`unsupported`  
No admissible support; may not circulate as a claim.

`hypothesis`  
Structurally admissible but unverified explanatory or screening claim.

`indication`  
Weak but meaningful evidence suggests the claim deserves attention; still conditional.

`screening_grade`  
Admissible for early filtering or triage only.

`decision_grade`  
Strong enough to support prioritization, no-go, scope definition, or diligence direction.

`partially_hardened`  
Support has increased materially, but closure remains bounded or incomplete.

`verification_ready`  
All major design conditions for verification are defined; execution or final local support still pending.

`verification_supported`  
Claim has strong local support and may carry stronger language within domain of validity.

`verified`  
Claim has completed the required hardening path and supports the strongest allowed bounded use.

### 4.4 Axis C — Publication state

`block`  
May not circulate.

`hold_for_validation`  
May remain active internally but not circulate as a mature outward finding.

`publish_with_degradation`  
May circulate only with weaker language or narrower scope.

`publish_bounded`  
May circulate as written inside explicit restrictions.

`publish_strong_bounded`  
May circulate with stronger language because support allows it, still within explicit domain of validity.

### 4.5 Rule separating presence from closure

A phase may be:

- present but only scaffolded,
- present and preliminary,
- present and decision-supporting,
- present and partially hardened,
- present and still not closed,
- or present with one verified component while the rest remains preliminary.

Presence never implies closure.

### 4.6 Layer closure states

For implementation and audit, each phase also carries a closure state:

`open`  
The phase still depends on unresolved structural inputs.

`provisionally_closed_for_current_use`  
Enough for current report purpose, not enough for stronger downstream use.

`hardened_closed_for_current_boundary`  
Enough to support stronger bounded use within a defined scope.

`verified_closed_for_current_boundary`  
Enough to support bounded verified use for the claim scope actually hardened.

### 4.7 Canonical status tuple

Every material phase-level output should be representable as:

```yaml
phase_output_status:
  phase_presence_state:
  claim_support_state:
  publication_state:
  closure_state:
  intended_use:
  domain_of_validity:
  upgrade_requirements: []
  downgrade_triggers: []
```

### 4.8 Why phases 5–8 can be present from the first report

Because presence means:

- the phase contributes bounded logic,
- not that it owns verification or final closure.

Phases 5–8 may therefore appear early as:

- probabilistic ranges,
- regulatory screening,
- belief-state governance,
- and conditional prioritization,

without pretending to be fully hardened.

## 5. How phases 5–8 enter from the first report

### 5.1 General rule

Phases 5–8 are decision-conditioning phases. They should be present from the first report because the user needs:

- economic framing,
- regulatory exposure framing,
- explicit belief management,
- and a priority structure

from the start.

What they must not do is overclaim closure that upstream evidence does not support.

### 5.2 Phase 5 — Probabilistic finance and risk

**What it may say from the first report**

- probable financial range families,
- downside and upside structure,
- sensitivity to unknown baseline,
- cost of waiting,
- cost of measuring,
- fragility of the current economic narrative,
- whether a case is too weak to finance confidently,
- and whether the case should remain on hold for financial overstatement risk.

**What it may not say from the first report**

- verified savings,
- deterministic ROI,
- bankable NPV,
- or finance-ready closure.

**How it should operate early**

- use ranges rather than points,
- express dependence on unresolved technical states,
- make value-of-information visible,
- separate scenario logic from performance closure,
- and preserve tariff / cost / baseline provenance explicitly.

**What makes it preliminary**

- baseline uncertainty,
- capex uncertainty,
- tariff uncertainty,
- operational uncertainty,
- unresolved regulatory cost exposure.

**What hardens it**

- full bills,
- tariff confirmation,
- local cost estimates,
- baseline hardening,
- verified performance ranges,
- implementation constraints,
- financing term confirmation.

### 5.3 Phase 6 — Computable normativity and compliance

**What it may map early**

- regulatory families,
- plausible applicability,
- likely trigger fields,
- evidence requests,
- timing windows,
- exposure categories,
- and possible compliance-relevant constraints.

**What early screening means**

`screening_regulatory_read` means:

- the rule family is plausibly relevant,
- trigger logic is partially known,
- threshold or exception logic may still be open,
- and the case should preserve this front explicitly.

**What trigger plausible means**

`trigger_plausible` means:

- known facts and proxy attributes make a rule relevant enough to track,
- but trigger confirmation is not yet closed.

`bounded_compliance_posture` means:

- the rule front is materially bounded,
- the governing rule and jurisdiction are preserved,
- and compliance language is still limited to the hardened boundary actually supported.

**What it may not do early**

- certify compliance,
- deny applicability definitively,
- or state violation conclusively.

**What hardens it**

- confirmed asset classification,
- confirmed size or occupancy,
- confirmed equipment or process type,
- local measurements,
- filing records,
- site evidence tied to the rule boundary.

### 5.4 Phase 7 — Cognitive layer / belief update

**Why it must exist from the first report**

Because the framework must already know how to:

- preserve contradiction,
- move claims up or down,
- register what evidence would matter,
- and stop silent reinterpretation.

**What it does early**

- records contradiction,
- logs uncertainty,
- sets initial support state,
- defines upgrade and downgrade conditions,
- defines trigger and dependency paths,
- assigns publication consequences,
- and preserves revision lineage.

**How it avoids arbitrariness**

- every belief-state change requires an evidence event or formal rule trigger,
- contradictory fronts remain visible,
- hold / block / downgrade are first-class outputs,
- publication consequences are explicit,
- and no confidence inflation is allowed by tone alone.

### 5.5 Phase 8 — TAD

**Why it must exist from the first report**

Because the user still needs ordering:

- what to investigate first,
- what to measure first,
- what not to fund yet,
- what is not decision-admissible,
- and where the highest value-of-information lies.

**What early TAD means**

Early TAD is not final recommendation. It is:

- uncertainty-aware priority logic,
- sequencing under incomplete evidence,
- admissibility filtering,
- and burden-aware action-family ordering.

**What it may say early**

- do not commit CAPEX yet,
- this front deserves validation first,
- this opportunity is too fragile,
- this regulatory front changes urgency,
- this measurement has the highest information value,
- this action is admissible only as a bounded candidate action.

**What hardens it**

- hardened upstream evidence,
- narrowed risk ranges,
- confirmed regulatory exposure,
- verified performance pathways,
- stable business constraints,
- and a burden profile compatible with downside and irreversibility.

## 6. Evidence vs assertion matrix

### 6.1 Canonical rule

The maximum force of assertion is a function of evidence class, not of analytical sophistication.

### 6.2 Matrix

| Evidence configuration | Maximum support state | Allowed language | Quantification ceiling | Recommendation ceiling | Publication ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Geography + climate + sector archetype + literature only | `hypothesis` or `screening_grade` | compatible with, plausible, indicative of, cannot rule out | broad ranges only | investigate, screen, do not close | publish only as preliminary |
| Above + minimum user inputs | `screening_grade` to weak `decision_grade` | likely relevant, deserves priority, remains conditional | bounded scenario ranges | prioritize diligence, no-go on obviously weak directions | publish bounded with visible assumptions |
| Above + public regulation/tariffs + facility descriptors | `decision_grade` for screening and exposure logic | plausible applicability, financial exposure may be material | scenario bands, trigger-conditioned ranges | prioritize validation, defer commitment | publish bounded |
| Above + partial utility bills or partial local operating data | `decision_grade` and selected `indication` | suggests, narrows, materially affects | narrower ranges, still conditional | validate specific fronts, shape scope | publish bounded |
| Above + historian / BMS trends without full baseline hardening | `decision_grade` to `partially_hardened` for some claims | operating evidence suggests, conditionally stronger | constrained bands, regime-specific values | targeted validation or intervention candidate only | publish bounded, often with degradation |
| Above + point measurements / field observation / equipment confirmation | `partially_hardened` | locally observed, conditionally supported | subsystem-specific quantified claims | bounded action candidate with explicit blockers | publish bounded or strong bounded |
| Above + baseline hardened + contrast logic accepted | `verification_ready` to `verification_supported` | supported within defined boundary, baseline-backed | tighter values with explicit domain of validity | bounded implementation decision, finance strengthening | publish strong bounded |
| Above + calibrated simulation validated against local evidence | `verification_supported` | model-supported and locally constrained | stronger scenario and counterfactual use | stronger prioritization and financial closure within boundary | publish strong bounded |
| Above + executed M&V / IPMVP-compliant support | `verified` | verified within domain of validity | strongest bounded quantification | final admissible implementation / finance / compliance use where relevant | publish strong bounded |

### 6.3 Prohibited jumps

The following jumps are forbidden:

- benchmark -> verified savings
- proxy -> compliance closure
- coarse twin -> final operating truth
- preliminary finance -> bankable case
- priority ranking -> final decision closure
- good narrative -> evidence promotion

### 6.4 Score policy

Permitted numerical treatment under low evidence:

- scenario ranges,
- sensitivity ranking,
- risk bands,
- prioritization weights,
- information value estimates.

Prohibited low-evidence numerical treatment:

- fake certainty percentages,
- deterministic ROI,
- verification scores,
- compliance certainty scores,
- truth scores.

## 7. Upgrade / downgrade policy

### 7.1 General upgrade rule

A claim, range, priority, or phase component may rise only when:

- new admissible evidence enters,
- the new evidence materially changes support,
- boundary remains explicit,
- critical restrictions remain preserved,
- and the stronger language is recomputed rather than assumed.

### 7.2 Evidence event classes

The framework recognizes, at minimum, these evidence events:

- new public dataset,
- utility bill,
- tariff confirmation,
- historian export,
- BMS / SCADA trend set,
- sensor deployment,
- spot measurement,
- field inspection,
- asset confirmation,
- regulatory filing or trigger record,
- model calibration success,
- model calibration failure,
- executed M&V result,
- contradiction event.

### 7.3 Cross-phase propagation rules

**When a bill enters**

- Phase 1 may tighten energy priors.
- Phase 3 may revise operating pattern plausibility.
- Phase 4 may strengthen baseline definition.
- Phase 5 may tighten financial ranges.
- Phase 7 must recalculate support states.
- Phase 8 may reorder priorities.

**When historian enters**

- Phase 2 twin states may harden.
- Phase 3 regime hypotheses may harden or fail.
- Phase 4 pathway design may become executable.
- Phase 5 uncertainty may narrow.
- Phase 7 must process upgrades or contradictions.

**When a sensor enters**

- Phase 4 gains stronger measurement route.
- Phase 1-3 claims may upgrade or die.
- Phase 5 may tighten downside/upside.
- Phase 8 may shift from investigate-first to act-first.

**When inspection enters**

- Phase 2 topology may confirm or refute.
- Phase 3 operating and control claims may tighten.
- Phase 6 trigger applicability may confirm or collapse.
- Phase 7 must register direct evidence impact.

**When a hypothesis is refuted**

- Phase 7 creates downgrade or block.
- Dependent financial and TAD outputs must be recomputed.
- Regulatory or operational priorities tied to that hypothesis lose force.
- Publication state may move to `block` or `publish_with_degradation`.

**When simulation is badly calibrated**

- Phase 1 physical claims weaken.
- Phase 2 twin credibility narrows.
- Phase 5 financial scenarios tied to the model widen or downgrade.
- Phase 8 must reduce decision force.

**When a regulatory constraint is confirmed**

- Phase 6 may rise from screening to stronger applicability.
- Phase 5 may shift economic exposure materially.
- Phase 8 may move urgency or create no-go flags.

**When a regulatory constraint falls**

- Phase 6 must downgrade or block the front.
- Phase 5 must remove or weaken related cost/risk.
- Phase 8 may reopen previously blocked options.

### 7.4 Outcome classes

`upgrade`  
Support gained materially; stronger bounded language allowed.

`maintain`  
No material gain and no material loss; current ceiling remains.

`degrade`  
Support weakened, boundary narrowed, or semantic force must fall.

`hold`  
Material front remains alive but a decisive dependency blocks stronger conclusion.

`block`  
Front no longer deserves publication or methodological investment in current form.

`do_not_upgrade`  
Partial evidence gain exists, but a named condition still forbids stronger language.

### 7.5 Downgrade triggers

At minimum:

- contradiction by stronger local evidence,
- missing boundary that was previously implied,
- baseline instability,
- failed calibration,
- unresolved decisive trigger field,
- widened variance,
- unsupported extrapolation,
- loss of a critical assumption,
- discovered instrumentation insufficiency,
- phase conflict not preservable under previous claim intensity.

### 7.6 Hold triggers

At minimum:

- historian unavailable,
- missing decisive meter,
- unconfirmed control sequence,
- unresolved regulatory trigger field,
- incomplete baseline,
- missing local cost critical to a financial claim,
- missing field classification critical to applicability.

### 7.7 Block triggers

At minimum:

- claim no longer materially affects the case,
- route to hardening is disproportionate,
- claim boundary is ungovernable,
- evidence remains purely proxy-based while stronger claim is being attempted,
- or the claim would mislead if published even after degradation.

### 7.8 Do-not-upgrade triggers

At minimum:

- unresolved decisive local evidence,
- still-proxy-only support,
- non-hardened baseline,
- unresolved instrumentation gap,
- unresolved model misfit,
- regulatory applicability still inferential,
- financial closure still dominated by unverified technical assumptions.

### 7.9 Publication restrictions

Nothing may circulate if:

- it exceeds current support state,
- it hides material uncertainty,
- it hides material conflict,
- it converts screening into closure,
- it uses precision to simulate certainty,
- or it presents a phase as hardened merely because it appears in the report.

### 7.10 Redaction rules

Preferred language:

- compatible with
- plausible
- bounded by
- remains conditional on
- requires validation of
- currently screening as
- not yet closed
- materially supported within
- verified only for

Blocked language under weak evidence:

- is
- proves
- confirms
- guarantees
- complies
- the best decision is
- will save
- bankable

## 8. Reporting architecture for a full-stack but graded report

### 8.1 Governing idea

The report may be structurally complete before it is evidentially complete.

That is acceptable if and only if:

- each phase is visibly status-tagged,
- each section preserves its semantic ceiling,
- the path to stronger closure is explicit,
- and the reader can tell what is preliminary, hardened, or verified.

### 8.2 Fixed report skeleton

1. Case framing and intended use
2. Phase 0 — observation design and evidence boundary
3. Phase 1 — physical prior and PIML reading
4. Phase 2 — systemic twin reading
5. Phase 3 — operational model reading
6. Phase 4 — validation / verification path
7. Phase 5 — financial range and risk
8. Phase 6 — regulatory screening / compliance state
9. Phase 7 — belief state and contradiction map
10. Phase 8 — TAD priorities and decision admissibility
11. Cross-phase uncertainty, blockers, and next evidence

### 8.3 Mandatory status card per phase section

Each phase section should expose:

```yaml
phase_section_status:
  phase_presence_state:
  strongest_claim_support_state:
  closure_state:
  intended_use:
  evidence_basis_summary:
  what_is_not_closed_yet:
  upgrade_path_summary:
```

### 8.4 How a preliminary phase remains serious

A preliminary phase remains serious when it does not apologize for incompleteness. Instead it:

- defines its bounded contribution,
- names what it can already support,
- names what it still cannot support,
- and shows the path to stronger closure.

### 8.5 Separation between complete structure and complete closure

The document may be complete in structure even if:

- only selected claims are decision-grade,
- some phases are scaffolded,
- some sections are dominated by uncertainty,
- and only a few bounded components are verified.

That is not weakness. It is honest architecture.

### 8.6 Cross-phase synthesis layer

The report should include a cross-phase synthesis table with:

- strongest live claims,
- live conflicts,
- principal blockers,
- validation priorities,
- regulatory exposure state,
- financial fragility state,
- and current TAD posture.

### 8.7 Executive presentation rule

Executive readability is allowed.

Executive hardening is prohibited.

The executive layer may compress text but may not remove:

- material uncertainty,
- regulatory incompleteness,
- financial fragility,
- or the conditional nature of priorities.

## 10. Brutal truth section

### 10.1 What is feasible now with public data and sparse inputs

Feasible now:

- Phase 0 constitution
- Phase 1 physical priors
- coarse Phase 2 structural twin
- early Phase 3 operating hypotheses
- Phase 4 as verification-path design only
- Phase 5 probabilistic finance as range logic
- Phase 6 regulatory screening
- Phase 7 belief-state governance
- Phase 8 preliminary prioritization

### 10.2 What is only feasible in preliminary form without local evidence

- physics-based causal narrowing,
- twin fidelity,
- operating regime certainty,
- financial ranges tight enough for capital commitment,
- compliance assertions stronger than screening,
- and TAD stronger than priority logic.

### 10.3 What requires real local evidence

- hardened subsystem claims,
- real baseline,
- measured load behavior,
- trigger-confirmed regulatory applicability,
- implementation-grade finance,
- and bounded final decisions with high decision weight.

### 10.4 What requires actual measurement and verification

- verified savings,
- validated operational effect,
- formal M&V outcomes,
- strong counterfactual claims,
- and any serious statement that performance has been proven.

### 10.5 What is product

Product:

- the phase contracts,
- data model,
- evidence handling,
- state transitions,
- reporting architecture,
- update engine,
- and governance enforcement.

### 10.6 What is still research

Research:

- how strong a PIML stack is truly useful under sparse industrial evidence,
- how coarse a twin can remain and still add value,
- the best probabilistic finance formalism for low-data industrial cases,
- the exact formalism for cross-phase belief revision,
- and the degree to which TAD can be standardized without hiding local value judgments.

### 10.7 What is smoke if promised too early

Smoke:

- real-time high-fidelity digital twin from sparse evidence,
- verified savings from public data,
- bankable finance from unvalidated technical ranges,
- compliance closure from screening,
- "AI knows the root cause" language,
- and presenting all 8 phases as equally mature from day one.

### 10.8 Main over-design risks

- epistemology ornamental enough to sound profound but too vague to govern outputs,
- phase inflation where every phase sounds powerful but none is bounded,
- twin inflation,
- PIML inflation,
- compliance theater,
- finance theater,
- and TAD inflation dressed up as optimization.

## 11. Final synthesis: governing doctrine of the complete framework

The doctrine of the complete framework is as follows:

1. All 8 phases may exist from the first report.
2. Their presence does not imply equal evidentiary force.
3. The framework is useful under sparse evidence because it can still narrow, screen, prioritize, and block bad decisions.
4. The framework becomes stronger not by sounding more certain, but by earning stronger support.
5. Decision-grade intelligence is early, useful, and bounded.
6. Verification-grade certainty is late, local, and expensive.
7. Phase 5, 6, 7, and 8 belong in the architecture from the start because capital, regulation, belief revision, and prioritization are present from the start.
8. They must enter proportionally, not theatrically.
9. No phase may become sovereign.
10. The LLM is never the final judge of truth.
11. Evidence accumulation must change claims, ranges, priorities, and publication rights in explicit and auditable ways.
12. A short honest report is superior to a complete-looking false closure.

The complete framework is therefore not a promise of total early knowledge.

It is a governed machine for turning partial knowledge into admissible decisions, and for progressively converging toward stronger closure without lying about where the system still remains uncertain.
