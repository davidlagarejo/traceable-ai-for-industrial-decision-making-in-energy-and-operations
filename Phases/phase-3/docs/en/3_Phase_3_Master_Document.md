# 3A — Scope, Primary Material Unit, and MVP Scope of Phase 3

> Canonical governance note: this document belongs to an earlier working line of the framework. The authoritative constitutional document for the integrated 8-phase architecture is `Phases/phase-0/docs/en/0_Phase_0_Master_Document.md`. If this document conflicts with Phase 0 on numbering, phase authority, allowed claims, support ceilings, or boundary logic, Phase 0 governs until this phase is formally reconstituted.

## 1. Exact purpose of the subphase
Define the constitution of Phase 3 as the layer of the framework that materializes, organizes, and makes visible content already supported by upstream objects, under explicit epistemic discipline. Its function is to fix what the Reporting Engine exactly is, what its primary material unit is, what its higher assembly unit is, what enters and what remains outside the MVP, what the minimum audience separation is, and where its authority ends relative to a possible Phase 4.

## 2. Why this subphase matters
Without this subphase, the system faces two opposite risks: that reporting becomes confused with terminal reasoning, or that out of fear of overreaching it collapses into a poor mechanics of summaries and tables. Phase 3 must block both errors.

The Reporting Engine should not settle for producing correct outputs. It must aspire to reports that are technically absorbing, structurally elegant, sectorally fine-grained, and capable of making a complex case intelligible as a system without exceeding the framework's real support.

## 3. What Phase 3 is and what it is not
Phase 3 is a governed materialization layer. Its authority consists of selecting, articulating, compacting, and assembling admissible upstream objects into visible outputs useful for human consumption while preserving conflict, uncertainty, assumptions, validation dependency, and traceability.

The mother law of Phase 3 is fixed here and governs the rest of the document: **no visible output of Phase 3 may say more than what is supported by the upstream objects on which it depends**. Later subphases do not create parallel laws; they only specialize this law in pipeline, assembly, audience, compaction, and circulation.

Phase 3 is not a layer for discovering new hypotheses, nor a layer for empirical validation, nor a final compliance layer, nor a terminal recommendation layer. It is also not a license to replace structure with ornamental storytelling, nor a phase whose value depends on decorative visual design or implicit persuasion.

## 4. What enters Phase 3 and what does not
Only the visible and governed transformation of admissible upstream content enters Phase 3. This includes material output assembly, proportional semantic compression, thematic organization, audience views, disciplined tables, governed captions, visible validation agenda, subordinated artifacts, and structured export.

Everything that would discover, verify, or close the case beyond available support remains outside. Phase 3 may organize and make already supported content readable without raising its force or closing through presentation what remains open upstream.

## 5. Primary material unit
The correct primary material unit of Phase 3 is the **`Output Block`**. It should be understood as the minimum visible unit that can be traced back to upstream objects, assembled into more than one view, audited, degraded, reassigned, or blocked without losing material identity or breaking the report as a whole.

`Output Block` is the correct unit because it separates governed content from documentary composition, carries explicit limits, preserves lineage, tolerates reuse, and supports fine-grained circulation control. It also works well in sparse cases, where the system must be able to publish little without inflating narrative to compensate for material scarcity.

`report_object`, `report_section`, `artifact_object`, and `delivery_package` are not adequate as the main unit because they mix levels, depend too heavily on final layout, cover only a portion of the visible surface, or belong to the assembly and distribution level.

## 6. Higher assembly unit
The higher assembly unit of Phase 3 is the **`Report Package`**. It is not the system's primary unit but the governed composition of already admitted `Output Blocks`.

The `Report Package` exists to make the case intelligible as a system. Its value lies not only in gathering correct pieces, but in ordering, contrasting, and articulating them so that the reader can read tensions, constraints, dependencies, and validation paths in an integrated sequence.

That integrative character does not grant sovereignty. No `Report Package` may contain content that does not first exist as a traceable block. The package composes, organizes, and projects; it does not invent or correct the case.

## 7. Minimum audience policy
The MVP allows only two human views: `technical_view` and `executive_view`. This separation is deliberately minimal. It responds to two real reading regimes without prematurely fragmenting the system into stakeholder taxonomies that would be too costly to govern.

The difference between views is documentary, not epistemic. `technical_view` exposes relationships, constraints, and traces with greater density; `executive_view` reduces friction, concentrates active fronts, and improves orientation. Neither may alter case status or make what remains conditional sound more resolved.

## 8. Realistic MVP scope
The MVP of Phase 3 must be compact in architecture and deep in documentary capacity. What is compact is the set of units, views, and outputs; what may be deep is the document whenever the case warrants it.

That minimum scope is fixed as:
- `output_block_register`
- `audience_view_register`
- `artifact_register`
- `machine_export_bundle`
- `report_package`
- two human views
- a minimum block taxonomy already aligned with the rest of the phase
- and circulation rules sufficient to govern publication

The MVP's minimum taxonomy should not introduce a provisional ontology different from the one used later. From 3A onward it is aligned with the material grammar developed in the rest of the document. At minimum, the following must exist:
- `executive_summary_block`
- `technical_summary_block`
- `evidence_table_block`
- `uncertainty_block`
- `conflict_block`
- `opportunity_block`
- `validation_agenda_block`
- `next_steps_block`
- `artifact_caption_block`

This taxonomy is minimal, not exhaustive. Its purpose is to provide a sufficient base for serious reports without yet opening an unnecessary proliferation of types.

## 9. What would be over-engineering in this subphase
In 3A, any expansion that increases surface area without increasing real governed materialization capacity constitutes over-engineering. This includes sophisticated visual theming, complex layouts, broad chart libraries, excessive stakeholder separation, dashboards, advanced customization, ambitious multimodal narrative, and final regulatory reporting. It is also over-engineering to multiply block types, views, or outputs before proving that the MVP core can produce strong documents with a still-compact architecture.

## 10. Boundary with Phase 4
Phase 3 ends when admissible upstream content has been converted into governed, readable, and exportable visible outputs. If a Phase 4 later exists, that phase may handle outputs of greater terminal weight. By definition, Phase 3 may not behave as a verification bridge, compliance engine, or final recommendation layer.

The boundary must remain hard. Phase 3 may show conflict, organize preliminary priorities, make a validation agenda visible, and articulate a powerful internal reading of the case. It may not close verification, certify compliance, or turn a candidate opportunity into a terminal decision.

## 11. Typical errors this subphase must block
- Confusing readability with permission to intensify certainty.
- Reducing Phase 3 to a defensive layer unable to produce genuinely strong documents.
- Treating `Report Package` as the primary unit and losing granular control.
- Using a provisional block ontology different from the one that later governs the phase.
- Turning audience into versions with different truth.
- Using design or storytelling to compensate for upstream material weakness.
- Expanding views, layouts, or outputs before closing the MVP core.

## 12. Exact decisions that 3A closes
- Phase 3 is a governed materialization layer for admissible upstream content.
- The phase's mother law is fixed in 3A and governs the rest of the document.
- The primary material unit is `Output Block`.
- The higher assembly unit is `Report Package`.
- The MVP only allows `technical_view` and `executive_view`.
- The MVP is compact in architecture but may produce deep documents when the case requires it.
- The minimum block taxonomy is aligned from 3A onward with the material grammar developed later.
- Empirical validation, terminal case closure, and ornamental proliferation of formats or products remain outside Phase 3's scope.
- The boundary with Phase 4 is explicitly closed.

## 13. Completion criterion for the subphase
Subphase 3A is considered closed when Phase 3 is clearly defined as a governed materialization layer; when the document's mother law is installed as a single central principle; when `Output Block`, `Report Package`, the minimum audience policy, and the realistic MVP scope are fixed without ambiguity; and when the phase appears as a compact architecture capable of producing strong documentation without exceeding the system's support.

# 3A.3 — Transversal Thematic Architecture of the Report Package

## 1. Exact purpose of the subphase
Define the transversal thematic architecture of the `Report Package` as the grammar that allows multiple fronts of the case to be organized without turning that organization into free invention. Its function is to distinguish material block type from transversal thematic layer, fix the MVP's minimum layers, and establish how themes and subthemes emerge from traceable upstream objects in order to produce a deep, integrated, and disciplined reading.

## 2. Why this subphase matters
Without transversal thematic architecture, the report falls into one of two forms of poverty. Either it becomes a succession of correct but disconnected blocks, unable to produce systemic vision. Or it compensates for that weakness with prestigious sections, decorative subtitles, and false sophistication.

This subphase matters because thematic architecture does not only discipline what may be shown. It also enables genuine documentary power: the capacity to articulate multiple fronts of the case, reveal cross-dependencies, order non-trivial tensions, and make intelligible the structure of constraints, trade-offs, and validation paths that define the case as a system.

## 3. What it means that Phase 3 does define the thematic architecture of the report
That Phase 3 defines the thematic architecture means that it may decide which fronts of the case deserve visibility, how to group them, which themes require greater development, and in what sequence they should be exposed. That authority is documentary and structural. It does not consist of freely deciding what would be interesting to say, but of organizing already supported content in a readable way.

Its authority consists in organizing, articulating, and making visible already supported content without exceeding the epistemic status of the case. Thematic architecture exists to make the report more powerful, not more arbitrary.

## 4. Difference between material block type and transversal thematic layer
Phase 3 architecture operates across two distinct dimensions.

The first is the **material block type**. This includes forms such as:
- `executive_summary_block`
- `technical_summary_block`
- `evidence_table_block`
- `uncertainty_block`
- `conflict_block`
- `opportunity_block`
- `validation_agenda_block`
- `next_steps_block`
- `artifact_caption_block`

The material type defines the visible function of the block, its form of compaction, and the kind of documentary operation it can perform.

The second is the **transversal thematic layer**. Each block may belong to one or more layers such as:
- `technical_layer`
- `regulatory_layer`
- `financial_layer`
- `prioritization_layer`
- `validation_lineage_layer`

The thematic layer does not define what kind of block the block is, but from which front of the case it is being read. This double structure allows depth without unnecessarily inflating material ontology.

## 5. Minimum transversal thematic layers of the MVP
The **`technical_layer`** makes visible technical structure, operating tensions, plausible mechanisms, material constraints, and functional dependencies of the case.

The **`regulatory_layer`** makes visible plausible regulatory pressure, preliminary regime, applicable flags, timing windows, and validation dependency needed to confirm applicability.

The **`financial_layer`** makes visible preliminary economic logic, CAPEX/OPEX constraints, viability sensitivity, tariff exposure, preliminary compatibility with financial mechanisms, and relevant benefits when support exists.

The **`prioritization_layer`** makes visible preliminary order of attention, reasons for focus, relative urgency, blockages, sequences, and action constraints.

The **`validation_lineage_layer`** makes visible how the current state of the case is supported, what strengthens or weakens it, what depends on benchmark, prior, hypothesis, or field experience, and why the next validation matters.

These five layers do not exhaust case reality, but they stabilize a sufficient transversal grammar so that the document may grow without losing structure.

## 6. How themes and subthemes emerge from the document
Themes and subthemes do not emerge only from the block type, nor only from the audience, nor from the LLM's free prose. They emerge from the combination of active upstream objects, materially admissible blocks, activated thematic layers, and real relevance for understanding, prioritization, or validation.

A **theme** is understood as a material reading front of the case. A **subtheme** is understood as a material refinement of that front when the reading requires more granularity in order to remain faithful and useful. **Structured technical narrative** is understood as the legitimate operation that articulates blocks, themes, and dependencies so that the reader can understand why a front matters, what conditions it, and how it connects to other fronts of the case.

That technical narrative is admissible when it orders already supported relationships. It ceases to be admissible when it fills gaps, invents causality, or uses fluent reading to simulate closure.

## 7. Documentary depth without epistemic inflation
Phase 3 may produce broad, detailed, technically absorbing, financially serious, regulatorily useful documents that are pleasant to read because of their organization. That depth is not a secondary luxury. It is part of the Reporting Engine's reason to exist.

Documentary depth must increase articulation, traceability, and systemic intelligibility, not epistemic force. A document may become richer because it better separates fronts, makes cross-dependencies visible, better organizes tensions, and makes a complex system readable. It does not therefore become more conclusive.

## 8. Admissible use of base documents and methodological frameworks
Phase 3 may use base documents, methodological frameworks, and admitted references to enrich structure, framing, thematic taxonomy, and order of exposition. That use is legitimate when it makes the document more mature and better organized.

Those materials do not replace local evidence, do not harden claims by themselves, and do not activate substantive themes that the case does not materially support. Their value lies in disciplining documentary construction, not in lending epistemic authority to the case.

## 9. Policy of bounded internal evaluation
Within the MVP, the `Report Package` may legitimately aspire to look like a **technical-operational-financial-regulatory internal evaluation bounded to the case**. That formulation should be read as a valid ambition of documentary robustness, not as a promise of terminal closure.

The report may feel serious, deep, and structurally mature. It may give the impression of reading the case from the inside. But that impression must come from articulated density and not from overassertion.

## 10. Anti-thematic-decoration rule
Thematic decoration is blocked. Themes, subthemes, or sections whose main function is to sound sophisticated, fill space, or imitate consulting style without real material load must not enter.

If a front does not improve understanding, prioritization, validation, or traceability, it should not enter. Structural elegance is desirable; empty ornamentation is not.

## 11. Typical errors this subphase must block
- Confusing thematic architecture with license to invent content.
- Treating documentary depth as a synonym for greater truth.
- Using audience alone to determine which themes exist.
- Replacing structured linkage with articulate but non-traceable prose.
- Turning `validation_lineage_layer` into decorative narrative.
- Multiplying themes through sector prestige or superficial density.

## 12. Exact decisions that 3A.3 closes
- Phase 3 does define the thematic architecture of the report.
- That architecture exists both to discipline and to strengthen the document.
- Theme, subtheme, and structured technical narrative are distinguished as different operations.
- Material block type and transversal thematic layer are distinct and complementary dimensions.
- The MVP's minimum layers are fixed as `technical_layer`, `regulatory_layer`, `financial_layer`, `prioritization_layer`, and `validation_lineage_layer`.
- Documentary depth may grow through articulation and organization, not through increased epistemic force.
- Base documents may enrich structure and framing, but not replace case support.
- The internal evaluation policy is authorized in a bounded sense.
- Any thematic decoration without material function is blocked.

## 13. Completion criterion for the subphase
Subphase 3A.3 is considered closed when thematic architecture is defined as a positive capability of the Reporting Engine and not only as a defensive mechanism; when material type, thematic layer, theme, subtheme, and structured technical narrative are differentiated without conceptual noise; and when the document may aspire to an integrated reading of the case without departing from the mother law fixed in 3A.

# 3A.4 — Sectoral Theme Library and Rules for Activating Industry Themes and Subthemes

## 1. Exact purpose of the subphase
Define the sectoral theme library as the Reporting Engine's capacity to expand coverage, depth, and industrial differentiation without sliding into narrative arbitrariness or content fabrication. Its function is to fix the multi-level architecture of sector modules, themes, and subthemes and the disciplined rules under which they are activated.

## 2. Why this subphase matters
An overly short thematic scheme impoverishes the report, makes the case generic, and loses real second-order tensions. An overly loose scheme turns depth into documentary theater. This subphase matters because it enables serious breadth and at the same time prevents empty exuberance.

Sectoral richness is not a secondary concession of the system. It is a central capability of the Reporting Engine. Without it, the report could not capture real operating constraints, infrastructure dependencies, regulatory frictions, prioritization structures, or sectoral conflicts that a generic scheme cannot express with enough value.

## 3. What it means to allow sectoral breadth and depth in Phase 3
Allowing sectoral breadth and depth means authorizing the `Report Package` not to remain limited to a short catalog of general themes. It may open industry modules, specialized themes, second-order subthemes, and extensive documentary developments whenever the case warrants it.

Documentary breadth may grow aggressively when the case demands it, as long as activation remains traceable, materially pertinent, and useful for understanding, prioritization, or validation. Sectoral expansion should not be read as a deviation from the framework, but as a condition for the document to have real technical value.

## 4. Multi-level thematic architecture of the Report Package
The thematic architecture of the `Report Package` operates at four levels:
- Level 1 — Fixed transversal layers
- Level 2 — Sectoral or industrial modules
- Level 3 — Main sectoral themes

This hierarchy is conceptual and normative. Its job is to order depth, not to produce premature technical implementation.

# 3B.1 — Transformation Pipeline: From Upstream Objects to Rendered Blocks

## 1. Exact purpose of the subphase
Define the minimum pipeline through which Phase 3 transforms structured upstream objects into visible, verifiable, and registrable `Output Blocks`. Its function is to close the nature of the input, the MVP's minimum transformation sequence, the pipeline's control unit, the separation between selection, mapping, construction, verification, and registration, and the formal intermediate output on which later assembly will rest.

## 2. Why this subphase matters
Without a pipeline, the step from upstream records to visible text would tend to happen through narrative continuity rather than controlled transformation. That weakens traceability, erases the material function of each piece, and obscures the relation between support and exposure.

The pipeline does not exist only to prevent overstatement. It also exists to ensure that each visible block reaches assembly with a clear function, legible support, coherent thematic placement, and real potential to contribute to a strong systemic reading of the case.

## 3. Nature of the Phase 3 input
The input of Phase 3 is not free text. Phase 3 consumes exclusively:
- structured objects from Phase 1
- structured objects from Phase 2
- inherited rules from Phase 0
- the already closed block taxonomy and thematic architecture
- and, when appropriate, admitted base documents only as structural or contextual support

Free notes, unregistered intuitions, uncontrolled narrative drafts, and any non-traceable input remain outside.

## 4. Minimum MVP transformation pipeline
The MVP's minimum pipeline contains six stages:

1. **Governed intake.** Receives the universe of candidate objects relevant to the case.
2. **Selection of admissible material.** Decides which objects deserve to enter rendering based on material relevance and real function.
3. **Thematic-material mapping.** Assigns each selected object to a block type, thematic layer, possible audience, and documentary function.
4. **Disciplined block construction.** Generates the visible block using only the material authorized by its type and purpose.
5. **Block verification.** Reviews linkage, limits, verbal proportionality, audience compatibility, and material function.
6. **Registration for assembly.** Incorporates approved blocks into the `output_block_register`.

The sequence is not bureaucratic. It is the minimum infrastructure required for each block to reach assembly with clear identity, recognizable support, and real documentary usefulness.

## 5. Prohibition of a direct jump to the final report
The pattern `upstream object -> final report` is prohibited. The formal pipeline rule is `upstream object -> governed block -> assembly`.

Among others, flows must be blocked where `hypothesis_register` feeds a final summary directly, `opportunity_candidate_matrix` becomes a visible recommendation, `validation_queue` is transformed without mediation into a roadmap, or an artifact circulates without caption and without semantic control.

## 6. Pipeline control unit
The pipeline's control unit is the `Output Block`. The pipeline does not operate on full chapters or finished packages. It operates on minimum units capable of carrying function, limits, traceability, and assembly potential.

A section is too large for fine control. The block allows precise selection, localized degradation, punctual blocking, and disciplined reassignment.

## 7. Rule of selection versus inclusion
Selection does not imply automatic inclusion. A valid upstream object does not thereby gain the right to appear in the report.

It should only advance if it changes the reading of the case, preserves a relevant tension, affects prioritization, explains a blockage, strengthens validation, or prevents overreading. Exhaustiveness alone is not sufficient.

## 8. Rule of mapping before compaction
Mapping must happen before any compaction. First lineage, block type, thematic layer, possible audience, limits, and documentary function are fixed. Only then is the visible block summarized or formulated.

Summarizing before classification usually produces distortion: conflicts that sound minor, hypotheses that look like conclusions, or validation agendas that already sound like action plans.

## 9. Degradation, reassignment, and blocking
The pipeline must allow semantic force to be degraded, material to be moved toward `uncertainty_block` or `conflict_block`, reassigned into the validation agenda, or completely blocked from visible circulation.

These outcomes do not indicate system failure. They indicate that the architecture retains control when the case does not support strong exposure in the form first considered.

## 10. Restricted use of base documents
Base documents may intervene only to enrich thematic mapping, structural framing, or block organization. They may not close hypotheses, replace local support, or harden claims.

## 11. Formal output of the subphase
The formal output of 3B.1 is the **`output_block_register`**.

It contains blocks that are already selected, mapped, constructed, verified, and ready for assembly or audit. It is the first fully governed material output of Phase 3.

## 12. Typical errors this subphase must block
- Treating the Phase 3 input as a prior narrative of the case.
- Converting upstream records directly into final text.
- Selecting by exhaustiveness instead of by function.
- Compacting before fixing lineage and function.
- Using sections as the pipeline control unit.
- Preventing degradation or blocking and forcing exposure by inertia.

## 13. Exact decisions that 3B.1 closes
- The input of Phase 3 is structured and traceable.
- The MVP's minimum pipeline contains exactly six stages.
- The pipeline control unit is the `Output Block`.
- No upstream object may jump directly to the final report.
- Selection is not equivalent to mandatory inclusion.
- Mapping precedes compaction.
- The pipeline must allow degradation, reassignment, and blocking.
- The formal output of the subphase is the `output_block_register`.

## 14. Completion criterion for the subphase
Subphase 3B.1 is considered closed when the path from upstream objects to governed blocks is unequivocal, sufficient, and does not require silent reinterpretation in order to produce visible material of high documentary quality.

# 3B.2 — Assembly of the Report Package and Relationship Between Views, Blocks, and Artifacts

## 1. Exact purpose of the subphase
Define the assembly of the `Report Package` as a governed composition operation built from already admitted blocks, projected into specific views and complemented by subordinated artifacts. Its function is to close what the package exactly is, how it is composed, what relationship it has to the MVP's views, which minimum composition rules it must obey, how sparse cases must be treated, and what the formal output of this stage is.

## 2. Why this subphase matters
Without this subphase, assembly would tend to behave like free drafting of the final document. Views would become parallel writing engines, artifacts would become persuasive channels, and the package would lose its fine link to the blocks that support it.

This subphase matters because the `Report Package` must not only compose admissible blocks. It must also be capable of making the case intelligible as a system, where sequence, contrast between fronts, thematic articulation, and artifact subordination contribute to systemic understanding rather than to the mere accumulation of correct pieces.

## 3. Nature of the Report Package
The `Report Package` is not a monolithic document or a sovereign narrative piece. It is a governed composition of previously admitted blocks, ordered to produce an integrated reading of the case.

Its value lies in the organized whole. It may contrast fronts, show dependencies, group materials that reveal trade-offs, and build progressions that move from technical structure toward validation and prioritization implications. That compositional sophistication is legitimate when it remains subordinate to the case's real support.

## 4. Exclusive assembly source
The `Report Package` may be composed only from the **`output_block_register`**. Any direct assembly from upstream records, loose text, free notes, or ad hoc formulations generated during composition is blocked.

The reason is structural: if the package could incorporate content outside the block register, assembly would effectively become a second analytical instance.

## 5. Nature of audience views
`technical_view` and `executive_view` do not create content. They are disciplined projections of the available universe of blocks.

They may select, reorganize, compact, or exclude admitted blocks, but they may not introduce new semantics. Their job is to modulate access and reading density without producing another version of the case.

## 6. Relationship between technical_view and executive_view
For the MVP, `technical_view` is the structurally more complete base view. `executive_view` derives from it through controlled compaction.

This hierarchy matters because the executive view should simplify access, not govern the case first and let the technical one qualify it afterward. Executive reading may not sound more conclusive, erase material conflict, or hide critical uncertainty.

## 7. Relationship between Report Package and artifact_register
Artifacts are subordinated to the `Report Package`. They may circulate only if they improve material understanding of the case and if their reading can remain faithful to the support of the blocks that sustain them.

Every visible artifact must be accompanied by an `artifact_caption_block` or equivalent semantic control. The artifact does not replace the block nor create a parallel truth channel; it amplifies understanding when it remains within the same limits as the rest of the package.

## 8. Minimum composition rules
Assembly must obey at least these rules:
- **Minimum coverage.** The package must cover, when the case requires it, technical state, relevant uncertainty, material conflicts, validation agenda, and preliminary priority or next-step logic.
- **No theatricality.** Pieces are not added merely so that the package “looks complete.”
- **No empty duplication.** The same content should not be repeated unless its function or audience changes.
- **Balance.** There may not be overexposure of opportunity with underexposure of uncertainty or conflict if the case remains open.
- **Semantic hierarchy.** Order must serve understanding, not persuasion.

In addition, composition may use contrast between fronts, sequences showing dependency, and groupings that reveal trade-offs when this improves systemic reading.

## 9. Sparse cases and valid asymmetry
A valid `Report Package` may be short, asymmetrical, dominated by uncertainty, with little or no visible opportunity, with a validation agenda taking precedence, and with no artifacts.

The package's validity does not depend on symmetry or on an appearance of completeness. A sparse case must be allowed to circulate as a brief, honest, and useful reading, even when its main value is to clarify uncertainty and orient validation.

## 10. Conditional inclusion and the law of compressibility by view
An admitted block does not necessarily enter both views. The law of compressibility is fixed as follows: **if a block cannot be compacted without losing critical limits, it should not enter `executive_view`**.

Exclusion by view is not a loss of coverage. It is a legitimate decision of fidelity.

## 11. Logical reading sequence of the package
Although this subphase does not design UI or final layout, there must still be a minimum reading logic. The sequence of the package should favor case orientation, technical structure, conflict and uncertainty, candidate opportunity where applicable, validation agenda, and next steps.

Packages that open with proposed action or a persuasive artifact before orienting the reading are blocked.

## 12. Formal output of the subphase
The formal output of 3B.2 is the **`view_assembly_manifest`**.

This manifest determines which blocks enter which view, in what order, with what degree of compression, with which associated artifacts, and under what circulation restrictions.

## 13. Typical errors this subphase must block
- Treating the package as monolithic text drafted from scratch.
- Assembling it from sources outside the `output_block_register`.
- Using views as parallel writing engines.
- Designing the executive view first and attaching the technical one afterward.
- Treating artifacts as visual persuasion rather than subordinated comprehension aids.
- Forcing symmetry and density in sparse cases.

## 14. Exact decisions that 3B.2 closes
- The `Report Package` is a governed and integrative composition.
- Its exclusive assembly source is the `output_block_register`.
- `technical_view` is the base view and `executive_view` derives from it.
- Artifacts are subordinated and not autonomous.
- Composition may be sophisticated when it improves systemic understanding.
- Sparse cases are valid.
- View compressibility governs inclusion or exclusion.
- The formal output of the subphase is the `view_assembly_manifest`.

## 15. Completion criterion for the subphase
Subphase 3B.2 is considered closed when `Report Package` assembly is defined as a disciplined, integrative, and non-sovereign operation; when the relationship among blocks, views, and artifacts is unequivocal; and when composition can produce a holistic reading of the case without breaking the mother law of Phase 3.

# 3B.3 — Rules of Epistemic Inheritance During Rendering

## 1. Exact purpose of the subphase
Define how the mother law of Phase 3 governs rendering. Its function is to fix three principles: every visible output inherits limits in addition to content; no rendering operation may harden the case; and if a format loses visible support or detaches critical restrictions, semantics must degrade.

## 2. Why this subphase matters
An output may be well written, well ordered, and visually clear, and still betray the case if conflict, uncertainty, assumptions, or validation dependency were lost during rendering. This subphase matters because Phase 3's main risk is not only inventing content, but silently hardening real content through format change.

## 3. What it means to inherit limits in addition to content
Every interpretive surface of the package inherits limits in addition to content. The rule covers blocks, views, artifacts, captions, titles, headers, labels, tables, order of exposition, and any other signal that guides reading.

Inheritance includes both what the material allows to be said and what it prevents from being said. A faithful output preserves the epistemic contour that defines the real force of what it materializes.

## 4. Rule of no increase in epistemic force
No rendering operation may increase the epistemic force of the material it processes. This applies to writing, summary, titling, ordering, visualization, compaction, view reassignment, and assembly.

A hypothesis does not become confirmation because it is better written. A conflict does not become reconciliation because it is compacted. A candidate opportunity does not become a decision because it changes position or view.

## 5. Compaction with preservation of critical limits
Compacting does not mean softening. Any valid compaction must preserve conditionality, material conflict, critical uncertainty, validation dependency, and any limit without which the block would change meaning.

If a block cannot be compacted while preserving those critical limits, that compaction is not admissible.

## 6. Inheritance by material dependency
Inheritance operates by material dependency rather than by textual proximity. A block inherits limits from all upstream objects necessary to sustain it, not only from the last object mentioned or from the nearest fragment.

This matters especially in composite blocks, summaries, opportunities, and views that condense several blocks. Documentary fragmentation cannot be used to drop inconvenient limits.

## 7. Inheritance of limits in audience views
`technical_view` and `executive_view` inherit the same epistemic contour even though they do not expose the same volume of text. The executive view may reduce density, but it may not reduce the case's substantive restrictions.

Less text does not mean fewer limits.

## 8. Epistemic inheritance in artifacts and captions
Artifacts and captions are subject to the same general rule. If they cannot make visible, or at least not contradict, conflict, uncertainty, and validation dependency, they should not circulate.

## 9. Prohibition of hardening through omission or reordering
Omission is valid only if it does not alter the substantive interpretation of the case. Likewise, reordering may not be used to induce more closure than support allows.

## 10. Inheritance in titles, headers, and labels
Titles, headers, labels, and captions are not an exception; they form part of the interpretive surfaces already covered by the general rule. Many overstatements are born there rather than in the body.

## 11. Mandatory semantic degradation
If, during rendering or assembly, a block loses visible support, changes view, is strongly compacted, or becomes detached from part of its restrictions, it may become mandatory to degrade its semantic intensity.

Degradation is not an optional stylistic refinement. It is a duty of fidelity when the new format no longer supports the prior formulation.

## 12. Inheritance as a circulation criterion
Epistemic inheritance is not a good editorial practice. It is a circulation criterion. If it fails, the output should not publish, should not export, or must be rewritten and degraded before circulation.

## 13. Typical errors this subphase must block
- Assuming that correct rendering equals epistemic fidelity.
- Inheriting content but not limits.
- Using compaction, order, or reading signals to harden the case.
- Treating the executive view as if it could expose fewer substantive restrictions.
- Treating degradation as optional when visible support decreases.

## 14. Exact decisions that 3B.3 closes
- Every interpretive surface of Phase 3 inherits limits in addition to content.
- No rendering operation may harden the case.
- Compaction requires preservation of critical limits.
- Inheritance operates by material dependency.
- Views inherit the same epistemic contour even if they differ in density.
- Artifacts, captions, titles, and labels are subject to the same discipline.
- Semantic degradation becomes mandatory when format loses support.
- A failure of inheritance is a cause of non-circulation.

## 15. Completion criterion for the subphase
Subphase 3B.3 is considered closed when epistemic inheritance is expressed in a central, compact, and sufficient way; when it governs every interpretive surface of the package; and when mandatory degradation is established as a normal response to loss of support.

# 3C — MVP Output Contract

## 1. Exact purpose of the subphase
Define the formal output contract of the Phase 3 MVP. Its job is to fix which outputs actually exist, what the minimum function of each one is, what structural relation they hold to one another, and what remains outside the current scope.

## 2. Why this subphase matters
Without an output contract, Phase 3 ends in a vague notion of “report.” With a contract, it ends in a small, sufficient, and epistemically honest set of governed outputs.

## 3. Formally authorized MVP outputs
The MVP authorizes exactly:
- `report_package`
- `output_block_register`
- `audience_view_register`
- `artifact_register`
- `machine_export_bundle`

This set is sufficient. Any additional output at this stage would be premature proliferation unless a future need is explicitly justified.

## 4. Nature and function of each output
- `report_package`: main documentary output for human consumption.
- `output_block_register`: formal, auditable register of already governed blocks.
- `audience_view_register`: formal register of `technical_view` and `executive_view`.
- `artifact_register`: subordinated register of eligible artifacts.
- `machine_export_bundle`: minimum technical output for traceability, interoperability, and structured handoff.

## 5. Minimum content of each output
- `report_package`: at least one valid view, traceable blocks, disciplined sequence, and preserved inherited limits.
- `output_block_register`: selected, mapped, constructed, verified blocks ready for assembly or audit.
- `audience_view_register`: view-specific included blocks, reading order, compression level, circulation restrictions, and allowed artifacts where applicable.
- `artifact_register`: only artifacts supported by admitted blocks and accompanied by caption or equivalent semantic control.
- `machine_export_bundle`: enough structure to reconstruct the relation among package, views, blocks, and artifacts.

## 6. Dependency relationship among outputs
- `output_block_register` precedes and supports everything else.
- `audience_view_register` derives from already admitted blocks.
- `artifact_register` depends on blocks and views.
- `report_package` composes eligible views, blocks, and artifacts.
- `machine_export_bundle` structurally reflects that relation.

These dependencies are not optional.

## 7. Outputs explicitly outside the MVP
The following remain outside the MVP as autonomous outputs:
- `regulatory_report`
- `financial_report`
- `investment_memo`
- `compliance_packet`
- `verification_report`
- `presentation_deck`
- `dashboard_runtime`
- `multi-stakeholder package`

If any such content appears, it must live inside the `report_package`, not as an independent product.

## 8. What the output contract does not promise
No MVP output promises final diagnosis, final compliance, final ROI, savings verification, terminal recommendation, or closed empirical verification. `executive_view` does not promise a “nice summary of everything,” `artifact_register` does not promise complete visual coverage, and `machine_export_bundle` does not promise immediate universal integration.

## 9. Sparse cases and valid asymmetry
A sparse case may legitimately produce a short `report_package`, a minimal `executive_view`, an empty `artifact_register`, and strong validation weight. This does not constitute system failure.

## 10. Typical errors this subphase must block
- Treating “report” as a single totalizing output.
- Creating new outputs for every marginal documentary need.
- Reducing `output_block_register` to internal residue.
- Treating `artifact_register` as an autonomous truth channel.
- Requiring symmetry across cases and outputs.

## 11. Exact decisions that 3C closes
- The MVP authorizes exactly five formal outputs.
- Each output has a defined minimum function and structural dependency.
- The output contract is small, sufficient, and honest.
- Regulatory, financial, verification, or presentation-driven autonomous outputs remain outside the MVP.
- Sparse cases are valid configurations of the contract.

## 12. Completion criterion for the subphase
Subphase 3C is considered closed when the end of Phase 3 ceases to be a vague idea of “report” and is fixed as a brief, precise, and sufficient contract of governed outputs.

# 3D — Audience Policy and Compaction

## 1. Exact purpose of the subphase
Define how Phase 3 adapts the same material base to two different audiences without altering conflict, uncertainty, relevant assumptions, validation dependency, or proportional semantic force. Its function is to close what formal difference exists between `technical_view` and `executive_view`, what may be compacted and what may not, when material must be excluded from the executive view, and what language and tone limits govern each view.

## 2. Why this subphase matters
Without audience policy, the pursuit of clarity would tend to buy readability at the price of epistemic loss. This subphase matters because Phase 3 must not produce two truths, but two distinct accesses to the same case.

Well-executed compaction does not impoverish the case; it makes it more readable without breaking its substantive complexity.

## 3. Minimum audience policy of the MVP
The MVP allows only:
- `technical_view`
- `executive_view`

Separate views for regulatory, financial, investor, operations, or external-audit audiences do not yet exist. Those fronts appear inside the two permitted views as thematic layers.

## 4. Legitimate difference between technical_view and executive_view
The legitimate difference between views may only occur in exposure density, visible volume, trace granularity, dependency explicitness, and tolerance for technical detail.

They may not differ in case epistemic status, material conflict, critical uncertainty, validation dependency, semantic direction of visible claims, or substantive interpretation.

## 5. Role of technical_view
`technical_view` is the base view of maximum explicitness allowed in the MVP. Its function is to expose with greater density case structure, tensions, constraints, dependencies among blocks, and validation agenda.

Greater explicitness does not mean greater truth. It means less compression.

## 6. Role of executive_view
`executive_view` exists to reduce reading friction, condense active fronts, improve documentary rhythm, and lower cognitive load.

It does not exist to soften the case, hide hard limits, or turn the package into persuasion. It may and should make the document more elegant and accessible, provided that it does not change the substantive interpretation.

## 7. Rules of admissible compaction
To compact means to reduce visible volume without reducing substantive restrictions. Any valid compaction must preserve conditionality, material conflict, critical uncertainty, validation dependency, relevant blockages, and the block's real function.

If a block cannot be compacted while preserving its critical limits, that compaction is not admissible.

Valid compaction improves orientation, rhythm, and readability. It does not rewrite the case.

## 8. Different compressibility by block type
Not all blocks are equally compressible.

**High admissible compressibility**
- `executive_summary_block`
- parts of `technical_summary_block`
- `next_steps_block` when the steps are already bounded

**Medium and conditional compressibility**
- `opportunity_block`
- `validation_agenda_block`
- certain simple tables of stabilized evidence

**Low compressibility or risky compressibility**
- `conflict_block`
- `uncertainty_block`
- captions with high limiting load
- complex tables whose summary would induce overreading

## 9. Exclusion rule by incompressibility
If a block cannot be compacted without damage, it must remain only in `technical_view`, be semantically degraded, or leave `executive_view`.

No block has an automatic right to be present in the executive view.

## 10. Language and tone limits by view
The executive view may not use terminal language if the base block does not support it. The technical view may not inflate certainty through density or technicality. Both views must prefer conditional grammar when the case requires it.

Titles, headers, subtitles, labels, and captions are also subject to these rules.

Formulations such as:
- “the main problem is...”
- “the best measure is...”
- “the system confirmed that...”
- “the site is non-compliant...”
- “expected savings will be...”

are blocked whenever support remains preliminary, conflictive, conditional, or validation-dependent.

## 11. Legitimate adaptation versus deformation
Legitimate adaptation reduces friction, improves order, compacts repetition, and makes access to the case more elegant while preserving substantive complexity without losing readability. Deformation hardens language, erases limits, hides conflict, or presents maturity the case does not have.

The rule is explicit: **clarity is admissible; simplification that alters interpretation is not**.

## 12. Normative examples
- **Example 1 — Correct compaction from technical to executive summary**
- `Incorrect:` “The site requires immediate HVAC intervention.”
- `Correct:` “The current reading prioritizes HVAC/controls as the main validation front, under still-visible restrictions on control logic and real operation.”

- **Example 2 — Incorrect compaction of conflict**
- `Incorrect:` “Electrification is the preferred path.”
- `Correct:` “Electrification remains conditioned by conflict among candidate opportunity, unvalidated electrical capacity, and site operating constraints.”

- **Example 3 — Legitimate incompressibility**
- A `conflict_block` that depends on uptime restriction, uncertainty about electrical capacity, and retrofit window should not enter `executive_view` if compaction removes any of those conditions.

- **Example 4 — Misleading title versus proportional title**
- `Incorrect:` “Main problem detected”
- `Correct:` “Primary validation focus under current analytical structure”

- **Example 5 — Correct next steps**
- `Incorrect:` “Implement VFDs across all motors.”
- `Correct:` “Confirm drive applicability, operating profile stability, and control feasibility before elevating variable-speed intervention beyond candidate status.”

## 13. Typical errors this subphase must block
- Treating `executive_view` as the simpler and therefore more conclusive version of the case.
- Using clarity to justify erasing conflict or uncertainty.
- Designing the executive view to persuade and the technical view to qualify later.
- Keeping in `executive_view` blocks whose compaction removes critical limits.
- Confusing adaptation to the reader with the right to soften the case.

## 14. Exact decisions that 3D closes
- The MVP only admits `technical_view` and `executive_view`.
- The difference between views is one of density and compaction, not truth.
- `technical_view` is the base view of maximum explicitness.
- `executive_view` exists to improve access and readability without altering interpretation.
- Compressibility is differential by block type.
- Exclusion by incompressibility is a legitimate fidelity decision.
- Clarity is admissible; simplification that alters reading is not.

## 15. Completion criterion for the subphase
Subphase 3D is considered closed when audience policy stops sounding like a defensive restriction and instead operates as a discipline of readability: able to reduce friction and improve the document without degrading the case's substantive complexity.

# 3E — Epistemic Control and Visible Circulation

## 1. Exact purpose of the subphase
Fix the final circulation barrier of Phase 3 through a brief and sufficient central law capable of deciding whether a visible output may circulate without proliferating micro-controls.

## 2. Why this subphase matters
Phase 3 does not need a publication bureaucracy. It needs a clear final barrier against misleading circulation.

## 3. Mother rule of circulation
The mother law fixed in 3A operates here as the final barrier: **no visible output of Phase 3 may say more than what is supported by the upstream objects on which it depends**.

## 4. Minimum operational consequences
Exactly three consequences follow from that law:
- **Do not invent content.** Nothing may circulate if it cannot be mapped to traceable upstream objects or to admitted structural framing.
- **Do not hide material limits.** Nothing may circulate if it erases or dilutes conflict, uncertainty, critical assumptions, or validation dependency that remain material.
- **Do not harden through presentation.** Nothing may circulate if summary, order, compaction, visualization, caption, or view induces a reading stronger than the case actually supports.

## 5. Permitted outcomes upon failure
When an output cannot circulate as-is, Phase 3 may only produce:
- `publish`
- `degrade`
- `reassign`
- `hold_for_validation`
- `block`

`publish` means the output may circulate as-is.

`degrade` means it may circulate only with lower semantic force.

`reassign` means it must not circulate in that view or form and must be relocated.

`hold_for_validation` means it must not circulate as a visible finding, but must remain alive in the validation agenda.

`block` means it may not circulate.

## 6. Sparse cases and honest publication
A sparse case is not a system failure. It may produce a short, asymmetrical output dominated by uncertainty or validation, and it should be allowed to circulate that way if it honestly represents the case.

Artificial completeness, filler sections, empty artifacts, and generic summaries used to conceal structural weakness are blocked.

## 7. Misleading publication and causes of non-circulation
Honest publication makes the case visible with exactly the force it supports, even if that produces an uncomfortable, brief, or fragile output.

Misleading publication is any output that appears to have more closure, more maturity, or more decisional clarity than support really allows, whether through omission, compaction, order, artifact, title, or tone.

## 8. Normative examples
- **Example 1 — `degrade`**
- `Incorrect:` “The best measure is to electrify the thermal system.”
- `Correct after degrade:` “Electrification remains a candidate pathway conditioned on validation of electrical capacity, operating compatibility, and real load sequence.”

- **Example 2 — `reassign`**
- Case: a technically valid block cannot enter `executive_view` without losing critical limits.
- `Correct:` reassign it to `technical_view` and leave in the executive view only a brief formulation that preserves the real blockage.

- **Example 3 — `hold_for_validation`**
- Case: there is a hypothesis with analytical value, but it still does not support exposure as a visible finding.
- `Correct:` move it into the validation agenda or a critical dependency instead of presenting it as a mature insight.

- **Example 4 — `sparse case`**
- `Correct:` short package, uncertainty dominant, validation agenda visible, little candidate opportunity, and no artifacts.
- `Incorrect:` fill it with generic summary, empty subtitles, or irrelevant tables so that it “looks complete.”

## 9. Typical errors this subphase must block
- Turning final control into a long list of minor gates without a central criterion.
- Allowing circulation of non-traceable content by “general sense.”
- Using omission or order to produce a more mature reading of the case.
- Treating the sparse case as a defect to be fixed with filler.
- Keeping ambiguous states instead of deciding among the authorized outcomes.

## 10. Exact decisions that 3E closes
- Visible circulation in Phase 3 is governed by a single mother rule.
- Exactly three operational consequences derive from that rule.
- Upon failure, only five outcomes are permitted.
- The sparse case is a legitimate configuration.
- The distinction between honest publication and misleading publication is fixed as the final circulation barrier.

## 11. Completion criterion for the subphase
Subphase 3E is considered closed when final circulation control has been reduced to an unequivocal law, a minimum set of consequences, unambiguous outcomes, and a clear distinction between honest publication and misleading publication.

# 3F — Completion Criterion and Technical Handoff

## 1. Exact purpose of the subphase
Define the completion criterion of Phase 3 and the exact point from which its design can be handed to implementation without silent reinterpretation. Its function is to fix what is frozen, what may vary technically without breaking the phase, what the MVP's minimum integrity proof is, why that proof must include a sparse case, and what remains explicitly outside the current closure.

## 2. Why this subphase matters
Phase 3 is not closed by written volume or by aspiration to completeness. It closes when its documentary logic no longer depends on implementation finishing the job of deciding what the phase means.

Closure by sufficiency is not system poverty. It is a condition of serious implementability. It guarantees that the technical layer will be able to build the Reporting Engine without reopening its epistemic scope, its material grammar, or its output contract.

## 3. What must be frozen when Phase 3 closes
When Phase 3 closes, implementation may not reinterpret:
- the phase's epistemic scope
- the prohibition against turning Phase 3 into an inference, verification, final compliance, or terminal recommendation engine
- the definition of `Output Block`
- the definition of `Report Package`
- the two human MVP views
- the MVP's minimum output contract
- and the three already consolidated central semantic laws:
- do not invent content
- do not hide material limits
- do not harden through presentation

These decisions are non-negotiable for implementation.

## 4. What may vary in implementation without breaking the phase
The following may vary:
- serialization formats
- persistence
- internal record structure
- final layout
- `PDF`, `DOCX`, or `HTML`
- visual style
- pagination
- tabular microformat
- degree of automation of renderers, exporters, validators, or assemblers
- and the MVP's effective visual richness

None of those variations may reopen the conceptual contract, alter epistemic inheritance, erase visible limits, create unauthorized views, or substitute structure with free improvisation.

## 5. Minimum integrity proof of the MVP
Phase 3 may only be declared closed if it can sustain, at least conceptually, an end-to-end path that includes:
- admissible upstream objects
- selection and mapping
- block construction
- `output_block_register`
- assembly into `technical_view`
- controlled derivation of `executive_view`
- subordinated artifacts where applicable
- `report_package`
- `machine_export_bundle`
- and application of the visible circulation rule

This proof does not yet require full implementation. It requires sufficient structural coherence so that implementation no longer has to reinterpret the phase in order to make it exist.

## 6. Minimum proof with a sparse case
In addition to a relatively rich case, the phase must support a minimum proof with a sparse case where:
- the package is short
- uncertainty dominates
- the validation agenda weighs more than opportunity
- few or no artifacts exist
- and the output remains publishable without artificial filler

This second proof is not optional. It protects the MVP against false completeness.

## 7. Closure by sufficiency and not by exhaustiveness
Phase 3 closes by MVP sufficiency, not by exhausting possibilities. It does not require maximum industry coverage, an exhaustive subtheme library, a broad visual library, extra stakeholder views, perfect final formats, or advanced customization.

It requires only enough for the Reporting Engine to have closed logic, to materialize a case seriously, and not to depend on later reinterpretation in order to exist. That closure by sufficiency does not reduce ambition; it makes ambition implementable as product discipline.

## 8. What remains explicitly outside the MVP
The following remains explicitly outside the MVP, even if desirable later:
- new stakeholder-specific views
- autonomous regulatory packages
- terminal financial memos
- final compliance reporting
- verification reports
- live dashboards
- automated presentations
- extensive visual libraries
- client- or industry-level customization as a finished product

These expansions belong to future backlog and do not condition the constitutive closure of Phase 3.

## 9. Normative examples
- **Example 1 — Admissible technical variation**
- `Correct:` implement export first in `Markdown` or simple `HTML`, and later in `PDF`, without changing the conceptual contract of the `Report Package`.
- `Incorrect:` introduce a new `investor_view` in implementation because “it would be useful,” without prior documentary authorization.

- **Example 2 — Minimum proof with a relatively rich case**
- `Correct:` case with multiple blocks, visible conflict, candidate opportunity, validation agenda, `technical_view`, `executive_view`, one or two subordinated artifacts, and consistent structured export.

- **Example 3 — Minimum proof with a sparse case**
- `Correct:` short case, dominant uncertainty, visible validation agenda, almost no `opportunity`, no artifacts, and honest publication.
- `Incorrect:` complete it with generic summary, empty subtitles, and irrelevant tables to “demonstrate robustness.”

- **Example 4 — Improper reopening in implementation**
- `Incorrect:` replace `Output Block` with a “free editable section” because it is more convenient for the renderer.
- `Incorrect:` let implementation use open prompts to “improve” the package's tone without respecting already closed circulation rules.

## 10. Typical errors this subphase must block
- Declaring the phase closed by documentary volume rather than conceptual closure.
- Letting implementation decide what a block is, what a view is, or what may circulate.
- Reopening semantic laws for renderer convenience.
- Accepting minimum proof only with rich cases and not with sparse ones.
- Requiring sectoral or visual exhaustiveness as a condition for MVP closure.

## 11. Exact decisions that 3F closes
- Phase 3 is considered closed when its epistemic, material, and contractual core is frozen for implementation.
- There is a zone of legitimate technical variation that does not break the phase.
- The MVP must sustain a minimum end-to-end proof.
- That proof must exist for both a relatively rich case and a sparse case.
- Closure by sufficiency constitutes product discipline, not renunciation.
- Future product expansions remain explicitly outside the MVP.

## 12. Completion criterion for the subphase
Subphase 3F is considered closed when implementation can already take Phase 3 as a stable documentary contract: sufficiently frozen to be built, sufficiently compact to be governable, and sufficiently powerful to produce serious reports without reopening its meaning in the technical layer.
