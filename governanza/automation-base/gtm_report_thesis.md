# GTM Report Thesis

## 1. Product identity

**Commercial name**

- `Decision-Admissibility Asset Brief`

**Commercial subtitle**

- `Minimum Evidence to Commit Capital`

**Internal truth state may remain**

- `Decision-Blocked Asset Brief`
- `Target Classification Brief`
- `Entity Address Classification Brief`
- `Minimum Evidence Report`

Rule:

- the internal runtime state may stay conservative;
- the external product identity must lead with buyer value, not framework degradation language.

---

## 2. Product promise

This product does not tell the client what to believe.
It tells the client whether the current evidence is sufficient to defend a capital-facing decision.

The promise is:

`ZLab identifies the exact decision that is blocked, the assumptions that make it dangerous to proceed, and the minimum evidence that changes the decision state.`

The value proposition is not “better analysis.”
The value proposition is:

- fewer underwriting errors,
- fewer false technical assumptions,
- faster evidence requests,
- and cleaner escalation from screening to defendable diligence.

---

## 3. Buyer priority

### Primary ICP

- funds
- lenders / banks
- asset managers

These buyers care most about:

- whether to proceed,
- what assumption is structurally unsafe,
- what evidence is worth paying for,
- and what diligence should happen before capital is committed.

### Secondary overlay

- industrial operators

These buyers care more about:

- controllability,
- operational bottlenecks,
- disruption risk,
- and process or system validation sequence.

Rule:

- The product spine must be optimized first for capital allocators.
- Operator emphasis can be added later as an overlay, but cannot dominate the core read.

---

## 4. Non-goals

This product is **not**:

- a full technical audit,
- a compliance certification,
- a closed ROI model,
- a lender’s engineering report,
- a consultant deck,
- or a generic AI summary.

It must never imply:

- verified savings,
- final compliance posture,
- investment-grade underwriting certainty,
- or operational conclusions stronger than the evidence ceiling.

---

## 5. Commercial wedge

The wedge is simple:

Most market outputs fail in one of two ways:

1. they pretend confidence where evidence is weak;
2. they expose uncertainty without turning it into a disciplined decision path.

ZLab’s product wins when it does three things at once:

- blocks bad decisions cleanly,
- translates uncertainty into financial and operational downside,
- and shows the shortest evidence path to a better decision.

That is the difference between:

- “interesting diligence,” and
- “capital-preserving diligence.”

---

## 6. What the buyer must understand in under 2 pages

Within the first two pages, the buyer must know:

1. what decision is currently blocked;
2. why proceeding now would introduce structural error;
3. which assumptions are unsupported;
4. what downside those assumptions create;
5. what evidence changes the state;
6. what the buyer can do now without overreaching.

If the first two pages do not answer those six items, the report is not GTM-ready.

---

## 7. Core decision fronts

The product must be usable against concrete decision fronts such as:

- acquisition underwriting
- financing / refinancing
- energy retrofit CAPEX
- compliance investment
- seller diligence request
- operator validation request
- hold / defer / no-go screening

Each case must map to one or more of those fronts explicitly.

---

## 8. Product differentiation

The product is differentiated only if it is visibly better than:

- a generic ESG or benchmarking report,
- a consultant pre-read,
- a lender memo with soft caveats,
- or an LLM-produced “asset summary.”

That means the artifact must visibly:

- connect evidence to admissibility,
- connect uncertainty to money,
- connect missing evidence to the next action,
- and eliminate internal framework noise from the commercial spine.

---

## 9. Failure modes that kill GTM

The product is commercially weak if it:

- leads with framework disclaimers instead of buyer impact,
- uses internal ontology or case codes above the fold,
- repeats itself across executive, TAD, and evidence sections,
- sounds like a downgraded technical report,
- lists evidence without tying it to unlocked decisions,
- or includes numbers and confidence signals the buyer cannot defend externally.

---

## 10. Definition of commercial success

The product succeeds when the buyer says:

`This saved us from committing capital under unresolved asset and evidence risk, and it told us exactly what to request next.`

The product fails when the buyer says:

- `This is sophisticated, but what do I do now?`
- `This looks incomplete.`
- `This reads like an AI report.`
- `This is a technical appendix, not a decision product.`
